### Reasons

**Self-understanding.** The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class
struggles: freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, lord and serf, and in the modern epoch,
bourgeoisie and proletariat. Political institutions, legal codes, philosophies, and religions
are the ideological superstructure resting upon a material economic base: the specific relations
of production that correspond to a given stage of material productive forces.

Capitalism revolutionizes technology and production, but it does so through the systematic
exploitation of the working class. The worker is alienated from the product of their labor,
from the act of production, from their human species-being, and from their fellow human beings.
The value of commodities is created by socially necessary labor time, yet the capitalist
appropriates the surplus value as private profit. This dynamic produces insoluble internal
contradictions: periodic crises of overproduction, declining rates of profit, and the
inevitable organization of the immiserated proletariat as a revolutionary class.

The proletariat cannot emancipate itself without simultaneously emancipating all of humanity
from class society entirely. By seizing the means of production, establishing the temporary
dictatorship of the proletariat to dismantle bourgeois resistance, and abolishing private
property in productive capital, society advances toward stateless, classless communism,
where "the free development of each is the condition for the free development of all."

**Key concepts.**

- **Historical materialism:** The view that material economic conditions and class relations
  are the primary drivers of societal development and historical change.
- **Surplus value & exploitation:** The difference between the value produced by the worker's
  labor and the wages paid to sustain them, appropriated by the owner of capital.
- **Alienation (_Entfremdung_):** The estrangement of workers from their labor, products,
  essential humanity, and each other under capitalist commodity production.
- **Dictatorship of the proletariat:** The democratic, transitional state of the organized
  working class during the revolutionary phase between capitalism and stateless communism.
- **Classless society:** The ultimate stage of social development in which class distinctions,
  the division of labor into rigid spheres, and the coercive state apparatus have withered away.

**Primary source evidence.**

- **"The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles."** — _The Communist Manifesto_ (1848), ch. I.
- **"The philosophers have only interpreted the world in various ways; the point is to change it."** — _Theses on Feuerbach_ (1845), XI.
- **"In place of the old bourgeois society, with its classes and class antagonisms, we shall have an association, in which the free development of each is the condition for the free development of all."** — _The Communist Manifesto_ (1848), ch. II.
- **"In a higher phase of communist society... after labor has become not only a means of life but life's prime want... only then can the narrow horizon of bourgeois right be crossed in its entirety and society inscribe on its banners: From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs!"** — _Critique of the Gotha Programme_ (1875), §1.
- **"The working class cannot simply lay hold of the ready-made state machinery, and wield it for its own purposes."** — _The Civil War in France_ (1871), ch. V.
