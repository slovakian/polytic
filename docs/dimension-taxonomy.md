# Dimension taxonomy (source-of-truth asset)

The canonical set of Dimensions Polytic maps people on: **27 Foundation** and **50
Political**, of which **7 Foundation and 12 Political are Core**. What a Dimension is,
and how Beliefs on it work, is defined in `framework.md`; terms are in `glossary.md`.

Locked by the project owner in
[Lock the Dimension set: Foundation and Political](https://github.com/slovakian/polytic/issues/21),
from three literature surveys (input, not decisions):
[`research/political-dimensions.md`](research/political-dimensions.md),
[`research/political-dimensions-second-pass.md`](research/political-dimensions-second-pass.md)
(the base for the Political set), and
[`research/foundation-dimensions.md`](research/foundation-dimensions.md). Full
steelmen, measurement instruments, and longer lineage for each Dimension are in those
surveys. The set changes only by deliberate revision by the project owner.

## Rules of the set

- **Full set plus a Core.** Every Dimension below is fixed and shared, and any of them
  can hold a Belief. The **Core** is what a first visit's skeleton sweep works through,
  after the person's opening thread, so that any two people (and any person and a
  Reference Figure) share enough Dimensions with more than Thin Coverage to compare.
  The rest are reached when the person raises them or chooses to deepen. A first visit
  is not expected to reach Solid Coverage on the whole Core.
- **One Position per Dimension (the split rule).** A Belief holds one Position, so a
  facet cannot hold a different answer from its Dimension. Where recognisable
  traditions answer two parts of a question differently, the parts are separate
  Dimensions; otherwise those people are averaged into a false middle. Facets are
  sub-topics the grill probes, not separate scores.
- **Two Poles, both defensible.** Every Dimension is a trade-off a thoughtful person
  could resolve either way (`framework.md`).
- **Self-chosen Pole names.** Each Pole is named as the people who hold it would name
  it ("shared heritage", not "ethnic"; "protective regulation", not "nanny state").
  Loaded terms appear only as literature vocabulary.
- **Pole order means nothing.** Pole A is not "left", "default", or "better". Order is
  deliberately mixed so that no hidden left/right column forms; any display keeps that.
- **No composite score.** Polytic never computes or shows a single left–right,
  liberal–conservative, GAL–TAN, or compass score from these Dimensions
  (`principles.md` §5).
- **Issues are not Dimensions.** A hot-button topic (abortion, guns, drugs, youth
  gender medicine) is an **Issue**: a place where several Dimensions collide in a given
  country, probed with Diagnostic Dilemmas and recorded on the Dimensions it tests.
- **Beliefs about facts can be Foundation.** Several Foundation Dimensions (Causes of
  fortune, Zero-sum, Race and ethnicity, Direction of history) are partly beliefs about
  how the world is. They count because they bear on politics, and the Result always
  presents them as the person's view, never as right or wrong.
- **Every tradition is describable.** The set must place every political tradition
  distinctly, including the most reprehensible by conventional standards (fascism and
  national socialism, Stalinism, ethnic nationalism, race realism), with the same
  dispassion as any other (`principles.md` §1 and §3). The second survey's stress test
  covers ~45 traditions, including fascism and Marxism–Leninism.

### Ruled not to be Dimensions

| Candidate                                                                                        | Ruling                                                               |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Economic left–right, GAL–TAN, libertarian–authoritarian, tradition–progress                      | Bundles of the Dimensions below; never computed (see above).         |
| Populism, nationalism, feminism, ecologism                                                       | **Thin Labels** riding on several Dimensions (`label-library.md`).   |
| Concepts of liberty, "equality of what?", models of democracy                                    | **Reasons** vocabulary (Freeden's decontestations), not axes.        |
| Corruption, competence                                                                           | Valence issues: nobody defends the other Pole.                       |
| Moral Foundations, Schwartz values, need for closure, and similar traits                         | Behind-the-scenes toolkit only, never scored (`framework.md`).       |
| Empirical causal claims ("feminization drives wokeness", "the elite is corrupt")                 | Recorded in **Reasons** and Evidence on the Dimensions they bear on. |
| Philosophy of mind, personal identity, aesthetics, and other questions without political bearing | Rejected; can surface as Off-map Beliefs or Reasons.                 |

## Diagnostic Dilemmas written into the set

Whether Diagnostic Dilemmas are pre-written per Dimension or generated live for the
person's country is decided in
[Decide how a grilling session runs behind the scenes](https://github.com/slovakian/polytic/issues/32).
Two are fixed here because the owner asked for them by name:

- **Abortion.** An Issue across _Life and body_ (Political), _Grounds of moral status_
  and _Transcendence_ (Foundation). Where moral status begins (conception, heartbeat,
  consciousness, viability, birth) is recorded in Reasons; a time limit is a Position
  between the Poles. "It's killing, but it should be legal" is a Tension between the
  Foundation and Political Beliefs, put to the person to reconcile or hold.
- **Medical transition for minors.** An Issue across _Paternalism_, _Technology and
  risk_, _Civil society vs. the state_ (parental authority), and _Expert trust_. It is
  also a standing dilemma for _Sex and gender_ (Foundation) and _Sex and gender
  identity_ (Political), so the grill reliably reaches it when gender comes up.

## Foundation Dimensions (27)

The philosophy beneath politics: what the person takes to be real, knowable, and good,
including their own ethic for living. Each earns its place by bearing on Political
Dimensions.

### Agency and fortune

#### Free will

- **Poles:** Self-authored agency ↔ Caused behavior
- **Definition:** Are people's choices ultimately up to them in a way that makes them
  truly responsible, or the product of causes (genes, upbringing, brain, circumstance)
  they did not choose?
- **Lineage:** Augustine; Hobbes and Hume (compatibilism); Kant's practical standpoint;
  d'Holbach; P. F. Strawson 1962; Pereboom 2001; Dennett & Caruso 2021. Shariff et al.
  2014; Everett et al. 2021.
- **Bears on:** Punishment, Distribution (via Desert).

#### Desert

- **Poles:** Earned ↔ Unearned
- **Definition:** Do people morally deserve the rewards and burdens that flow from their
  talents, effort, and choices, or are talent and even effort largely gifts of luck?
- **Lineage:** Aristotle, _Nicomachean Ethics_ V; Feinberg 1970; Rawls 1971 ("natural
  lottery"); Nozick 1974; M. Young 1958; Sandel 2020.
- **Bears on:** Distribution, Reciprocity in support, Punishment.

#### Causes of fortune — Core

- **Poles:** Effort and choice ↔ Luck and structure
- **Definition:** When people succeed or fail, what mostly explains it: their own effort
  and choices, or circumstances they did not control (birth, luck, discrimination,
  economic structure)?
- **Lineage:** Weber 1905; Lerner 1980 (just world); Feagin 1975; Giddens 1984.
- **Bears on:** Distribution, Welfare, Reciprocity in support.

#### Work

- **Poles:** Work is a source of worth ↔ Work is a means to living
- **Definition:** Is work a central source of dignity, character, and meaning, or mainly
  a means to the things that make life worth living?
- **Lineage:** Luther's "calling"; Locke 1689; Weber 1905; Marx 1844; Lafargue 1883;
  Russell 1935; Graeber 2018.
- **Bears on:** Reciprocity in support, Welfare, Growth.

### Human nature

#### Human nature — Core

- **Poles:** Fixed and flawed ↔ Malleable and improvable
- **Definition:** Is human nature essentially fixed, with permanent limits of
  selfishness, knowledge, and virtue that institutions must work around, or largely
  shaped by circumstance, so better institutions can make people substantially better?
- **Lineage:** Augustine; Hobbes 1651; Madison, _Federalist_ 51; Burke 1790 / Rousseau
  1755; Godwin 1793; Condorcet 1795; Sowell, _A Conflict of Visions_ 1987.
- **Bears on:** Tempo of change, Leadership, Design vs. evolved order.

#### Given or made

- **Poles:** Given by nature ↔ Made by society
- **Definition:** Are differences between individuals (ability, intelligence,
  personality, sexuality) mainly given by biology, or mainly produced by upbringing,
  culture, and social arrangements? Sex and race are their own Dimensions below.
- **Lineage:** Plato's myth of the metals; Locke's _tabula rasa_; Galton; Berger &
  Luckmann 1966; Hacking 1999; Pinker 2002.
- **Bears on:** Distribution, Paternalism, education policy.

#### Sex and gender

- **Poles:** Gender follows from sex ↔ Gender is made by society
- **Definition:** Are differences between men and women in temperament, interests, and
  social role mainly an outgrowth of biological sex, or mainly produced by society; and
  is gender something distinct from sex? The far end of the second Pole holds that sex
  itself is socially constructed.
- **Lineage:** Aristotle; Mill, _The Subjection of Women_ 1869; Darwin 1871; Beauvoir,
  _The Second Sex_ 1949; Butler, _Gender Trouble_ 1990; Fausto-Sterling 2000; Pinker
  2002; Baron-Cohen 2003; Stock, _Material Girls_ 2021.
- **Bears on:** Sex roles in family and public life, Sex and gender identity, Group
  difference.
- **Note:** Split from _Given or made_ because people answer differently by domain.

#### Race and ethnicity

- **Poles:** Group differences are largely inherited ↔ Group differences are largely
  made by history and society
- **Definition:** Are average differences between racial and ethnic groups (in
  outcomes, abilities, or behavior) mainly inherited, or mainly produced by history,
  environment, and social arrangements; and is race a natural kind or a social
  construction?
- **Lineage:** Gobineau 1853; Galton 1869; Du Bois 1897; Boas 1911; UNESCO statement on
  race 1950; Lewontin 1972; Omi & Winant 1986; Herrnstein & Murray 1994; Appiah &
  Gutmann 1996; Reich 2018.
- **Bears on:** Group difference, Basis of nationhood, Immigration and borders, Social
  hierarchy, Historical injustice.
- **Note:** Split from _Given or made_ for the same reason as _Sex and gender_.

### Morality

#### Moral objectivity

- **Poles:** Objective moral truth ↔ Human-made morality
- **Definition:** Are some moral claims true regardless of what anyone believes, or is
  morality ultimately a product of human attitudes, agreements, or cultures?
- **Lineage:** Plato; Hume 1739–40; Moore 1903; Ayer 1936; Mackie 1977; Harman 1975;
  Korsgaard 1996; Parfit 2011.
- **Bears on:** Foreign-policy aims, Neutrality vs. perfectionism, universality of
  rights.

#### Source of moral authority

- **Poles:** Given from beyond us ↔ Worked out by us
- **Definition:** Is moral authority something given that we discover and submit to
  (God, nature's order, sacred tradition), or something humans construct and revise
  through reason, experience, or agreement?
- **Lineage:** Aquinas; Finnis 1980; Adams 1999 / Kant 1785; Hume; Gauthier 1986;
  Scanlon 1998. Hunter, _Culture Wars_ 1991 (orthodox vs. progressive).
- **Bears on:** Religion and state, Legal moralism, Sex roles, Life and body.
- **Note:** Kept apart from _Moral objectivity_: orthodox and progressive believers
  agree morality is real but disagree on whether our grasp of it should evolve.

#### Outcomes or rules

- **Poles:** Best outcomes ↔ Inviolable rules
- **Definition:** Is the right act the one with the best overall results, or are there
  things you must never do even when breaking the rule would turn out better?
- **Lineage:** Bentham 1789; Mill 1861; Sidgwick 1874 / Kant 1785; Ross 1930; Nozick
  1974 (side-constraints).
- **Bears on:** Security and civil liberties, Use of force, Punishment.

#### One master value or many

- **Poles:** Unified values ↔ Plural, conflicting values
- **Definition:** Do all genuine values fit one coherent scheme or ranking, or are there
  many that can conflict tragically with no common measure?
- **Lineage:** Plato; Bentham; Dworkin 2011 / Berlin 1958, 1969; Williams; Raz 1986;
  Galston 2002.
- **Bears on:** Neutrality vs. perfectionism, Pluralism vs. unity.

#### Sacred or tradeable

- **Poles:** Some things are beyond price ↔ Everything can be weighed
- **Definition:** Are some things (life, land, faith, honor, the body, the nation)
  sacred, so that trading them is itself a wrong, or can every value be weighed against
  others, money included?
- **Lineage:** Durkheim 1912; Eliade 1957; Kant (price vs. dignity); Tetlock 2003;
  Radin 1996; Sandel 2012 / Becker 1976.
- **Bears on:** Moral limits of markets, Life and body, Land and natural resources.

### Who counts

#### Moral circle — Core

- **Poles:** Near and dear first ↔ Everyone counts equally
- **Definition:** Do we owe more to those close to us (family, community, nation), or
  should everyone's interests count equally wherever they are?
- **Lineage:** Confucian graded love vs. Mozi; Godwin 1793; Singer 1972, 1981; Williams
  1981; MacIntyre 1984; Nussbaum 1994; Waytz et al. 2019.
- **Bears on:** Immigration and borders, Compatriot priority, Foreign-policy aims.

#### Standing of nature

- **Poles:** Human-centered ↔ Nature-centered
- **Definition:** Does nature matter morally only because of what it means for humans,
  or do animals, species, and ecosystems have value of their own?
- **Lineage:** Genesis 1:28; Aristotle; Kant's indirect duties / Leopold 1949; Routley
  1973; Naess 1973; Singer 1975.
- **Bears on:** Environmental protection, Animals, Growth.

#### Grounds of moral status — Core

- **Poles:** Life itself is inviolable ↔ What matters is the life's quality and
  capacities
- **Definition:** What gives a being full moral status: being a living human (or having
  a soul, or the potential to become a person), or having capacities such as
  consciousness, the ability to suffer, or self-awareness?
- **Lineage:** Hippocratic tradition; Catholic sanctity-of-life doctrine; Kant /
  Thomson 1971; Tooley 1972; Warren 1973; Marquis 1989; Singer 1994.
- **Bears on:** Life and body, Animals.

### The social world

#### Hierarchy or equality — Core

- **Poles:** Hierarchy is natural ↔ Equality is basic
- **Definition:** Are people naturally ranked (by ability, virtue, role, or strength),
  so that well-ordered hierarchy is part of the good, or fundamentally equal in worth,
  so that every hierarchy needs justification? Asked as what is true, not what society
  should do (that is _Social hierarchy_).
- **Lineage:** Plato; Aristotle, _Politics_ I; the great chain of being; Nietzsche 1887
  / Locke 1689; Rousseau 1755; Kant; Waldron 2017.
- **Bears on:** Social hierarchy, Distribution, Sex roles.

#### Zero-sum or positive-sum

- **Poles:** One's gain is another's loss ↔ Gains can be shared
- **Definition:** Does one person's or group's gain generally come at another's
  expense, or can everyone gain together through cooperation, trade, and growth?
- **Lineage:** Montaigne 1580; mercantilism; Smith 1776; Marx; Foster 1965; Thurow 1980;
  Chinoy et al. 2023.
- **Bears on:** Trade, Immigration, Distribution, Growth.

#### Individuals or wholes

- **Poles:** Only individuals are real ↔ Groups and relations are real
- **Definition:** Is society nothing over and above its individuals, or are
  communities, nations, classes, and institutions real entities with goods and
  responsibilities of their own?
- **Lineage:** Hobbes; Bentham; Weber; Popper 1945; Hayek / Hegel 1820; Durkheim 1895;
  Marx; Taylor 1985; Sandel 1982.
- **Bears on:** Group difference, Civil society vs. the state, Neutrality vs.
  perfectionism. Asked as what is true.

#### Rights: natural or man-made

- **Poles:** Rights exist before the state ↔ Rights are created by society
- **Definition:** Do people have rights simply by being persons, before any law, or are
  rights rules societies create and can reshape for good reasons?
- **Lineage:** Locke 1689; the 1776 and 1789 Declarations; Nozick 1974 / Hume; Bentham
  ("nonsense upon stilts"); Burke 1790; Hart 1955.
- **Bears on:** Property, Popular sovereignty vs. constitutional constraint, Political
  authority.
- **Note:** Kept apart from _Moral objectivity_: Bentham was a moral realist of sorts who
  rejected natural rights.

### Knowledge

#### Grounds for belief

- **Poles:** Belief proportioned to evidence ↔ Belief rightly held on other grounds
- **Definition:** Should you believe things only as far as public evidence supports
  them, or are some beliefs rightly held on other grounds (faith, intuition, lived
  experience, revelation)?
- **Lineage:** Locke 1689; Hume 1748; Clifford 1877 / Pascal 1670; Kierkegaard 1846;
  James 1896; Wittgenstein 1969; Plantinga.
- **Bears on:** Religion and state, Popular judgment vs. expertise.

#### Expert trust — Core

- **Poles:** Defer to expert institutions ↔ Judge for yourself
- **Definition:** When you cannot check something yourself, should you defer to experts
  and their institutions, or keep final judgment for yourself and your community?
- **Lineage:** Plato; Kant 1784 (_sapere aude_); Mill 1859; Lippmann 1922 vs. Dewey
  1927; Feyerabend 1978; Hardwig 1985; Goldman 2001.
- **Bears on:** Popular judgment vs. expertise, Technology and risk, Paternalism.

#### Tradition or reason

- **Poles:** Inherited practice carries wisdom ↔ Reason should audit inheritance
- **Definition:** How much authority should inherited practices have: do they embody
  knowledge no one can reconstruct, so change bears the burden of proof, or should
  practices be judged by explicit reasoning, with no authority from age alone?
- **Lineage:** Burke 1790; Hume; Hayek 1945, 1960; Oakeshott 1962; Chesterton 1929 /
  Descartes 1637; Paine 1791; Godwin.
- **Bears on:** Tempo of change, Design vs. evolved order.
- **Note:** Kept apart from _Expert trust_: a Burkean and a do-your-own-research
  rationalist both distrust experts, but only one trusts tradition.

### Reality and history

#### Transcendence — Core

- **Poles:** A transcendent order exists ↔ Nature is all there is
- **Definition:** Is there a reality beyond the natural world (God, gods, a divine
  order, an afterlife) that gives existence its meaning and standards, or is nature all
  there is, with meaning made by humans?
- **Lineage:** Aquinas; Pascal 1670; Hume 1779; Feuerbach 1841; Nietzsche 1882; James
  1902; Russell 1927; Plantinga.
- **Bears on:** Religion and state, Life and body, Sex roles, Legal moralism.

#### Direction of history

- **Poles:** History is progressing ↔ Progress is not assured
- **Definition:** Is history on the whole a story of moral and material progress, or is
  progress fragile, cyclical, or illusory, with real goods often lost in change?
- **Lineage:** Augustine; Polybius; Ibn Khaldun 1377; Vico 1725; Kant 1784; Condorcet
  1795; Hegel 1837; Marx; Spengler 1918; Butterfield 1931.
- **Bears on:** Tempo of change, Technology and risk, Growth.

### The person's own life

#### What makes a life go well

- **Poles:** It depends on the person's own experience and wants ↔ Some things are good
  for anyone
- **Definition:** Is a life going well a matter of the person's own pleasure,
  satisfaction, or desires, or are some things (knowledge, friendship, achievement,
  virtue, faith) good for a person whether or not they want them?
- **Lineage:** Epicurus; Bentham; Mill 1861 / Aristotle; Finnis 1980; Parfit 1984
  (Appendix I); Hurka 1993; Sen 1999; Nussbaum 2000.
- **Bears on:** Neutrality vs. perfectionism, Paternalism.

#### Self-authorship or given role

- **Poles:** Author your own life ↔ Live up to a given role
- **Definition:** Is the good life one you author yourself by choosing your own
  commitments, or one in which you faithfully fill roles and duties you did not choose
  (family, community, faith, vocation, nation)?
- **Lineage:** Rousseau; Herder; Mill 1859 ch. 3; Nietzsche; Sartre 1946; Taylor 1991 /
  Confucius; Epictetus; Aristotle; Bradley 1876; MacIntyre 1981.
- **Bears on:** Sex roles, Citizenship, Neutrality vs. perfectionism.
- **Note:** Kept apart from _What makes a life go well_: Raz holds that goods are
  objective and that self-authorship is one of them.

## Political Dimensions (50)

What society should do, collectively or by law.

### Economy

#### Distribution — Core

- **Poles:** Market entitlement ↔ Egalitarian redistribution
- **Definition:** Should the law leave the distribution of income and wealth as fair
  exchange produces it, or reshape it toward greater equality?
- **Lineage:** Aristotle; Rawls 1971; Nozick 1974; Hayek 1976; Dworkin 1981; Frankfurt
  1987; Bobbio 1994.
- **Facets:** Tax progressivity; wealth and inheritance taxes; equality, priority, or
  sufficiency; meritocracy; equality of opportunity.

#### Economic coordination — Core

- **Poles:** Public direction ↔ Market coordination
- **Definition:** Should the economy be coordinated mainly through prices and
  competition, or through deliberate public direction (ownership, planning, industrial
  policy, heavy regulation)?
- **Lineage:** Mises 1920; Lange 1936; Polanyi 1944; Hayek 1945; Hall & Soskice 2001.
- **Facets:** Ownership of key sectors; industrial policy; price controls; regulatory
  intensity; public vs. private delivery.

#### Property in capital and goods

- **Poles:** Robust private ownership ↔ Socially conditioned ownership
- **Definition:** Are property rights mostly prior to the state, to be protected and
  rarely redrawn, or conventions the community defines and may condition on social
  purposes?
- **Lineage:** Locke 1689; Nozick 1974; _Rerum Novarum_ 1891; Duguit; Murphy & Nagel 2002.
- **Facets:** Takings; regulation as taking; inheritance; intellectual property.

#### Land and natural resources

- **Poles:** Common inheritance ↔ Private appropriation
- **Definition:** Should land and natural resources be treated like any other owned
  good, or as a common inheritance whose value belongs to everyone?
- **Lineage:** Locke's proviso; Paine 1797; George 1879; Steiner 1994; Ostrom 1990.
- **Facets:** Land value tax; land reform; resource ownership and sovereign wealth
  funds; commons; indigenous land title.

#### Economic scale and concentration

- **Poles:** Scale and efficiency ↔ Dispersed ownership and small scale
- **Definition:** Should the law tolerate large firms and concentrated ownership when
  they deliver efficiency, or break up concentrations and favour widely held,
  small-scale property?
- **Lineage:** Brandeis 1914; Belloc 1912; Eucken 1952; Schumacher 1973; Bork 1978; Khan 2017.
- **Facets:** Antitrust standard; small firms and family farms; wide asset ownership;
  big-tech power; media ownership.

#### Moral limits of markets

- **Poles:** Protected non-market spheres ↔ Markets without fixed limits
- **Definition:** Should some goods (bodies, organs, sex, surrogacy, votes, citizenship)
  be kept out of the market by law, or should voluntary exchange be allowed wherever it
  is otherwise permissible?
- **Lineage:** Titmuss 1970; Walzer 1983; Radin 1996; Satz 2010; Sandel 2012 / Brennan
  & Jaworski 2016.
- **Facets:** Organ markets; commercial surrogacy; sex work; paid citizenship; money in
  politics; for-profit public services.

#### Welfare and social insurance

- **Poles:** Self-reliance and voluntary provision ↔ Public social insurance
- **Definition:** How far should the community, through the state, insure people against
  sickness, old age, unemployment, and disability?
- **Lineage:** Beveridge 1942; Marshall 1950; Esping-Andersen 1990 / Friedman 1962;
  Murray 1984.
- **Facets:** Universal vs. means-tested; cash vs. services; pensions; health-care
  model.

#### Reciprocity in support

- **Poles:** Unconditional support ↔ Conditional (reciprocal) support
- **Definition:** Should public support be conditional on contribution (work, job
  search, service), or given unconditionally as a right?
- **Lineage:** Rawls 1988; Van Parijs 1995 / Mead 1986; White 2003.
- **Facets:** Work requirements; sanctions; basic income; care work; contributory vs.
  tax-funded entitlement.

#### Workplace governance

- **Poles:** Worker voice and self-management ↔ Owner and managerial authority
- **Definition:** Should firms be governed by their owners and managers, or should the
  people who work in them have a binding say?
- **Lineage:** Webbs 1897; Pateman 1970; Dahl 1985; Anderson 2017 / Coase 1937; Jensen &
  Meckling 1976.
- **Facets:** Unions; co-determination; co-operatives; employment protection; minimum
  wage.

#### Trade and economic openness

- **Poles:** Open exchange ↔ Economic protection and self-reliance
- **Definition:** Should goods, capital, and investment flow freely across borders, or
  should a community protect and build its own productive base?
- **Lineage:** Smith 1776; Ricardo 1817 / Hamilton 1791; List 1841; Prebisch 1950;
  Rodrik 2011.
- **Facets:** Tariffs; capital controls; investment screening; food and energy
  self-sufficiency.

#### Money and public finance

- **Poles:** Discretion and active management ↔ Rules and fiscal discipline
- **Definition:** Should money and budgets be governed by firm rules, or should
  governments and central banks actively manage demand and use deficits?
- **Lineage:** Keynes 1936; Lerner 1943 / Simons 1936; Kydland & Prescott 1977; Buchanan
  & Wagner 1977.
- **Facets:** Debt rules; central-bank mandate; hard money vs. fiat; inflation vs.
  unemployment.

### Nature, technology, and the future

#### Environmental protection — Core

- **Poles:** Prosperity and development first ↔ Ecological protection first
- **Definition:** When protecting ecosystems and the climate costs jobs, growth, or cheap
  energy, which should give way?
- **Lineage:** Leopold 1949; Carson 1962; Dryzek 1997; Nordhaus 2008; UN Declaration on
  the Right to Development 1986.
- **Facets:** Climate-policy stringency; conservation and land use; energy mix; climate
  burden-sharing; rights of nature.

#### Growth

- **Poles:** Sufficiency and limits ↔ Growth and abundance
- **Definition:** Should society aim for continued growth and material abundance, or for
  a stable, sufficient economy within limits?
- **Lineage:** Mill 1848; Meadows et al. 1972; Daly 1977; Jackson 2009 / Simon 1981;
  Friedman 2005.
- **Facets:** GDP as a goal; working time; consumption limits; housing and
  infrastructure abundance.

#### Technology and risk

- **Poles:** Proaction ↔ Precaution
- **Definition:** When a powerful new technology has uncertain risks, should society let
  it proceed and fix problems as they appear, or require proof of safety first?
- **Lineage:** Bacon 1627; More 2005; Thierer 2014 / Jonas 1979; Collingridge 1980; Rio
  Principle 15.
- **Facets:** AI; biotech and GMOs; nuclear power; human enhancement; new medical
  treatments; automation.

#### Future generations

- **Poles:** Present people's claims ↔ Future people's claims
- **Definition:** How much should today's policy sacrifice for people not yet born?
- **Lineage:** Burke 1790; Rawls §44; Parfit 1984; Stern 2007 vs. Nordhaus 2007; Gardiner 2011.
- **Facets:** Discount rate; public debt; pensions; climate; existential risk.

#### Animals

- **Poles:** Legal protection of animal interests ↔ Human priority in using animals
- **Definition:** How far should the law protect animals' interests when they conflict
  with human uses such as food, research, and land?
- **Lineage:** Bentham 1789; Singer 1975; Regan 1983; Donaldson & Kymlicka 2011 / Kant;
  Carruthers 1992.
- **Facets:** Factory farming; animal research; hunting; religious slaughter.

### The state and the good life

#### Neutrality vs. perfectionism

- **Poles:** State neutrality ↔ State perfectionism
- **Definition:** Should law and policy stay neutral among citizens' ideas of the good
  life, or may the state promote ways of living it judges genuinely valuable?
- **Lineage:** Rawls 1993; Dworkin 1978; Larmore 1987; Quong 2011 / Raz 1986; Sher 1997;
  Chan 2014.
- **Facets:** Public funding of arts, culture, and religion; curriculum values;
  promoting marriage.

#### Paternalism

- **Poles:** Protection ↔ Choice
- **Definition:** May the law restrict or steer a person's choices for their own good,
  or should adults be free to make choices that mainly risk themselves?
- **Lineage:** Mill 1859; G. Dworkin 1972; Feinberg, _Harm to Self_ 1986; Thaler &
  Sunstein 2008.
- **Facets:** Drugs and alcohol; sugar and tobacco taxes; safety rules; nudges;
  protections for minors.
- **Note:** Split from _Legal moralism_: the public-health left is paternalist but not
  moralist; some social conservatives are the reverse.

#### Legal moralism

- **Poles:** Liberty of self-regarding conduct ↔ Enforcement of shared morality
- **Definition:** May the law forbid conduct that harms no one else because the
  community holds it to be wrong?
- **Lineage:** Mill 1859; Hart 1963 / Devlin 1965; Feinberg 1984–88; George 1993.
- **Facets:** Sexual morality; obscenity and pornography; gambling; public decency; sex
  work.

#### Life and body — Core

- **Poles:** Bodily and reproductive autonomy ↔ Protection of human life and dignity
- **Definition:** At the edges of life and about the body, should the law defer to the
  individual's choice, or protect human life and dignity even against that choice?
- **Lineage:** Thomson 1971; R. Dworkin 1993 / Finnis 1973; Marquis 1989; Kass 1997;
  Habermas 2003.
- **Facets:** Abortion; assisted dying; IVF and embryo research; gene editing.

#### Sex roles in family and public life — Core

- **Poles:** Distinct roles for the sexes ↔ Equal and interchangeable roles
- **Definition:** Should law and policy uphold distinct roles for men and women and the
  family form built on them, at home and in public institutions, or treat the sexes as
  interchangeable and diverse family forms alike?
- **Lineage:** Aristotle; Hegel 1820; Girgis et al. 2012 / Mill 1869; Okin 1989; Fraser
  1994; Brake 2012.
- **Facets:** Marriage law and same-sex relationships; childcare vs. homemaker support;
  natalism; women in leadership, quotas, and the military; sex education.

#### Sex and gender identity

- **Poles:** Law follows sex ↔ Law follows gender identity
- **Definition:** When law and institutions sort people as men and women (documents,
  sports, prisons, single-sex spaces, medicine), should they go by sex or by gender
  identity?
- **Lineage:** Butler 1990; Bettcher 2014; Yogyakarta Principles 2006; Argentina's
  Gender Identity Law 2012 / Stock 2021; Lawford-Smith 2022; _For Women Scotland v
  Scottish Ministers_ 2025.
- **Facets:** Legal gender recognition and self-ID; sports; prisons and single-sex
  spaces; pronouns in law and schools. Youth medical transition is an Issue (see
  Diagnostic Dilemmas).
- **Note:** Split from _Sex roles_: gender-critical feminists want equal roles but
  sex-based law.

#### Religion and state — Core

- **Poles:** Public role for religion ↔ Secular public order
- **Definition:** Should public institutions be kept free of religion, or should the
  state recognise, support, or be guided by religion?
- **Lineage:** Locke 1689; Madison 1785; _Dignitatis Humanae_ 1965; Kuru 2009; Bhargava
  1998; An-Na'im 2008.
- **Facets:** Establishment and funding; religious schools; symbols; exemptions;
  blasphemy; religious law in family matters.

#### Speech and expression — Core

- **Poles:** Maximal expressive freedom ↔ Protection from harmful expression
- **Definition:** Should expression be protected even when offensive, hateful, or false,
  or may the law restrict speech that harms dignity, equality, or public order?
- **Lineage:** Milton 1644; Mill 1859; Meiklejohn 1948 / Matsuda 1989; MacKinnon 1993;
  Waldron 2012.
- **Facets:** Hate speech; misinformation and platform moderation; campaign finance;
  academic freedom.

### Order, security, and justice

#### Security and civil liberties — Core

- **Poles:** Civil liberties and due process ↔ Collective security and order
- **Definition:** When protecting the public from crime, terrorism, or disorder
  conflicts with individual rights, which should give way?
- **Lineage:** Hobbes 1651; Packer 1964; Wilson & Kelling 1982 / Blackstone; Westin
  1967; Solove 2007.
- **Facets:** Surveillance; policing powers; trial rights; emergency powers; gun rights
  and the state's monopoly of force.

#### Punishment

- **Poles:** Desert and deterrence ↔ Rehabilitation and restoration
- **Definition:** Should punishment mainly give offenders what they deserve and deter
  others, or mainly repair harm and return offenders to society?
- **Lineage:** Kant 1797; Beccaria 1764; von Hirsch 1976 / Braithwaite 1989; Zehr 1990;
  Garland 2001.
- **Facets:** Death penalty; sentence length; alternatives to prison; prison abolition.

#### Rule of law vs. discretion

- **Poles:** Judgment for the case at hand ↔ Rule by general, fixed rules
- **Definition:** Should public power act through general rules applied alike to all, or
  through the trained judgment of officials who can respond to each case?
- **Lineage:** Aristotle, _Politics_ III; Confucius vs. Han Feizi; Dicey 1885; Hayek
  1960; Fuller 1964; Schmitt 1922.
- **Facets:** Emergency powers; judicial formalism; administrative discretion; mercy and
  pardon.

#### Historical injustice

- **Poles:** Forward-looking ↔ Rectification
- **Definition:** Do past wrongs create present obligations (reparations, restitution,
  return of land), or should justice concern only present and future arrangements?
- **Lineage:** Nozick 1974 (rectification); Waldron 1992; Fanon 1961; Táíwò 2022.
- **Facets:** Reparations; land restitution; colonial legacies; apologies and memory.

### Who decides

#### Political authority

- **Poles:** Legitimate state authority ↔ Voluntary order
- **Definition:** Should there be a state with a general right to rule and coerce, or
  should order rest only on voluntary association?
- **Lineage:** Hobbes 1651 / Kropotkin 1892; Wolff 1970; Rothbard 1973; D. Friedman 1973;
  Simmons 1979; Huemer 2013.
- **Facets:** Minimal state; market anarchism; communist anarchism; political
  obligation.
- **Note:** Lets anarchists be described as a position, not as the far end of other
  Dimensions.

#### Civil society vs. the state

- **Poles:** Family, community, and voluntary association ↔ The state
- **Definition:** When something must be done for people (care, education, welfare,
  moral formation), should the default be families, churches, and voluntary
  associations, with the state stepping in only when they cannot, or the state, which
  can guarantee equal standards?
- **Lineage:** Althusius 1603; _Quadragesimo Anno_ 1931; Burke's "little platoons";
  Tocqueville 1835; Nisbet 1953; Berger & Neuhaus 1977 / Rousseau 1762; Hegel 1820.
- **Facets:** Parental authority vs. the state (schooling, children's medical
  decisions); charity and church provision; homeschooling.
- **Note:** The horizontal half of subsidiarity, split from _Local vs. central_.

#### Local vs. central

- **Poles:** Local and regional self-rule ↔ Central and uniform authority
- **Definition:** Within a country, should power sit at the smallest capable level of
  government, or at the centre that can act uniformly and guarantee equal standards?
- **Lineage:** _Federalist_; Tocqueville 1835; Tiebout 1956; Oates 1972; Ostrom 1990;
  Hooghe & Marks 2003; TEU Art. 5(3).
- **Facets:** Fiscal federalism; local standards in schools, policing, and health;
  devolution and secession; housing and zoning control.
- **Note:** The vertical half of subsidiarity. The supranational tier is _Sovereignty
  vs. pooled governance_.

#### Sovereignty vs. pooled governance

- **Poles:** Pooled, rule-bound international governance ↔ Sovereign self-government
- **Definition:** Should authority be pooled in international and supranational bodies,
  or kept with the self-governing nation-state?
- **Lineage:** Bodin 1576; Kant 1795; Haas 1958; Milward 1992; Hooghe & Marks 2009;
  Rodrik 2011.
- **Facets:** Regional integration; international courts; trade-rule bodies; global
  institutions.

#### Popular sovereignty vs. constitutional constraint

- **Poles:** Entrenched rights and judicial review ↔ Popular and legislative supremacy
- **Definition:** Should basic rights and rules be put beyond ordinary majorities and
  enforced by courts, or should the people and their legislature have the final word?
- **Lineage:** Rousseau 1762; Thayer 1893; Waldron 2006 / _Federalist_ 78; Kelsen; Ely
  1980; R. Dworkin 1996.
- **Facets:** Judicial review; bills of rights; amendment difficulty; guardian bodies.

#### Majoritarian vs. consensus

- **Poles:** Clear majority rule and accountability ↔ Power-sharing and broad consensus
- **Definition:** Should the winning majority govern alone and be clearly accountable,
  or should power be shared so most groups have a say?
- **Lineage:** Schumpeter 1942; Riker 1982; Powell 2000 / Lijphart 1977, 1999; Wiredu 1995.
- **Facets:** Electoral system; coalitions; minority vetoes; supermajorities.

#### Representation vs. participation

- **Poles:** Direct and participatory self-rule ↔ Representative, trustee government
- **Definition:** Should citizens mostly choose representatives who use their own
  judgment, or decide and deliberate directly?
- **Lineage:** Burke 1774; Madison; Schumpeter; Achen & Bartels 2016 / Rousseau; Pateman
  1970; Barber 1984.
- **Facets:** Referendums; citizens' assemblies and sortition; recall; participatory
  budgeting.

#### Popular judgment vs. expertise — Core

- **Poles:** Rule by the knowledgeable ↔ Equal popular judgment
- **Definition:** Should political decisions, or the choice of who makes them, give
  special weight to knowledge and competence, or should every citizen's judgment count
  equally?
- **Lineage:** Plato; Mill 1861; Lippmann 1922; Brennan 2016; Bell 2015 / Dewey 1927;
  Estlund 2008; Landemore 2013.
- **Facets:** Independent central banks and regulators; weighted franchise; meritocratic
  selection; scientific advice.

#### Pluralism vs. unity

- **Poles:** Legitimate pluralism and opposition ↔ Unified common purpose
- **Definition:** Is politics rightly a lasting contest among many legitimate interests
  and parties, or should it express one common will or common good of the people?
- **Lineage:** Madison; Dahl 1971; Müller 2016 / Rousseau 1762; Schmitt; Lenin; Mudde 2004.
- **Facets:** Legitimacy of dissent; party competition (these can come apart, as in
  Wiredu's non-party polity); corporatism; banning anti-system parties.

#### Leadership — Core

- **Poles:** Strong, decisive leadership ↔ Dispersed, checked authority
- **Definition:** Should executive power be concentrated in a strong leader able to act
  decisively, or divided and checked so that no one can act alone?
- **Lineage:** Machiavelli; Hamilton, _Federalist_ 70; Weber 1919 / Montesquieu 1748;
  Madison, _Federalist_ 51; Linz 1990.
- **Facets:** Presidential vs. parliamentary; term limits; decree powers; monarchy as
  executive.

#### Social hierarchy

- **Poles:** Equality of status ↔ Ordered rank and deference
- **Definition:** Should society and its institutions recognise ranks of status and
  authority that command deference (by role, age, office, lineage, or merit), or treat
  everyone as equals in standing?
- **Lineage:** Rousseau 1755; Ambedkar 1936; Anderson 1999 / Burke 1791; Confucius;
  Dumont 1966; Bell & Wang 2020.
- **Facets:** Monarchy and hereditary office; honours; caste and status law; elder
  authority.

#### Citizenship: rights vs. duties

- **Poles:** Citizenship as shared obligation ↔ Citizenship as protected liberty
- **Definition:** Is citizenship mainly a set of protected freedoms, including the
  freedom not to take part, or does it carry enforceable duties to serve and
  participate?
- **Lineage:** Machiavelli 1531; Rousseau; Sandel 1996; Dagger 1997 / Constant 1819;
  Nozick; Brennan 2011.
- **Facets:** National service; compulsory voting; jury duty; civic education.

### Membership and the world

#### Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality

- **Poles:** Special obligations to compatriots ↔ Impartial concern for all people
- **Definition:** May a political community put its own members first, or should it
  weigh the interests of all people equally?
- **Lineage:** Mencius vs. Mozi; Godwin 1793; Singer 1972; Beitz 1979; Pogge 2002 /
  Miller 1995; Nagel 2005.
- **Facets:** Foreign aid; global distributive justice; welfare for non-citizens;
  priority for national workers.

#### Basis of nationhood

- **Poles:** Shared civic principles ↔ Shared heritage and culture
- **Definition:** What should make someone a full member of the nation: allegiance to
  shared political principles, or a shared culture, language, history, and way of life?
- **Lineage:** Herder; Renan 1882; Kohn 1944; Brubaker 1992; Smith 1991; Habermas 1992;
  Hazony 2018.
- **Facets:** Citizenship by descent vs. birthplace; official language; national
  history and symbols; minority nations.

#### Immigration and borders — Core

- **Poles:** Community control over membership ↔ Freedom of movement
- **Definition:** Should a political community decide who and how many may enter and
  settle, or do people have a strong claim to move and live where they choose?
- **Lineage:** Walzer 1983; Wellman 2008; Miller 2016 / Carens 1987; Abizadeh 2008;
  Huemer 2010.
- **Facets:** Levels; selection criteria; asylum; enforcement; path to citizenship.

#### Group difference

- **Poles:** Group-differentiated recognition ↔ Difference-blind common rules
- **Definition:** Should the law recognise cultural, religious, ethnic, and indigenous
  groups with group-specific rights, exemptions, or remedies, or apply one set of rules
  to every citizen alike?
- **Lineage:** Taylor 1992; Kymlicka 1995; Young 1990; Fraser 1995 / Barry 2001; Okin
  1999; Joppke 2004.
- **Facets:** Affirmative action and reservations; minority-language rights; indigenous
  self-government; religious exemptions; integration model.

#### Use of force

- **Poles:** Readiness to use force ↔ Reluctance to use force
- **Definition:** How ready should a country be to build up, threaten, and use military
  force, as opposed to diplomacy and restraint?
- **Lineage:** Clausewitz 1832; Schelling 1966; Walzer 1977 / Kant 1795; Tolstoy 1894;
  Wittkopf 1990.
- **Facets:** Defence spending; nuclear deterrence; alliances; humanitarian
  intervention.

#### Foreign-policy aims

- **Poles:** Universal values ↔ National interest
- **Definition:** Should foreign policy pursue the nation's security and interests, or
  also promote values such as democracy and human rights abroad?
- **Lineage:** Thucydides; Carr 1939; Morgenthau 1948; Waltz 1979 / Kant 1795; Wilson
  1918; Doyle 1983.
- **Facets:** Democracy promotion; sanctions; responsibility to protect; relations with
  autocracies.

### Political change

#### Tempo of change — Core

- **Poles:** Fundamental transformation ↔ Incremental reform
- **Definition:** When society needs to change, should it change step by step, keeping
  what works, or fundamentally and quickly? Direction-neutral: one can be an
  incremental progressive or a radical restorationist.
- **Lineage:** Burke 1790; Oakeshott 1956; Popper 1945; Lindblom 1959 / Paine 1791; Marx
  & Engels 1848; Unger 1987.
- **Facets:** Speed; scope; reversibility.

#### Design vs. evolved order

- **Poles:** Evolved, spontaneous order ↔ Deliberate design
- **Definition:** When building or reforming institutions, should society extend orders
  that evolved without a designer, or design them deliberately from principles and
  evidence?
- **Lineage:** Ferguson 1767; Hayek 1973; Oakeshott 1947; Scott 1998 / Bentham;
  Condorcet 1795; Neurath.
- **Facets:** Common law vs. codification; planned and charter cities; evidence-based
  policy.
- **Note:** Political; its epistemic counterpart is _Tradition or reason_ (Foundation).

#### Channels of change

- **Poles:** Within institutions ↔ Beyond institutions
- **Definition:** Should political change go only through lawful channels, or is action
  outside them (civil disobedience, political strikes, blockades, revolution)
  legitimate when institutions fail?
- **Lineage:** Hobbes; Kant / Locke ch. 19; Thoreau 1849; King 1963; Rawls 1971
  §§55–59; Delmas 2018.
- **Facets:** Civil disobedience; political strikes; blockades; tax resistance.

#### Force in domestic politics

- **Poles:** Permissible force against injustice ↔ Principled nonviolence
- **Definition:** Is force ever a legitimate means of political change or resistance
  within a society, or must political action always be nonviolent?
- **Lineage:** Locke; the 1776 Declaration; Fanon 1961 / Tolstoy; Gandhi 1909; King;
  Sharp 1973; Chenoweth & Stephan 2011.
- **Facets:** Right of revolution; armed self-defence; tyrannicide in history.
- **Note:** Mapped like any other Dimension. Diagnostic Dilemmas for it are historical
  or abstract, never calls to action (`principles.md` §1).
