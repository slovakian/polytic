# Fascism, the radical right, and reaction: Label cards

Cards for this family, written to the template in `../label-library.md`, in the order
of the list. Every card here is written with the same dispassion as any other: the
self-description is the tradition's own self-understanding, steelmanned, and never
Polytic's verdict (`../principles.md`). Where a tradition is defined partly by violence,
the card records that plainly, and probes on it stay historical or abstract.

## Fascism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** generic fascism (scholars' term for the family); Third Position (some
  postwar self-names); the word is also used loosely by critics for any authoritarian
  right, which this card does not cover

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The nation is a living, spiritual community that outlasts the
individuals in it, and in times of decadence and division it must be reborn. Liberalism
dissolves the nation into selfish individuals and quarrelling parties; Marxism splits it
into warring classes. Fascism offers a third way: a people united above class and party
in one will, mobilised by a revolutionary movement and its leader, with capital and
labour reconciled in corporations under the state, and every citizen finding meaning in
discipline, service and sacrifice for the nation's greatness.

**Key concepts.**

- **National rebirth (palingenesis):** the nation can rise again from decadence through
  a revolutionary act of will.
- **Totalitarian state:** the state embodies the nation, so nothing of value lies
  outside it.
- **Corporatism:** employers and workers organised in state-supervised corporations by
  sector, in place of class struggle and free markets.
- **Third way:** beyond both liberal capitalism and Marxist socialism.
- **The new man:** a citizen remade by discipline, action and sacrifice, not comfort.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Everything within the state, nothing outside the state, nothing against the state."
- "The nation comes before the individual and above every class."
- "Neither capitalism nor communism: a third way."
- "A decadent nation needs rebirth, not reform."
- "Life is struggle, duty and sacrifice, not comfort."

**Telling it apart.**

- **National Socialism:** a branch that makes biological race, not the state, the basis
  of the nation (_Race and ethnicity_; centered group).
- **Falangism (national syndicalism):** a branch that is Catholic and national-syndicalist
  (_Religion and state_; Reasons).
- **Counter-revolutionary traditionalism:** wants to restore throne and altar, and has
  often opposed fascism as another modern mass movement (_Tempo of change_, _Religion and
  state_).
- **Conservative Revolution:** an intellectual current that shared much of the diagnosis
  but mostly stayed outside the mass party (Reasons).
- **National populism:** works within electoral democracy and accepts party competition
  (_Pluralism vs. unity_, _Channels of change_, _Leadership_).
- **Marxism–Leninism:** also a one-party mobilising state, organised around class, not
  nation (_Property in capital and goods_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Alfredo Rocco, "The Political Doctrine of Fascism" (1925); Giovanni Gentile,
_Origins and Doctrine of Fascism_ (1929); Giovanni Gentile and Benito Mussolini, "The
Doctrine of Fascism" (1932); Zeev Sternhell, _The Birth of Fascist Ideology_ (1989);
Roger Griffin, _The Nature of Fascism_ (1991); Stanley Payne, _A History of Fascism,
1914–1945_ (1995); Robert Paxton, _The Anatomy of Fascism_ (2004).

## National Socialism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Fascism
- **Also called:** Nazism (originally an opponents' abbreviation, now universal);
  neo-Nazism (its postwar forms); Strasserism (its anti-capitalist current)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** a people of common descent: historically the German _Volk_; for
  postwar neo-Nazis, the "Aryan" or White race

| Leadership                                  | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_             | Yes     |
| Pluralism vs. unity                         | Strongly _Unified common purpose_                  | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes                       | Strongly _Groups and relations are real_           | No      |
| Moral circle                                | Strongly _Near and dear first_                     | No      |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_      | No      |
| Immigration and borders                     | Strongly _Community control over membership_       | No      |
| Citizenship: rights vs. duties              | Strongly _Citizenship as shared obligation_        | No      |
| Security and civil liberties                | Strongly _Collective security and order_           | No      |
| Force in domestic politics                  | Strongly _Permissible force against injustice_     | No      |
| Use of force                                | Strongly _Readiness to use force_                  | No      |
| Tempo of change                             | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_              | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life         | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_            | No      |
| Economic coordination                       | Leans _Public direction_ – Middle                  | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A people is a community of blood and soil, formed by its ancestry
over millennia, and the highest political duty is to secure its existence and the health
of its stock. The German nation, betrayed in 1918, divided by class war and party
squabbling and, in this view, undermined by an alien race, could survive only as a
_Volksgemeinschaft_: one people, united across class under a leader who embodies its
will, with land enough to live on and a state devoted to its biological and cultural
health. Postwar adherents apply the same principles to the White or "Aryan" race as a
whole.

**Key concepts.**

- **Volksgemeinschaft:** the national community of descent, above classes, parties and
  individuals.
- **Race:** the basic unit of history, ranked in worth. The doctrine casts Jews as a
  hostile race behind both finance capitalism and Bolshevism; in power, it ended in the
  Holocaust.
- **Leader principle (_Führerprinzip_):** authority flows down from a leader who embodies
  the people's will; responsibility flows up.
- **Living space (_Lebensraum_):** a people needs territory proportionate to its numbers
  and vitality, to be won by expansion.
- **Blood and soil:** a people is bound to the land it farms and defends.

**Signature reasons.**

- "One people, one Reich, one leader."
- "The common good comes before the individual good."
- "A people that doesn't protect its blood will disappear."
- "Neither capitalist nor Marxist: worker and nation together."
- "We must secure the existence of our people and a future for White children." (its
  postwar form)

**Telling it apart.**

- **Fascism:** the parent, centred on the state and the nation as a spiritual community;
  Italian fascism had no racial doctrine until 1938 (_Race and ethnicity_; centered
  group).
- **White nationalism:** mostly separatist, without a leader-state, one-party rule, or
  political violence (_Leadership_, _Pluralism vs. unity_, _Force in domestic politics_).
- **Ethnic nationalism:** a Thin Label for nations of shared descent, which can ride on
  democratic hosts and need not rank peoples (_Hierarchy or equality_, _Leadership_).
- **Falangism (national syndicalism):** Catholic and not racial (_Race and ethnicity_,
  _Religion and state_).
- **Conservative Revolution:** many of its thinkers rejected biological racism and the
  mass party, though some later joined it (_Race and ethnicity_; Reasons).

**Sources.** NSDAP Twenty-Five Point Programme (1920); Adolf Hitler, _Mein Kampf_
(1925–26); Alfred Rosenberg, _The Myth of the Twentieth Century_ (1930); George Lincoln
Rockwell, _White Power_ (1967); Ian Kershaw, _The Nazi Dictatorship_ (4th ed., 2000);
Richard J. Evans, _The Coming of the Third Reich_ (2003); Johann Chapoutot, _The Law of
Blood_ (2014); Jeffrey Kaplan (ed.), _Encyclopedia of White Power_ (2000).

## Falangism (national syndicalism)

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Fascism
- **Also called:** national syndicalism (_nacionalsindicalismo_); joseantonianism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                        | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Pluralism vs. unity                 | Strongly _Unified common purpose_                                                            | Yes     |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Public role for religion_ – Leans _Public role for religion_                       | Yes     |
| Economic coordination               | Strongly _Public direction_ – Leans _Public direction_                                       | Yes     |
| Leadership                          | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_                 | No      |
| Transcendence                       | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_                                                       | No      |
| Property in capital and goods       | Middle – Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_                                              | No      |
| Distribution                        | Middle – Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_                                                  | No      |
| Citizenship: rights vs. duties      | Strongly _Citizenship as shared obligation_                                                  | No      |
| Local vs. central                   | Leans _Central and uniform authority_ – Strongly _Central and uniform authority_             | No      |
| Basis of nationhood                 | Middle – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                                              | No      |
| Force in domestic politics          | Strongly _Permissible force against injustice_ – Leans _Permissible force against injustice_ | No      |
| Legal moralism                      | Leans _Enforcement of shared morality_ – Strongly _Enforcement of shared morality_           | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_                                                      | No      |
| Life and body                       | Strongly _Protection of human life and dignity_                                              | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Spain is not a sum of regions, classes or parties but "a unity of
destiny in the universal", a historic mission inseparable from the Catholic faith.
Liberal democracy divides the nation into parties, and capitalism and Marxism divide it
into warring classes; national syndicalism unites them by organising all economic life
into vertical syndicates in which employers and workers serve the nation together, with
credit nationalised and land reformed for those who work it. A disciplined movement must
lead this national revolution against both the old oligarchy and the left.

**Key concepts.**

- **Unity of destiny:** the nation is defined by a shared historic mission, not by race
  or by contract.
- **Vertical syndicate:** a single organisation per branch of production, joining
  employers and workers under the state.
- **National revolution:** a transformation against both liberal capitalism and Marxism,
  which later Falangists called "the pending revolution".
- **Catholic sense of life:** the faith as the core of the national tradition and the
  ground of human dignity.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Spain is a unity of destiny in the universal."
- "Neither right nor left: national syndicalist."
- "The economy should be one great syndicate of producers serving the nation."
- "The Catholic sense of life is the heart of our national tradition."
- "The revolution is still pending."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Fascism:** the parent; Falangism adds Catholicism and a syndicalist economy
  (_Religion and state_; Reasons).
- **National Socialism:** racial, where Falangism defines the nation by mission and faith
  (_Race and ethnicity_).
- **Counter-revolutionary traditionalism:** the Carlist tradition seeks a restored
  monarchy and estates; Falangism is a modern mass movement (_Tempo of change_,
  _Leadership_; Reasons).
- **Catholic integralism:** a doctrine of Church and state without mass mobilisation or
  a single party (_Pluralism vs. unity_; Reasons).
- **Neo-corporatism:** voluntary bargaining between organised capital and labour inside
  pluralist democracy (_Pluralism vs. unity_).

**Sources.** José Antonio Primo de Rivera, founding speech at the Teatro de la Comedia
(1933); Falange Española de las JONS, _Twenty-Seven Points_ (1934); Ramiro Ledesma Ramos,
_Discurso a las juventudes de España_ (1935); Sheelagh Ellwood, _Spanish Fascism in the
Franco Era_ (1987); Stanley Payne, _Fascism in Spain, 1923–1977_ (1999).

## Conservative Revolution

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** _Konservative Revolution_; revolutionary conservatism; Young
  Conservatives and National Revolutionaries (its main currents)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                                    | Central |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Tempo of change                   | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_ – Leans _Fundamental transformation_               | Yes     |
| Pluralism vs. unity               | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_                       | Yes     |
| Social hierarchy                  | Leans _Ordered rank and deference_ – Strongly _Ordered rank and deference_               | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes             | Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                                                 | No      |
| Hierarchy or equality             | Strongly _Hierarchy is natural_ – Leans _Hierarchy is natural_                           | No      |
| Direction of history              | Strongly _Progress is not assured_                                                       | No      |
| Tradition or reason               | Strongly _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ – Leans _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ | No      |
| Leadership                        | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_             | No      |
| Rule of law vs. discretion        | Strongly _Judgment for the case at hand_ – Leans _Judgment for the case at hand_         | No      |
| Citizenship: rights vs. duties    | Strongly _Citizenship as shared obligation_                                              | No      |
| Basis of nationhood               | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_             | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                                                     | No      |
| Foreign-policy aims               | Strongly _National interest_                                                             | No      |
| Economic coordination             | Leans _Public direction_ – Middle                                                        | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The old conservatism defended a world that 1918 had already
destroyed. Liberalism and parliamentarism have dissolved the nation into interest groups
and calculating individuals, and Western civilisation is entering its decline. What is
needed is neither restoration nor progress but a conservative revolution: to create
conditions worth conserving, a new organic order of community, hierarchy and service,
rooted in the people's living traditions and led by an authority able to decide. It is a
revolution of spirit and of the state, not a return to the past.

**Key concepts.**

- **Culture against civilisation:** a living, rooted culture (_Kultur_) against
  mechanical, commercial civilisation (_Zivilisation_).
- **The decision:** sovereignty is the power to decide in the exception, and politics is
  the distinction between friend and enemy (Schmitt).
- **Prussian socialism:** duty, service and order binding all classes, not class struggle
  (Spengler).
- **Organic community:** _Gemeinschaft_, bound by shared life, against _Gesellschaft_,
  bound by contract.
- **The Reich:** a new national order beyond both the monarchy and the Weimar Republic
  (Moeller van den Bruck's term, later taken over by the Nazis).

**Signature reasons.**

- "To be conservative is to create things worth conserving."
- "Nations perish of liberalism."
- "Sovereign is he who decides on the exception."
- "Parliaments talk; history is made by decisions."
- "Our civilisation is in its autumn, and only duty and discipline will carry it through."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Counter-revolutionary traditionalism:** wants to restore the pre-revolutionary order
  of throne and altar; the Conservative Revolution looks forward, often on secular or
  pagan grounds (_Religion and state_; Reasons).
- **Fascism:** a mass party in power; most Conservative Revolutionaries stayed elitist
  intellectuals, and several opposed the regimes that followed (Reasons).
- **National Socialism:** most of its thinkers rejected biological racism, though some
  joined the party (_Race and ethnicity_; Reasons).
- **European New Right:** its postwar heir, which rejects totalitarianism and favours a
  federal Europe of regions (_Local vs. central_; Reasons).
- **Traditionalism:** grounds its critique of modernity in a metaphysics of primordial
  tradition, not in the nation (_Transcendence_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Oswald Spengler, _The Decline of the West_ (1918–22) and _Prussianism and
Socialism_ (1919); Arthur Moeller van den Bruck, _Das dritte Reich_ (1923); Carl Schmitt,
_Political Theology_ (1922) and _The Concept of the Political_ (1932); Edgar Julius Jung,
_Die Herrschaft der Minderwertigen_ (1927); Ernst Jünger, _Der Arbeiter_ (1932); Armin
Mohler, _Die Konservative Revolution in Deutschland 1918–1932_ (1950); Jeffrey Herf,
_Reactionary Modernism_ (1984); Roger Woods, _The Conservative Revolution in the Weimar
Republic_ (1996).

## Traditionalism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** the Traditionalist School; perennialism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                    | Central |

| Social hierarchy                    | Strongly _Ordered rank and deference_                                    | Yes     |
| Hierarchy or equality               | Strongly _Hierarchy is natural_                                          | No      |
| Tradition or reason                 | Strongly _Inherited practice carries wisdom_                             | No      |
| Source of moral authority           | Strongly _Given from beyond us_                                          | No      |
| Grounds for belief                  | Strongly _Belief rightly held on other grounds_                          | No      |
| Self-authorship or given role       | Strongly _Live up to a given role_                                       | No      |
| What makes a life go well           | Strongly _Some things are good for anyone_                               | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise      | Strongly _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_ | No      |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Public role for religion_                                      | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                 | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_       | No      |
| Growth                              | Strongly _Sufficiency and limits_ – Leans _Sufficiency and limits_       | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_                                  | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Every authentic religion and civilisation once expressed a single
primordial Tradition: knowledge of the sacred, transcendent principle on which all true
order rests. History is not progress but a descent through cosmic cycles, and the modern
world, with its materialism, individualism, egalitarianism and worship of quantity, is
the dark end of that decline. The task is to recover contact with Tradition through an
orthodox religious path and, for those drawn to politics, to stand upright among the
ruins and uphold a spiritual hierarchy in which the higher rules the lower.

**Key concepts.**

- **Tradition:** a primordial, superhuman wisdom of which the world's orthodox religions
  are outward forms.
- **The cycle of ages:** history descends from a golden age to the present dark age (the
  Kali Yuga).
- **The reign of quantity:** modernity measures everything and values nothing; the mass
  replaces the person and the elite.
- **Spiritual hierarchy:** authority descends from the sacred; Guénon puts spiritual
  authority above temporal power, Evola exalts the sacred warrior-king.
- **Initiation:** real knowledge is reached through traditional spiritual paths, by the
  few.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The modern world is not progress but the last stage of a long decline."
- "All true religions share one primordial Tradition."
- "Quantity has replaced quality, and the masses have replaced the elite."
- "Hierarchy reflects a sacred order, not a social convention."
- "Ride the tiger: stand upright among the ruins."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Counter-revolutionary traditionalism:** rejects modernity from Catholic doctrine and
  seeks to restore a specific Christian order (Reasons).
- **Traditionalist conservatism:** defends inherited institutions within modern society
  and changes them gradually (_Social hierarchy_, _Direction of history_).
- **Conservative Revolution:** a national, often secular current; Evola bridged the two
  (_Transcendence_; Reasons).
- **Eurasianism:** borrows Traditionalist metaphysics for a geopolitical programme
  centred on Russia-Eurasia (centered group; Reasons).
- **Fascism:** Evola criticised it from the right as too modern and plebeian (Reasons).

**Sources.** René Guénon, _The Crisis of the Modern World_ (1927) and _The Reign of
Quantity and the Signs of the Times_ (1945); Julius Evola, _Revolt Against the Modern
World_ (1934), _Men Among the Ruins_ (1953) and _Ride the Tiger_ (1961); Frithjof Schuon,
_The Transcendent Unity of Religions_ (1948); Mark Sedgwick, _Against the Modern World_
(2004); Benjamin Teitelbaum, _War for Eternity_ (2020).

| Immigration and borders          | Strongly _Community control over membership_ – Leans _Community control over membership_         | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Humanity exists only as a plurality of peoples, each with its own
culture, history and way of being, and that diversity is the world's true wealth. Liberal
universalism, global markets and Americanisation are levelling every people into
interchangeable consumers, and the ideology of human rights is their instrument. The
European New Right defends every people's right to difference, Europe's included: a
federal Europe of rooted regions and nations, organic and participatory democracy, and a
struggle won first in culture and ideas before it can be won in politics.

**Key concepts.**

- **Ethnopluralism:** every people has the right to its own identity and territory;
  mixing and homogenisation, not difference, are the threat.
- **Metapolitics:** cultural hegemony precedes political power, so the work is in ideas,
  not elections (a "Gramscianism of the right").
- **Anti-universalism:** universal human rights and liberal values are one civilisation's
  particular values presented as everyone's.
- **Empire:** a federal, subsidiarist Europe of regions and peoples, against both the
  nation-state's centralism and the European Union's technocracy.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The real threat to identity isn't the stranger; it's the global market that makes
  everyone the same."
- "The diversity of peoples is the world's wealth; uniformity is its death."
- "Every people has the right to remain itself."
- "Political power follows cultural power, so win the culture first."
- "Human rights are one civilisation's values dressed up as universal law."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Identitarianism:** its activist offshoot, focused on stopping immigration into Europe
  (_Immigration and borders_, _Channels of change_; centered group).
- **Multicultural liberalism:** also recognises groups, but grounds that in individual
  autonomy and universal rights (_Rights: natural or man-made_, _Moral objectivity_).
- **Conservative Revolution:** the ancestor it chose, without the authoritarian state
  (_Leadership_, _Local vs. central_; Reasons).
- **National conservatism:** defends the nation-state and often Christianity; the European
  New Right is regionalist, federalist, often pagan and anti-capitalist (_Local vs.
  central_, _Moral limits of markets_).
- **White nationalism:** centers a race across nations and often uses biological race;
  the European New Right defends each people's culture (_Race and ethnicity_; centered
  group).
- **Eurasianism:** shares anti-universalism and multipolarity, centred on Russia-Eurasia
  (centered group).

**Sources.** Alain de Benoist, _Vu de droite_ (1977); Alain de Benoist and Charles
Champetier, "Manifesto for a European Renaissance" (1999); Guillaume Faye,
_Archeofuturism_ (1998); Alain de Benoist, _Beyond Human Rights_ (2004); Pierre-André
Taguieff, _Sur la Nouvelle droite_ (1994); Michael O'Meara, _New Culture, New Right_
(2004); Tamir Bar-On, _Where Have All the Fascists Gone?_ (2007).

## Identitarianism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** European New Right
- **Also called:** the Identitarian movement (_Génération Identitaire_, _Identitäre
  Bewegung_)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** Europe's native peoples, at the regional, national and European
  levels together

### Positions

| Dimension                                   | Range                                                                                                               | Central |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Immigration and borders                     | Strongly _Community control over membership_                                                                        | Yes     |
| Basis of nationhood                         | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                                        | Yes     |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_ – Leans _Special obligations to compatriots_                          | No      |
| Moral circle                                | Strongly _Near and dear first_ – Leans _Near and dear first_                                                        | No      |
| Individuals or wholes                       | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                                    | No      |
| Race and ethnicity                          | Leans _Group differences are largely inherited_ – Leans _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ | No      |
| Channels of change                          | Middle – Leans _Beyond institutions_                                                                                | No      |
| Force in domestic politics                  | Middle – Leans _Principled nonviolence_                                                                             | No      |
| Speech and expression                       | Strongly _Maximal expressive freedom_ – Leans _Maximal expressive freedom_                                          | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance           | Middle – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                                                                       | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Europe's peoples have a right to remain themselves, and that
identity is now threatened by mass immigration and Islamisation, which within a
generation or two will make native Europeans minorities in their own homelands.
Identitarians are a youth movement of Europeans who refuse that future: through peaceful
activism, provocative direct action and work in culture and ideas, they defend their
regional, national and European identity, and call for an end to mass immigration and for
remigration.

**Key concepts.**

- **Identity:** rooted belonging at three levels at once: region, nation and European
  civilisation.
- **The Great Replacement:** the claim that native Europeans are being demographically
  replaced by non-European immigrants.
- **Remigration:** the return of non-European immigrants to their countries of origin,
  which most Identitarians frame as lawful and voluntary or incentivised.
- **Ethnopluralism:** every people has a right to its own homeland, inherited from the
  European New Right.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Zero percent racism, one hundred percent identity."
- "We are the generation that will not be replaced."
- "Every people has a right to its homeland, Europeans included."
- "Remigration, not integration."
- "Defend Europe."

**Telling it apart.**

- **European New Right:** the parent, a school of ideas; Identitarianism is the activist
  movement, centred on immigration (_Immigration and borders_, _Channels of change_).
- **White nationalism:** centers a race spanning nations and often argues from biology;
  Identitarians center Europe's peoples ethnoculturally (centered group; _Race and
  ethnicity_).
- **National populism:** a party-electoral movement that defines the nation by culture
  and citizenship and accepts assimilated minorities (_Basis of nationhood_, _Channels of
  change_).
- **Ethnic nationalism:** a Thin Label for one nation of shared descent; Identitarians
  defend region, nation and Europe together (centered group).

**Sources.** Guillaume Faye, _Why We Fight_ (2001); Renaud Camus, _Le Grand Remplacement_
(2011); Markus Willinger, _Generation Identity: A Declaration of War Against the '68ers_
(2013); Martin Sellner, _Remigration: Ein Vorschlag_ (2024); José Pedro Zúquete, _The
Identitarians: The Movement against Globalism and Islam in Europe_ (2018).

## White nationalism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** White advocacy, racial nationalism, White separatism (for its
  separatist form); critics: "White supremacy" (a distinct claim; see _Telling it
  apart_)
- **Variants:** Alt-right
- **Centered group:** people of European descent, across national borders

### Positions

| Dimension                                   | Range                                                                                      | Central |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Basis of nationhood                         | Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                                                     | Yes     |
| Immigration and borders                     | Strongly _Community control over membership_                                               | Yes     |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_ – Leans _Special obligations to compatriots_ | Yes     |
| Moral circle                                | Strongly _Near and dear first_ – Leans _Near and dear first_                               | No      |
| Race and ethnicity                          | Strongly _Group differences are largely inherited_ – Middle                                | No      |
| Individuals or wholes                       | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_           | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                         | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                    | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance           | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                   | No      |
| Trade and economic openness                 | Middle – Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_                                     | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Peoples of European descent form a distinct extended family,
bound by shared ancestry and a shared civilizational inheritance. Like every other
people, they have a legitimate interest in their own survival and in homelands where
they remain the majority and set the culture. Mass immigration and low birth rates are
turning them into minorities in the countries their ancestors built, and they should be
free to say so and to organize to prevent it, as other groups already do.

**Key concepts.**

- **Nation:** a people of common descent, not the holders of a passport; citizenship
  should follow peoplehood.
- **Self-determination:** the right of a people, not only of individuals, to a homeland
  and to its own future, applied to Europeans as to anyone.
- **Equality:** for its separatist majority, peoples are different and each is entitled to
  its own space; for a supremacist minority, peoples are ranked (see _Telling it
  apart_).
- **Diversity:** a source of conflict, distrust, and the dilution of a people, rather than
  a strength.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Every people has a right to its own homeland, and that includes White people."
- "A nation is a people, not a passport."
- "Demographic change is replacing the historic majority, and nobody asked us."
- "Other groups are allowed ethnic solidarity. We are told ours is evil."
- "Racial differences are real, and they matter for how societies work." (its
  hereditarian strand)

**Centered group.** People of European descent as one pan-national group, rather than
any single nation such as the French or the Hungarians.

**Telling it apart.**

- **Ethnic nationalism:** centers one particular nation (Hungarians, Serbs); White
  nationalism centers a race that spans nations (centered group).
- **National populism:** also restricts immigration, but defines the nation by culture
  and citizenship and accepts non-White fellow citizens (_Basis of nationhood_, the
  central Dimension).
- **Identitarianism:** rooted in regional and national European identities and framed
  ethnoculturally, mostly avoiding biological race (centered group; _Race and
  ethnicity_; Reasons).
- **National Socialism:** adds a totalitarian one-party state, a leader principle, and
  political violence (_Leadership_, _Pluralism vs. unity_, _Force in domestic
  politics_).
- **"White supremacy":** strictly, the claim that White people should rule over other
  races (_Social hierarchy_, _Hierarchy or equality_). Most self-described White
  nationalists are separatists and reject it; a person who holds it is described by those
  Dimensions, not by this Label's name alone.
- **Black nationalism:** the same shape, centering people of African descent (centered
  group).

**Sources.** Jared Taylor, _White Identity_ (2011); Greg Johnson, _The White
Nationalist Manifesto_ (2018); Carol M. Swain, _The New White Nationalism in America_
(2002); Carol M. Swain & Russ Nieli (eds.), _Contemporary Voices of White Nationalism
in America_ (2003); George Hawley, _Making Sense of the Alt-Right_ (2017); Cas Mudde,
_The Far Right Today_ (2019).

## National populism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** right-wing populism; adherents: "patriots", "sovereigntists";
  scholars: populist radical right; critics: "far right"
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                                         | Range                                                                                      | Central |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Immigration and borders                           | Strongly _Community control over membership_ – Leans _Community control over membership_   | Yes     |
| Security and civil liberties                      | Leans _Collective security and order_ – Strongly _Collective security and order_           | Yes     |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise                    | Leans _Equal popular judgment_ – Strongly _Equal popular judgment_                         | Yes     |
| Basis of nationhood                               | Middle – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                                            | No      |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality       | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_ – Leans _Special obligations to compatriots_ | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance                 | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                   | No      |
| Popular sovereignty vs. constitutional constraint | Middle – Strongly _Popular and legislative supremacy_                                      | No      |
| Expert trust                                      | Leans _Judge for yourself_ – Strongly _Judge for yourself_                                 | No      |
| Punishment                                        | Strongly _Desert and deterrence_ – Leans _Desert and deterrence_                           | No      |
| Group difference                                  | Leans _Difference-blind common rules_ – Strongly _Difference-blind common rules_           | No      |
| Leadership                                        | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Middle                                            | No      |
| Representation vs. participation                  | Leans _Direct and participatory self-rule_ – Middle                                        | No      |
| Trade and economic openness                       | Middle – Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_                                     | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                               | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                    | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Ordinary working people have been ignored by political, media and
business elites who embraced mass immigration, globalisation and supranational rule
without asking them. National populists speak for the nation's people: they want
democratic control back over borders, laws and the economy, safe streets and firm order,
and a national culture that newcomers join rather than replace. They are democrats, not
revolutionaries; what they reject is an establishment that treats voters' concerns as
illegitimate.

**Key concepts.**

- **The people:** the nation's ordinary, decent majority, as against both elites above and
  outsiders.
- **The elite:** a cosmopolitan class in politics, media, courts and business, detached
  from the nation and its concerns.
- **Nativism:** the state belongs first to the nation's own people, and large-scale
  immigration threatens its culture and cohesion.
- **Sovereignty:** decisions belong to the national electorate, not to courts,
  bureaucracies or supranational bodies.
- **Order:** firm law enforcement and punishment as the first duty of the state.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Take back control of our borders and our laws."
- "The people are sovereign, not judges, bureaucrats or Brussels."
- "Our own people first."
- "Nobody voted for mass immigration."
- "Safe streets are the first freedom."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Populism:** the Thin Label it carries; National populism is populism hosted on
  nativism and order (_Immigration and borders_, _Security and civil liberties_).
- **National conservatism:** an intellectual, often religious current led by elites;
  National populism makes anti-elitism and popular judgment central (_Popular judgment vs.
  expertise_; Reasons).
- **White nationalism:** defines the nation by race, where National populism accepts
  fellow citizens of other races who share the culture (_Basis of nationhood_).
- **Identitarianism:** an ethnocultural activist movement outside party politics (_Basis
  of nationhood_, _Channels of change_).
- **Fascism:** rejects electoral democracy and party competition; National populism works
  within them (_Pluralism vs. unity_, _Channels of change_).

**Sources.** Marine Le Pen, _Pour que vive la France_ (2012); Cas Mudde, _Populist
Radical Right Parties in Europe_ (2007); Jan-Werner Müller, _What Is Populism?_ (2016);
Roger Eatwell and Matthew Goodwin, _National Populism: The Revolt Against Liberal
Democracy_ (2018); Pippa Norris and Ronald Inglehart, _Cultural Backlash_ (2019).

## Eurasianism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** neo-Eurasianism (its post-Soviet form); the Fourth Political Theory
  (Dugin's own name for his version)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** the peoples of Russia-Eurasia, a civilisation of Slavic, Turkic,
  Finno-Ugric and other peoples around a Russian core

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Foreign-policy aims                 | Leans _National interest_ – Strongly _National interest_                                         | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes               | Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                                                         | Yes     |
| Rights: natural or man-made         | Leans _Rights are created by society_ – Strongly _Rights are created by society_                 | No      |
| Tradition or reason                 | Strongly _Inherited practice carries wisdom_                                                     | No      |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Public role for religion_ – Leans _Public role for religion_                           | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                 | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_                               | No      |
| Leadership                          | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_                     | No      |
| Basis of nationhood                 | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                     | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance   | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                         | No      |
| Use of force                        | Strongly _Readiness to use force_ – Leans _Readiness to use force_                               | No      |
| Trade and economic openness         | Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_ – Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_ | No      |
| Direction of history                | Leans _Progress is not assured_ – Strongly _Progress is not assured_                             | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_                   | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Russia is neither Europe nor Asia but a distinct civilisation,
Eurasia, formed by the shared history of Slavic, Turkic and other peoples across one great
continental space. Liberalism, which claims to be universal, is the particular ideology
of the Atlantic West, and its global dominance destroys every other civilisation's way of
life. Eurasianists call for a multipolar world of sovereign civilisations, each living by
its own traditions, faiths and values, with Russia-Eurasia as one great pole against
Atlanticist hegemony.

**Key concepts.**

- **Civilisation:** the real subject of history, a community of peoples shaped by a shared
  space and destiny, larger than a nation-state.
- **Multipolarity:** a world order of several civilisational poles, against a unipolar,
  American-led one.
- **Land against sea:** a geopolitics of continental, tradition-bound powers against
  maritime, commercial ones.
- **The Fourth Political Theory:** a politics beyond liberalism, communism and fascism,
  taking a people's being, not the individual, class or race, as its subject (Dugin).
- **Rights:** values belong to particular civilisations; universal human rights are the
  West's values in universal dress.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Russia is not a nation-state; it is a civilisation."
- "Liberal values are Western values, not universal ones."
- "The world should have many poles, not one master."
- "Every civilisation has the right to its own path."
- "Sea powers have always sought to encircle the land."

**Telling it apart.**

- **European New Right:** shares anti-universalism and ethnopluralism, centred on Europe's
  peoples (centered group).
- **National conservatism:** defends the nation-state; Eurasianism a multi-ethnic
  civilisation-state (_Basis of nationhood_; centered group).
- **Traditionalism:** the metaphysics Eurasianism borrows, without its geopolitical
  programme (Reasons).
- **Realism:** also national interest and multipolarity, without civilisational
  metaphysics (_Individuals or wholes_; Reasons).
- **Fascism:** a single nation's rebirth through a mass party; Eurasianism claims to move
  beyond it, centred on a multi-ethnic civilisation (centered group; Reasons).

**Sources.** Nikolai Trubetzkoy, _Europe and Mankind_ (1920); Petr Savitsky and others,
_Exodus to the East_ (1921); Lev Gumilev, _Ethnogenesis and the Biosphere_ (1978);
Aleksandr Dugin, _Foundations of Geopolitics_ (1997) and _The Fourth Political Theory_
(2009); Marlene Laruelle, _Russian Eurasianism: An Ideology of Empire_ (2008); Mark
Bassin, Sergey Glebov and Marlene Laruelle (eds.), _Between Europe and Asia_ (2015).

## Neoreaction

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** NRx; the Dark Enlightenment (Land's term); neocameralism and formalism
  (Yarvin's terms for its programme)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                      | Range                                                                            | Central |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Leadership                     | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_                                           | Yes     |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise | Strongly _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_         | Yes     |
| Political authority            | Strongly _Legitimate state authority_ – Middle                                   | No      |
| Hierarchy or equality          | Strongly _Hierarchy is natural_ – Leans _Hierarchy is natural_                   | No      |
| Human nature                   | Strongly _Fixed and flawed_ – Leans _Fixed and flawed_                           | No      |
| Given or made                  | Strongly _Given by nature_ – Leans _Given by nature_                             | No      |
| Race and ethnicity             | Strongly _Group differences are largely inherited_ – Middle                      | No      |
| Direction of history           | Leans _Progress is not assured_ – Strongly _Progress is not assured_             | No      |
| Expert trust                   | Leans _Judge for yourself_ – Strongly _Judge for yourself_                       | No      |
| Security and civil liberties   | Leans _Collective security and order_ – Strongly _Collective security and order_ | No      |
| Economic coordination          | Middle – Strongly _Market coordination_                                          | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Democracy does not deliver good government: it rewards short-term
promises, grows the state without limit, and hands real power to an unaccountable,
self-perpetuating establishment of universities, media and bureaucracy. Neoreactionaries
propose to make power formal and accountable instead: run the state like a well-managed
company, with a single sovereign executive answerable for results, secure property and
order, and let citizens vote with their feet, choosing among many competing polities
rather than voting within one.

**Key concepts.**

- **The Cathedral:** the informal power of universities and the press, which sets
  opinion and policy without being elected or accountable.
- **Formalism:** make the real distribution of power official and secure, so it no
  longer needs to be fought over.
- **Neocameralism:** the state as a sovereign corporation run for its owners, with every
  reason to govern well.
- **Exit over voice:** freedom to leave a polity (the facet "exit and competing
  jurisdictions") matters more than a vote within it; a "patchwork" of many small
  sovereign states.
- **Whig history:** the progressive story of history, which neoreaction rejects as the
  victors' narrative.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Voice doesn't work; exit does."
- "A state run like a company would have every reason to govern well."
- "Real power belongs to the universities and the press, and nobody elects them."
- "Democracy turns long-term problems into short-term promises."
- "History isn't on anyone's side; the Whigs just wrote it."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Counter-revolutionary traditionalism:** also rejects democracy and equality, on
  religious grounds and for throne and altar; neoreaction is secular and corporate
  (_Transcendence_, _Religion and state_).
- **Anarcho-capitalism:** also prizes exit and competing jurisdictions, but without a
  sovereign; neoreaction keeps absolute authority inside each polity (_Political
  authority_, _Leadership_).
- **Network state:** also founds new polities people choose to join, but by consent and
  usually with internal self-government (_Leadership_; Reasons).
- **Landian accelerationism:** Land co-founded neoreaction, but accelerationism celebrates
  techno-capital beyond human control, not stable sovereign order (_Technology and risk_;
  Reasons).
- **Epistocracy:** weights political power toward knowledge within a democratic frame
  (_Leadership_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Hans-Hermann Hoppe, _Democracy: The God That Failed_ (2001); Curtis Yarvin
(as Mencius Moldbug), "An Open Letter to Open-Minded Progressives" (2008) and "Patchwork:
A Political System for the 21st Century" (2008); Nick Land, "The Dark Enlightenment"
(2012); Joshua Tait, "Mencius Moldbug and Neoreaction", in Mark Sedgwick (ed.), _Key
Thinkers of the Radical Right_ (2019).

## Ecofascism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Fascism / Ecologism
- **Also called:** green nationalism (some adherents); the name "ecofascism" began as a
  critics' term and is now used by some adherents themselves
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                    | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| ---------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Environmental protection     | Strongly _Ecological protection first_                                                           | Yes     |
| Immigration and borders      | Strongly _Community control over membership_                                                     | Yes     |
| Leadership                   | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_                     | Yes     |
| Standing of nature           | Leans _Nature-centered_ – Strongly _Nature-centered_                                             | No      |
| Growth                       | Strongly _Sufficiency and limits_ – Leans _Sufficiency and limits_                               | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity          | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_                               | No      |
| Individuals or wholes        | Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                                                         | No      |
| Moral circle                 | Strongly _Near and dear first_ – Leans _Near and dear first_                                     | No      |
| Basis of nationhood          | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                     | No      |
| Security and civil liberties | Leans _Collective security and order_ – Strongly _Collective security and order_                 | No      |
| Technology and risk          | Leans _Precaution_ – Strongly _Precaution_                                                       | No      |
| Trade and economic openness  | Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_ – Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_ | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The earth is finite, and human numbers and consumption are
destroying the living world on which every people depends. Liberal democracy and global
capitalism cannot stop this, because they are built on growth, open borders and
short-term consumer desire. Ecofascists hold that only a strong national state, willing
to override individual preferences, could defend a people's land and natural heritage,
end mass immigration, cut population and consumption, and bind the nation to its soil
again.

**Key concepts.**

- **Blood and soil:** a people and its land form one whole; destroying the land destroys
  the people.
- **Overpopulation:** human numbers, globally and through immigration, as the root
  ecological threat.
- **Carrying capacity:** a land can support only so many people at a given level of
  consumption.
- **Natural order:** human society should conform to nature's hierarchies and limits,
  not to liberal ideals of equality.

**Signature reasons.**

- "A finite planet can't support endless growth, or endless people."
- "A people and its land are one; destroy the land and you destroy the people."
- "Democracy will never vote for the sacrifices survival requires."
- "Mass immigration is an ecological problem as well as a national one."
- "Saving nature means putting the whole before the individual."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Deep ecology:** shares the intrinsic value of nature and population reduction, but is
  egalitarian and anti-authoritarian (_Leadership_, _Moral circle_, _Immigration and
  borders_).
- **Eco-conservatism:** love of home and local stewardship within liberal democracy
  (_Leadership_, _Pluralism vs. unity_).
- **Green politics:** grassroots democracy, social justice and nonviolence (_Leadership_,
  _Immigration and borders_).
- **Fascism:** a parent, for which ecology is not the core (_Environmental protection_).
- **National Socialism:** a historical source through "blood and soil", but centred on
  race and expansion (_Race and ethnicity_; centered group).

**Sources.** Garrett Hardin, "Lifeboat Ethics" (1974), an influence it draws on; Anna
Bramwell, _Blood and Soil: Richard Walther Darré and Hitler's "Green Party"_ (1985);
Janet Biehl and Peter Staudenmaier, _Ecofascism: Lessons from the German Experience_
(1995); Pentti Linkola, _Can Life Prevail?_ (2004); Sam Moore and Alex Roberts, _The
Rise of Ecofascism_ (2022).
