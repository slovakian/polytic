# National movements and national-developmental regimes: Label cards

Cards for this family, written to the template in `../label-library.md`, in the order
of the list. National movements without a distinct canon (Scottish, Catalan, Québécois)
are not here; they match the generic Thin Labels under Nationalism. Every card here is
written with the same dispassion as any other: the self-description is the tradition's
own self-understanding, steelmanned, and never Polytic's verdict (`../principles.md`).

## Peronism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Justicialism (_justicialismo_, its official name); Kirchnerism and
  Menemism (later currents)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Workplace governance              | Strongly _Worker voice and self-management_ – Leans _Worker voice and self-management_           | Yes     |
| Leadership                        | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_                     | Yes     |
| Economic coordination             | Strongly _Public direction_ – Middle                                                             | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance      | Leans _Public social insurance_ – Strongly _Public social insurance_                             | No      |
| Distribution                      | Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_ – Strongly _Egalitarian redistribution_                       | No      |
| Trade and economic openness       | Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_ – Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_ | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                         | No      |
| Foreign-policy aims               | Leans _National interest_ – Strongly _National interest_                                         | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise    | Leans _Equal popular judgment_ – Strongly _Equal popular judgment_                               | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity               | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                          | No      |
| Individuals or wholes             | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                 | No      |
| Social hierarchy                  | Strongly _Equality of status_ – Leans _Equality of status_                                       | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A nation is free only if it is socially just, economically
independent and politically sovereign. Peronism is the movement of the workers and the
humble, organised in their unions and gathered around a leader who carries their will
against the oligarchy and foreign interests. It rejects both individualist capitalism and
collectivist communism for a third position: an organised community in which capital and
labour cooperate under a state that guarantees workers' rights, dignity and a fair share
of the nation's wealth.

**Key concepts.**

- **Social justice:** the dignity of the worker and a fair share of national wealth,
  guaranteed by the state.
- **The organised community:** society organised through its unions, professions and
  associations in harmony with the state, beyond class struggle.
- **The Third Position:** neither capitalism nor communism at home, neither bloc abroad.
- **Leadership (_conducción_):** a leader who interprets and directs the people's will,
  joined to them directly rather than through parties.
- **The unions:** the "backbone" of the movement, not only bargaining agents.

**Signature reasons.**

- "A socially just, economically free and politically sovereign nation."
- "Neither Yankees nor Marxists: Peronists."
- "Where there is a need, a right is born."
- "The unions are the backbone of the movement."
- "The only privileged ones are the children."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Social democracy:** also built on unions and welfare, but parliamentary and pluralist,
  with leaders less central (_Leadership_, _Pluralism vs. unity_).
- **Bolivarianism (socialism of the 21st century):** also leader-centred and
  anti-imperialist, but aims at socialism through communal councils (_Property in capital
  and goods_; Reasons).
- **Fascism:** also a corporatist third way, but abolishes elections and independent
  unions; Peronism competes in elections and rests on organised labour (_Pluralism vs.
  unity_, _Channels of change_).
- **Gaullism:** also a strong executive and state-led modernisation, without a
  labour-movement base (_Workplace governance_).
- **Left populism:** a Thin Label that Peronism's Kirchnerist current often carries
  (Reasons).

**Sources.** Juan Domingo Perón, _La comunidad organizada_ (1949) and _Conducción
política_ (1952); "Las veinte verdades del justicialismo" (1950); Eva Perón, _La razón de
mi vida_ (1951); Daniel James, _Resistance and Integration: Peronism and the Argentine
Working Class_ (1988); Steven Levitsky, _Transforming Labor-Based Parties in Latin
America_ (2003).

## Kemalism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Atatürkism (_Atatürkçülük_); the Six Arrows (_Altı Ok_)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Secular public order_                                                      | Yes     |
| Design vs. evolved order            | Leans _Deliberate design_ – Strongly _Deliberate design_                             | Yes     |
| Tempo of change                     | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_ – Leans _Fundamental transformation_           | No      |
| Tradition or reason                 | Leans _Reason should audit inheritance_ – Strongly _Reason should audit inheritance_ | No      |
| Expert trust                        | Strongly _Defer to expert institutions_ – Leans _Defer to expert institutions_       | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise      | Strongly _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_             | No      |
| Economic coordination               | Strongly _Public direction_ – Leans _Public direction_                               | No      |
| Basis of nationhood                 | Leans _Shared civic principles_ – Middle                                             | No      |
| Group difference                    | Strongly _Difference-blind common rules_                                             | No      |
| Local vs. central                   | Leans _Central and uniform authority_ – Strongly _Central and uniform authority_     | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance   | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_             | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_ – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_ | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                 | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                              | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A nation can be free and strong only if it joins contemporary
civilisation, through reason, science and secular law, not superstition, dynastic rule or
religious authority. Kemalism built the Turkish Republic on six principles:
republicanism, nationalism, populism (a classless nation of citizens), laicism, statism
and reformism, carried out by a modernising state from above. Sovereignty belongs
unconditionally to the nation, citizenship rather than religion or ancestry makes a Turk,
and the republic must guard its secular and unitary character against those who would
undo it.

**Key concepts.**

- **The Six Arrows:** republicanism, nationalism, populism, laicism, statism, reformism.
- **Laicism (_laiklik_):** religion kept out of law, education and politics, and
  supervised by the state, more than a separation of church and state.
- **Populism (_halkçılık_):** the nation as one solidarist people without class privilege,
  not rule by popular majorities.
- **Statism (_devletçilik_):** the state builds the industry private capital cannot.
- **Reformism (_inkılapçılık_):** continuing revolutionary transformation of law, script,
  dress and education.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Sovereignty belongs unconditionally to the nation."
- "The truest guide in life is science."
- "Peace at home, peace in the world."
- "How happy is the one who says, 'I am a Turk.'"
- "We will raise our nation to the level of contemporary civilisation."

**Telling it apart.**

- **French republicanism:** also secular, unitary and civic, but liberal-democratic and
  without statist tutelage (_Economic coordination_, _Popular judgment vs. expertise_).
- **Gaullism:** also a strong state and national independence, without a laicist
  revolution (_Religion and state_, _Tempo of change_).
- **Muslim democracy:** the opposite answer on religion in public life (_Religion and
  state_).
- **Three Principles of the People:** also nation-building through staged tutelage, from
  Confucian and Chinese sources rather than militant laicism (_Religion and state_;
  Reasons).
- **Developmental state:** an economic doctrine of bureaucratic guidance, without
  Kemalism's cultural revolution (Reasons).

**Sources.** Mustafa Kemal Atatürk, _Nutuk_ (the Great Speech, 1927); Republican
People's Party programme (1931), with the Six Arrows written into the constitution in
1937; Ziya Gökalp, _The Principles of Turkism_ (1923); Andrew Mango, _Atatürk_ (1999); M.
Şükrü Hanioğlu, _Atatürk: An Intellectual Biography_ (2011); Erik J. Zürcher, _Turkey: A
Modern History_ (4th ed., 2017).

## Labour Zionism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Zionism
- **Also called:** Socialist Zionism; Labor Zionism (US spelling)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** the Jewish people

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                                        | Central |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Basis of nationhood               | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                 | Yes     |
| Economic coordination             | Strongly _Public direction_ – Middle                                                         | Yes     |
| Workplace governance              | Strongly _Worker voice and self-management_ – Leans _Worker voice and self-management_       | No      |
| Work                              | Strongly _Work is a source of worth_ – Leans _Work is a source of worth_                     | No      |
| Land and natural resources        | Strongly _Common inheritance_ – Leans _Common inheritance_                                   | No      |
| Distribution                      | Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_ – Strongly _Egalitarian redistribution_                   | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance      | Leans _Public social insurance_ – Strongly _Public social insurance_                         | No      |
| Religion and state                | Middle – Leans _Secular public order_                                                        | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity               | Strongly _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_ – Leans _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_ | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                     | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The Jewish people could be redeemed as a nation only by returning
to its land and to productive work: by becoming farmers and workers again, instead of a
people confined to the margins of other nations' economies. Labour Zionists built that
national home through collective settlement (the kibbutz and moshav), a workers'
federation (the Histadrut) and national ownership of land, joining national liberation to
social justice. A Jewish state should be democratic and egalitarian, with its
institutions built by those who labour.

**Key concepts.**

- **Conquest of labour:** Jews doing the nation's work themselves, on the land and in
  industry, as the ground of national rebirth.
- **Religion of labour:** physical work on the land as the renewal of the person and the
  people (A. D. Gordon).
- **Constructivism:** the state built practically, settlement by settlement and
  institution by institution, before it is declared.
- **Class and nation:** the Jewish working class as the vanguard of national liberation
  (Borochov).
- **National land:** land held by the nation and leased to those who work it.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Through labour, the Jewish people will become a nation again."
- "A homeland is built by its own people's hands, one plot of land after another."
- "National liberation and social liberation are one struggle."
- "The land belongs to the nation, not to speculators."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Zionism:** the parent (Thin), to which Labour Zionism adds socialist economics and the
  ethic of labour (_Economic coordination_, _Work_).
- **Revisionist Zionism:** a liberal-national economy and military strength first
  (_Economic coordination_, _Use of force_).
- **Religious Zionism:** grounds the return in religious redemption (_Transcendence_,
  _Religion and state_).
- **Social democracy:** the same economics without a national project (centered group;
  _Basis of nationhood_).
- **Co-operativism:** shares the kibbutz and co-operative model without the national aim
  (centered group; Reasons).

**Sources.** Nachman Syrkin, _The Jewish Problem and the Socialist-Jewish State_ (1898);
Ber Borochov, "Our Platform" (1906); A. D. Gordon, _Selected Essays_ (trans. 1938); David
Ben-Gurion, _Rebirth and Destiny of Israel_ (1954); Arthur Hertzberg (ed.), _The Zionist
Idea_ (1959); Anita Shapira, _Berl: The Biography of a Socialist Zionist_ (1984).

## Revisionist Zionism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Zionism
- **Also called:** Jabotinskyism; national-liberal Zionism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** the Jewish people

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                                  | Central |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Basis of nationhood               | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_           | Yes     |
| Use of force                      | Strongly _Readiness to use force_                                                      | Yes     |
| Economic coordination             | Middle – Leans _Market coordination_                                                   | Yes     |
| Workplace governance              | Middle – Leans _Owner and managerial authority_                                        | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance      | Middle – Leans _Public social insurance_                                               | No      |
| Individuals or wholes             | Leans _Only individuals are real_ – Middle                                             | No      |
| Citizenship: rights vs. duties    | Strongly _Citizenship as shared obligation_ – Leans _Citizenship as shared obligation_ | No      |
| Foreign-policy aims               | Leans _National interest_ – Strongly _National interest_                               | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_               | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity               | Strongly _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_ – Middle                                | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A Jewish state will not be granted by the goodwill of others, and
no native people has ever agreed to become a minority by persuasion alone, so security
must come first. Revisionists hold that only an "iron wall" of Jewish military strength,
which the Arabs cannot breach, will bring them in time to a settlement. The goal is a
Jewish majority and state in the whole Land of Israel, with a liberal, parliamentary order
and a national economy free of class struggle, in which every citizen's basic needs are
met and the young are trained in discipline, dignity and self-defence.

**Key concepts.**

- **The iron wall:** an agreement becomes possible only once the other side accepts that
  force cannot defeat the Jewish national home.
- **Monism (_had-nes_, "one banner"):** the national goal comes before any social or class
  ideology.
- **Hadar:** dignity, honour and bearing, the ethic of the Betar youth movement.
- **The whole Land of Israel:** historic Israel, on both banks of the Jordan in
  Jabotinsky's original programme, as the Jewish homeland.
- **National arbitration:** compulsory arbitration of labour disputes instead of strikes
  and class struggle.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Agreement will come only after an iron wall makes it clear we cannot be defeated."
- "One banner, not two: the national flag before the red one."
- "In the beginning God created the individual."
- "A people that cannot defend itself will not be given a state."
- "The Land of Israel belongs to the Jewish people, whole."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Labour Zionism:** socialist economics and class organisation (_Economic coordination_,
  _Workplace governance_).
- **Religious Zionism:** grounds the claim to the whole Land in religious redemption
  (_Transcendence_, _Religion and state_).
- **Zionism:** the parent (Thin), to which Revisionism adds military primacy and a
  non-socialist economy (_Use of force_, _Economic coordination_).
- **National conservatism:** overlaps in Israel's current right, but as a general doctrine
  for any nation-state (centered group; Reasons).

**Sources.** Ze'ev Jabotinsky, "The Iron Wall" and "The Ethics of the Iron Wall" (1923)
and _The Idea of Betar_ (1934); Menachem Begin, _The Revolt_ (1951); Yaacov Shavit,
_Jabotinsky and the Revisionist Movement, 1925–1948_ (1988); Colin Shindler, _The
Triumph of Military Zionism_ (2006); Hillel Halkin, _Jabotinsky: A Life_ (2014).

## Religious Zionism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Zionism
- **Also called:** national-religious (_dati leumi_); Mizrachi (its founding movement);
  Kookism (Rabbi Kook's school)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** the Jewish people

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                              | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Transcendence                       | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_                                             | Yes     |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Public role for religion_ – Leans _Public role for religion_             | Yes     |
| Basis of nationhood                 | Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                                             | Yes     |
| Source of moral authority           | Strongly _Given from beyond us_                                                    | No      |
| Individuals or wholes               | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_   | No      |
| Direction of history                | Strongly _History is progressing_ – Leans _History is progressing_                 | No      |
| Legal moralism                      | Leans _Enforcement of shared morality_ – Strongly _Enforcement of shared morality_ | No      |
| Self-authorship or given role       | Leans _Live up to a given role_ – Strongly _Live up to a given role_               | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_     | No      |
| Use of force                        | Strongly _Readiness to use force_ – Leans _Readiness to use force_                 | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance   | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_           | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The return of the Jewish people to the Land of Israel is not only a
political event but the beginning of the redemption the prophets foretold. Settling,
building and defending the Land is itself a commandment, and even secular pioneers were
instruments of a divine process without knowing it. Religious Zionists join full Torah
observance to full part in national life, the army and the state, and hold that the
Jewish state should be shaped by Jewish law and tradition, with the whole Land of Israel
as the inheritance of the Jewish people.

**Key concepts.**

- **The beginning of redemption (_atchalta di-geula_):** the state as a stage in a
  messianic process unfolding in history.
- **Settling the Land:** a religious commandment, weighed by some as equal to all the
  others.
- **Torah and state:** the state's public life (Sabbath, marriage, education) shaped by
  Jewish law.
- **Holiness of the secular:** secular national work as raw material for holiness
  (Rabbi Kook).
- **The wholeness of the Land:** the Land of Israel as a divine inheritance not to be
  given up.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The State of Israel is the first flowering of our redemption."
- "Settling the Land of Israel is a commandment like any other."
- "The Torah, the people and the Land are one."
- "The secular pioneers were doing God's work without knowing it."
- "No government may give away part of the Land God promised."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Zionism:** the parent (Thin), which Religious Zionism grounds in faith and
  redemption (_Transcendence_; Reasons).
- **Labour Zionism:** a secular, socialist national project (_Transcendence_, _Religion
  and state_).
- **Revisionist Zionism:** shares the claim to the whole Land on national and security
  grounds, not religious ones (_Transcendence_; Reasons).
- **Christian nationalism:** the same shape, centering a nation's Christian people
  (centered group).
- **Hindutva:** the same shape, centering Hindus (centered group).

**Sources.** Abraham Isaac Kook, _Orot_ (1920); Arthur Hertzberg (ed.), _The Zionist
Idea_ (1959), for Kook, Reines and the Mizrachi; Zvi Yehuda Kook, _Le-netivot Yisrael_
(1967); Ehud Sprinzak, _The Ascendance of Israel's Radical Right_ (1991); Dov Schwartz,
_Religious-Zionism: History and Ideology_ (2009).

## Hindutva

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Hindu nationalism; the Sangh Parivar (the family of organisations
  around the RSS); critics: "Hindu majoritarianism"
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** Hindus: in Savarkar's definition, all who regard India as both
  fatherland and holy land, including Sikhs, Jains and Buddhists

### Positions

| Dimension                                   | Range                                                                                      | Central |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Basis of nationhood                         | Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                                                     | Yes     |
| Group difference                            | Leans _Difference-blind common rules_ – Strongly _Difference-blind common rules_           | Yes     |
| Religion and state                          | Strongly _Public role for religion_ – Leans _Public role for religion_                     | No      |
| Tradition or reason                         | Strongly _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ – Leans _Inherited practice carries wisdom_   | No      |
| Sacred or tradeable                         | Strongly _Some things are beyond price_ – Leans _Some things are beyond price_             | No      |
| Individuals or wholes                       | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_           | No      |
| Historical injustice                        | Leans _Rectification_ – Strongly _Rectification_                                           | No      |
| Citizenship: rights vs. duties              | Strongly _Citizenship as shared obligation_ – Leans _Citizenship as shared obligation_     | No      |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_ – Leans _Special obligations to compatriots_ | No      |
| Immigration and borders                     | Strongly _Community control over membership_ – Leans _Community control over membership_   | No      |
| Use of force                                | Strongly _Readiness to use force_ – Leans _Readiness to use force_                         | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance           | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                   | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                         | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                    | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** India is not a mere territory or a recent state but an ancient
civilisational nation, and its national identity is Hindu in the cultural sense, not the
narrowly religious one: the shared heritage of everyone who regards this land as both
fatherland and holy land. For centuries that civilisation was conquered and humiliated,
and after independence a "pseudo-secular" elite went on appeasing minorities while
treating the majority's culture as suspect. Hindutva seeks a confident, united and strong
Hindu nation: one law for every citizen, the restoration of sacred sites, and a people
organised through discipline and service to rebuild its greatness.

**Key concepts.**

- **Hindutva ("Hinduness"):** a civilisational and cultural identity, wider than the
  Hindu religion (Savarkar).
- **Hindu Rashtra:** India as a Hindu nation, whatever the formal constitution says.
- **Fatherland and holy land (_pitribhumi_, _punyabhumi_):** the test of full national
  belonging.
- **Pseudo-secularism:** a state secularism that, in this view, privileges minorities
  over the majority.
- **Integral humanism:** the BJP's official doctrine of an organic, dharma-based society
  (Upadhyaya).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Hindutva is not a religion; it is the identity of a civilisation."
- "One nation, one law: a uniform civil code for everyone."
- "Real secularism means appeasing no community."
- "Bharat has been a Hindu nation for thousands of years."
- "A nation must remember the temples it lost."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Ethnic nationalism:** a generic Thin Label for nations of shared descent; Hindutva has
  its own canon and centered group (centered group; Reasons).
- **Christian nationalism:** the same shape, centering a nation's Christian people
  (centered group).
- **Religious Zionism:** the same shape, centering the Jewish people (centered group).
- **Gandhism:** Hindu-inspired, but a composite nationalism of all faiths with
  nonviolence at its core (_Basis of nationhood_, _Force in domestic politics_).
- **Ambedkarism:** seeks to annihilate caste and opposed a Hindu Rashtra (_Social
  hierarchy_; Reasons).

**Sources.** V. D. Savarkar, _Essentials of Hindutva_ (1923); M. S. Golwalkar, _Bunch of
Thoughts_ (1966); Deendayal Upadhyaya, _Integral Humanism_ (1965); Walter Andersen and
Shridhar Damle, _The Brotherhood in Saffron_ (1987); Christophe Jaffrelot, _The Hindu
Nationalist Movement and Indian Politics_ (1996) and (ed.) _Hindu Nationalism: A Reader_
(2007).

## Black nationalism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Black Power; Black separatism (its separatist form); Garveyism (its
  first mass form)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** people of African descent, chiefly in the diaspora

### Positions

| Dimension                                   | Range                                                                                                          | Central |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Basis of nationhood                         | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                                   | Yes     |
| Group difference                            | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_                         | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes                       | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                               | No      |
| Civil society vs. the state                 | Strongly _Family, community, and voluntary association_ – Leans _Family, community, and voluntary association_ | No      |
| Local vs. central                           | Strongly _Local and regional self-rule_ – Leans _Local and regional self-rule_                                 | No      |
| Historical injustice                        | Leans _Rectification_ – Strongly _Rectification_                                                               | No      |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_ – Leans _Special obligations to compatriots_                     | No      |
| Force in domestic politics                  | Strongly _Permissible force against injustice_ – Middle                                                        | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** People of African descent form a nation, bound by a shared history
of enslavement, oppression and resistance and by a culture they made in exile.
Integration into institutions built by others has not delivered freedom or respect, so
Black people must define themselves, control their own communities, economies and
institutions, and decide their own future, for many in a homeland of their own.
Self-respect and self-reliance come first: a people that does not build for itself will
always depend on others.

**Key concepts.**

- **Self-determination:** Black people deciding their own political, economic and
  cultural future, from community control to a separate homeland.
- **Black Power:** political and economic power in Black hands, built through Black-led
  organisations.
- **Self-reliance:** Black-owned businesses and institutions, "doing for self" rather
  than depending on others.
- **Separation and integration:** integration seen as assimilation into a society built
  on exclusion; separation as the basis of dignity.
- **Black consciousness:** pride in African heritage and a self-definition not imposed by
  others.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Up, you mighty race, you can accomplish what you will!"
- "Black people must define ourselves and control our own communities."
- "Integration asks us to join a house built on our exclusion."
- "A people needs its own economy: buy Black, build Black."
- "Freedom by any means necessary."

**Telling it apart.**

- **White nationalism:** the same shape, centering people of European descent (centered
  group).
- **Pan-Africanism:** a Thin Label for the unity of Africa and its diaspora against
  imperialism; Black nationalism centres self-determination for diaspora communities
  through their own institutions (Reasons).
- **Critical race theory:** shares the analysis of structural racism, but mostly seeks
  remedies through law and integration (_Group difference_; Reasons).
- **Identity-conscious progressivism:** seeks justice within shared, integrated
  institutions (_Basis of nationhood_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Martin Delany, _The Condition, Elevation, Emigration, and Destiny of the
Colored People of the United States_ (1852); Marcus Garvey, _Philosophy and Opinions of
Marcus Garvey_ (1923–25); Elijah Muhammad, _Message to the Blackman in America_ (1965);
Malcolm X, "The Ballot or the Bullet" (1964); Stokely Carmichael and Charles V. Hamilton,
_Black Power: The Politics of Liberation_ (1967); Wilson Jeremiah Moses, _Classical Black
Nationalism_ (1996); Tommie Shelby, _We Who Are Dark_ (2005).

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The Jews are a people, not only a religion, with a continuous
history and a bond to the Land of Israel that survived two thousand years of exile.
Centuries of persecution, culminating in the Holocaust, showed that a people without a
state of its own lives at the mercy of others' tolerance. Zionism holds that the Jewish
people, like any nation, has the right to self-determination in its historic homeland,
and that a Jewish state is its refuge and the place where Jewish national life can
flourish.

**Key concepts.**

- **Jewish peoplehood:** the Jews as a nation, whatever their religious practice.
- **Self-determination:** a people's right to a state of its own.
- **Return:** the ingathering of the exiles; every Jew's right to immigrate (the Law of
  Return).
- **Normalisation:** becoming a nation like others, with land, labour and a state.
- **A Jewish and democratic state:** a Jewish majority and character joined to equal
  citizenship.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The Jews are a nation, and every nation has a right to self-determination."
- "Never again: a people without a state has no protector."
- "If you will it, it is no dream."
- "Every Jew has the right to come home."
- "A Jewish and democratic state."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Labour Zionism:** an Ideology built on it, adding socialist economics and the ethic
  of labour (_Economic coordination_, _Work_).
- **Revisionist Zionism:** an Ideology built on it, adding military primacy and a
  non-socialist economy (_Use of force_, _Economic coordination_).
- **Religious Zionism:** an Ideology built on it, grounding the return in religious
  redemption (_Transcendence_, _Religion and state_).
- **Ethnic nationalism:** the generic Thin Label for nations of shared descent; Zionism
  names the Jewish people and its homeland (centered group).
- **Liberal nationalism:** liberal Zionists can match both, as the host Ideology and the
  centered group allow (Reasons).
- **Pan-Arabism:** the same Thin shape, centering Arabs (centered group).

(1897); Basel Program (1897); Arthur Hertzberg (ed.), _The Zionist Idea_ (1959); Walter
Laqueur, _A History of Zionism_ (1972); Shlomo Avineri, _The Making of Modern Zionism_
(1981).

## Pan-Arabism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Nationalism
- **Also called:** Arab nationalism (_al-qawmiyya al-'arabiyya_)
- **Variants:** Ba'athism, Nasserism
- **Centered group:** Arabs, the Arabic-speaking peoples from the Atlantic to the Gulf

### Positions

| Dimension                                   | Range                                                                                      | Central |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Basis of nationhood                         | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_               | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes                       | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_           | No      |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_ – Leans _Special obligations to compatriots_ | No      |
| Religion and state                          | Middle – Leans _Secular public order_                                                      | No      |
| Economic coordination                       | Strongly _Public direction_ – Middle                                                       | No      |
| Foreign-policy aims                         | Leans _National interest_ – Strongly _National interest_                                   | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The Arabs, from the Atlantic to the Gulf, are one nation, united by
language, history and culture, divided into separate states by colonial powers and kept
weak by that division. Pan-Arabists hold that the Arab nation should achieve unity,
independence from foreign domination, and a renaissance of its civilisation, overcoming
sectarian and tribal divisions in a shared Arab identity open to Muslims and Christians
alike.

**Key concepts.**

- **The Arab nation:** defined by language, history and culture, not by religion or by
  the borders of existing states.
- **Unity (_wahda_):** political union of the Arab states as the nation's natural form.
- **Colonial borders:** the existing state system as an imposed partition of one nation.
- **Renaissance (_nahda_, _ba'th_):** the revival of Arab civilisation after centuries of
  decline and foreign rule.
- **Independence:** freedom from foreign domination and from great-power blocs.

**Signature reasons.**

- "One Arab nation with an eternal mission."
- "Whoever speaks Arabic and shares our history is an Arab."
- "These borders were drawn by colonial powers, not by Arabs."
- "Divided, the Arabs are weak; united, they are a great power."
- "Unity, freedom, socialism." (its Ba'athist form)

**Telling it apart.**

- **Islamism:** unites Muslims as an umma under sharia; Pan-Arabism unites Arabs as a
  nation, often on secular grounds (_Religion and state_; centered group).
- **Pan-Africanism:** the same Thin shape for Africa and its diaspora (centered group).
- **Zionism:** the same Thin shape, centering the Jewish people (centered group).
- **Non-alignment:** the foreign-policy stance Nasserism helped found, which needs no
  national centre (Reasons).

**Sources.** George Antonius, _The Arab Awakening_ (1938); Constantin Zurayk, _The
Meaning of the Disaster_ (1948); Gamal Abdel Nasser, _The Philosophy of the Revolution_
(1954); Michel Aflaq, _Fi sabil al-ba'th_ (1959); Sati' al-Husri, _Ma hiya al-qawmiyya?_
(1959); Youssef Choueiri, _Arab Nationalism: A History_ (2000); Adeed Dawisha, _Arab
Nationalism in the Twentieth Century_ (2003).
