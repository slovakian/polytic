# Marxism and communism: Label cards

Cards for this family, written to the template in `../label-library.md`, in the order
of the list. Every card here is written with the same dispassion as any other: the
self-description is the tradition's own self-understanding, steelmanned, and never
Polytic's verdict (`../principles.md`).

## Marxism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Socialism
- **Also called:** scientific socialism; orthodox Marxism (its Second International
  form, Kautsky)
- **Variants:** Western Marxism / critical theory, Historical materialism
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |

| Tempo of change                             | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_ – Leans _Fundamental transformation_             | Yes     |
| Direction of history                        | Strongly _History is progressing_ – Leans _History is progressing_                     | No      |
| Workplace governance                        | Strongly _Worker voice and self-management_ – Leans _Worker voice and self-management_ | No      |
| Distribution                                | Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_ – Strongly _Egalitarian redistribution_             | No      |
| Channels of change                          | Middle – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                                                | No      |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Leans _Impartial concern for all people_ – Strongly _Impartial concern for all people_ | No      |
| Religion and state                          | Leans _Secular public order_ – Strongly _Secular public order_                         | No      |
| Zero-sum or positive-sum                    | Strongly _One's gain is another's loss_ – Middle                                       | No      |

| Transcendence                               | Leans _Nature is all there is_ – Strongly _Nature is all there is_                     | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** History is the history of class struggles. How a society produces
its means of life shapes its laws, politics, and ideas, and each economic order is
driven by conflict between those who own the means of production and those who work
them. Capitalism has unleashed enormous productive power, but it rests on the
exploitation of wage labour and moves through crises it cannot resolve. The working
class, which has nothing to lose but its chains, can abolish class society itself and
build communism, an association of free and equal producers.

**Key concepts.**

- **Class:** a relation to the means of production (owning them or having to sell one's
  labour), not a matter of income, status, or culture.
- **Exploitation:** the surplus value workers produce beyond their wage, taken by
  capital as profit through the normal working of the system, not by fraud or abuse.
- **History:** a material, dialectical process in which productive forces outgrow
  relations of production, moving toward communism.
- **The state:** an instrument of class rule that, once classes are abolished, withers
  away.
- **Freedom:** emancipation from alienated labour and from the compulsion of the market:
  the "realm of freedom" beyond necessity.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggles."
- "Capitalism exploits workers through its normal working, not only through its abuses."
- "Social being determines consciousness: ideas follow material conditions."
- "From each according to ability, to each according to need."
- "Workers of the world, unite!"

**Telling it apart.**

- **Social democracy:** reforms capitalism within its institutions; Marxism expects and
  works for capitalism's supersession (_Tempo of change_, _Property in capital and
  goods_).
- **Democratic socialism:** can share the goal of social ownership without Marxism's
  theory of history and class struggle (_Direction of history_; Reasons).
- **Marxism–Leninism:** adds the vanguard party, democratic centralism, and the
  one-party state (_Pluralism vs. unity_, _Leadership_).
- **Anarcho-communism:** shares the communist goal, but rejects any transitional state
  and the materialist theory of history (_Political authority_; Reasons).
- **Council communism:** the branch that places power in workers' councils rather than
  a party (_Representation vs. participation_).

**Sources.** Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels, _The Communist Manifesto_ (1848); Marx,
Preface to _A Contribution to the Critique of Political Economy_ (1859), _Capital_, vol. 1
(1867), _Critique of the Gotha Programme_ (1875); Engels, _Socialism: Utopian and
Scientific_ (1880); Karl Kautsky, _The Class Struggle_ (1892); G. A. Cohen, _Karl Marx's
Theory of History_ (1978); Leszek Kołakowski, _Main Currents of Marxism_ (1978).

## Marxism–Leninism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism
- **Also called:** Leninism; communism (in its parties' own usage)
- **Variants:** Yugoslav self-management socialism
- **Centered group:** —

| Channels of change             | Leans _Beyond institutions_ – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                                 | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise | Strongly _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_                     | No      |
| Leadership                     | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_                 | No      |
| Security and civil liberties   | Leans _Collective security and order_ – Strongly _Collective security and order_             | No      |
| Speech and expression          | Leans _Protection from harmful expression_ – Strongly _Protection from harmful expression_   | No      |
| Tempo of change                | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_                                                        | No      |
| Design vs. evolved order       | Leans _Deliberate design_ – Strongly _Deliberate design_                                     | No      |
| Religion and state             | Strongly _Secular public order_                                                              | No      |
| Direction of history           | Strongly _History is progressing_ – Leans _History is progressing_                           | No      |
| Transcendence                  | Strongly _Nature is all there is_                                                            | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Capitalism will not be voted away. The capitalist class controls
the state, the press, and the economy, and it will use force to defend its rule.
Workers therefore need a disciplined vanguard party, armed with Marxist theory and
organised by democratic centralism, to lead them in taking power, establishing the
dictatorship of the proletariat, and building socialism through public ownership and
central planning. Adherents point out that this road turned agrarian empires into
industrial powers and brought universal literacy, health care, and employment.

**Key concepts.**

- **The party:** the vanguard of the working class, its most conscious and organised
  section, which brings revolutionary theory to the movement.
- **Democratic centralism:** free discussion inside the party, then binding unity in
  action once a decision is made.
- **Democracy:** "bourgeois democracy" is the rule of capital behind electoral forms;
  proletarian democracy is the rule of the working majority.
- **Dictatorship of the proletariat:** the working class's rule over its former
  exploiters during the transition to communism.
- **Imperialism:** the monopoly stage of capitalism, which divides the world among the
  great powers and makes national liberation part of the revolution.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Without revolutionary theory there can be no revolutionary movement."
- "Bourgeois democracy is the dictatorship of capital with elections attached."
- "Freedom of discussion, unity of action."
- "Imperialism is the highest stage of capitalism."
- "The socialist states guaranteed everyone work, housing, and education."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Marxism:** the Second International's "orthodox" Marxism expected a parliamentary,
  mass-party road; Leninism adds the vanguard party and revolutionary seizure of power
  (_Pluralism vs. unity_, _Channels of change_).
- **Stalinism:** the branch with a cult of the leader and mass repression of internal
  enemies (_Leadership_, _Security and civil liberties_, _Force in domestic politics_).
- **Council communism:** puts power in workers' councils, not a party (_Representation
  vs. participation_, _Popular judgment vs. expertise_).
- **Democratic socialism:** pursues social ownership through multiparty democracy
  (_Pluralism vs. unity_, _Channels of change_).
- **Social democracy:** its historical rival, committed to parliamentary reform
  (_Channels of change_, _Tempo of change_).

**Sources.** V. I. Lenin, _What Is to Be Done?_ (1902), _Imperialism, the Highest Stage
of Capitalism_ (1917), _The State and Revolution_ (1917); J. V. Stalin, _Foundations of
Leninism_ (1924); Alfred G. Meyer, _Leninism_ (1957); Archie Brown, _The Rise and Fall of
Communism_ (2009); Leszek Kołakowski, _Main Currents of Marxism_, vol. 2 (1978).

## Stalinism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism–Leninism
- **Also called:** Marxism–Leninism and anti-revisionism (its adherents' usual
  self-names); Hoxhaism (its Albanian form); "Stalinism" itself began as a Trotskyist and
  Western critics' term, though some adherents now use it
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                     | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Leadership                    | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_                                                           | Yes     |
| Security and civil liberties  | Strongly _Collective security and order_                                                         | Yes     |
| Force in domestic politics    | Strongly _Permissible force against injustice_                                                   | Yes     |
| Economic coordination         | Strongly _Public direction_                                                                      | Yes     |
| Pluralism vs. unity           | Strongly _Unified common purpose_                                                                | Yes     |
| Property in capital and goods | Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_                                                        | No      |
| Speech and expression         | Strongly _Protection from harmful expression_                                                    | No      |
| Rule of law vs. discretion    | Strongly _Judgment for the case at hand_ – Leans _Judgment for the case at hand_                 | No      |
| Growth                        | Leans _Growth and abundance_ – Strongly _Growth and abundance_                                   | No      |
| Environmental protection      | Strongly _Prosperity and development first_ – Leans _Prosperity and development first_           | No      |
| Trade and economic openness   | Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_ – Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_ | No      |
| Use of force                  | Strongly _Readiness to use force_ – Leans _Readiness to use force_                               | No      |
| Design vs. evolved order      | Strongly _Deliberate design_                                                                     | No      |
| Tempo of change               | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_                                                            | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** After the revolution, the Soviet Union stood alone, encircled by
hostile capitalist powers and threatened from within by saboteurs and the remnants of
the old exploiting classes. Socialism had to be built in one country, and fast:
collectivised agriculture and planned heavy industry turned a peasant land into the
industrial power that defeated Nazi Germany. That took iron discipline, strong
leadership, and constant vigilance, because the class struggle sharpens rather than
softens as socialism advances.

**Key concepts.**

- **Socialism in one country:** socialism can and must be built in a single state
  without waiting for world revolution.
- **Class struggle:** intensifies under socialism, as defeated classes and foreign powers
  work through internal enemies.
- **Vigilance:** the party and state must root out wreckers, spies, and deviations, or
  the revolution will be lost.
- **Industrialisation:** rapid, planned heavy industry as a matter of survival, not
  only of prosperity.
- **Revisionism:** the post-1956 Soviet turn away from Stalin, which anti-revisionists
  hold opened the road back to capitalism.

**Signature reasons.**

- "We are fifty or a hundred years behind the advanced countries. We must make good the
  distance in ten years, or they will crush us."
- "The Soviet Union defeated fascism because it industrialised in time."
- "As socialism advances, the class enemy fights harder."
- "Khrushchev's secret speech was revisionism that opened the road back to capitalism."
- "Strong leadership is what kept the revolution alive."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Marxism–Leninism:** shares the party-state and planning; Stalinism adds the cult of
  the leader, mass repression, and the doctrine that class struggle intensifies under
  socialism (_Leadership_, _Security and civil liberties_, _Force in domestic politics_).
- **Trotskyism:** opposes Stalinism as bureaucratic degeneration and rejects socialism in
  one country (_Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality_, _Representation vs.
  participation_; Reasons).
- **Maoism:** kept Stalin's legacy, but turned mass mobilisation against the party's own
  bureaucracy (_Popular judgment vs. expertise_, _Social hierarchy_).
- **Juche:** shares the leader-centred party-state, but puts national self-reliance in
  place of Marxist class analysis (_Trade and economic openness_; Reasons).
- **National Socialism:** also a leader-led one-party state, but on a basis of descent
  rather than class, with private property preserved (_Basis of nationhood_, _Property
  in capital and goods_; Reasons).

**Sources.** J. V. Stalin, _Foundations of Leninism_ (1924), "The Tasks of Business
Executives" (1931), _Dialectical and Historical Materialism_ (1938); _History of the
Communist Party of the Soviet Union (Bolsheviks): Short Course_ (1938); Enver Hoxha,
_Imperialism and the Revolution_ (1978); Robert C. Tucker (ed.), _Stalinism: Essays in
Historical Interpretation_ (1977); Domenico Losurdo, _Stalin: History and Critique of a
Black Legend_ (2008); Stephen Kotkin, _Stalin_, vols. 1–2 (2014, 2017).

## Trotskyism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism–Leninism
- **Also called:** Bolshevik-Leninism (its adherents' early self-name); the Fourth
  International tradition
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                                   | Range                                                                                        | Central |
| ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Tempo of change                             | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_                                                        | Yes     |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Leans _Impartial concern for all people_ – Strongly _Impartial concern for all people_       | Yes     |
| Economic coordination                       | Strongly _Public direction_ – Leans _Public direction_                                       | Yes     |
| Property in capital and goods               | Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_                                                    | No      |
| Workplace governance                        | Strongly _Worker voice and self-management_                                                  | No      |
| Representation vs. participation            | Strongly _Direct and participatory self-rule_ – Leans _Direct and participatory self-rule_   | No      |
| Channels of change                          | Middle – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                                                      | No      |
| Force in domestic politics                  | Strongly _Permissible force against injustice_ – Leans _Permissible force against injustice_ | No      |
| Direction of history                        | Strongly _History is progressing_ – Leans _History is progressing_                           | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The October Revolution was genuine, but isolated in a backward
country it was strangled by a privileged bureaucracy, which Stalin came to embody.
Socialism cannot be built in one country, because capitalism is a world system. The
revolution has to be permanent and international, spreading until it reaches the
advanced economies, and workers' states must be ruled by workers' democracy through
their councils, not by a caste of officials. Until then revolutionaries organise in a
world party, fighting for transitional demands that lead from today's struggles toward
power.

**Key concepts.**

- **Permanent revolution:** in late-developing countries the working class must lead the
  revolution beyond democratic tasks to socialism, and the revolution must spread
  internationally to survive.
- **Internationalism:** the world working class, not the nation, is the unit of
  revolutionary politics.
- **Bureaucracy:** the USSR became a "degenerated workers' state", whose nationalised
  property was worth defending even as its ruling caste was to be overthrown.
- **Transitional demands:** demands that start from present needs but cannot be met
  within capitalism, leading workers toward taking power.
- **Workers' democracy:** soviet democracy, with the freedom of soviet parties that
  the bureaucracy destroyed.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Socialism in one country is a contradiction in terms."
- "The Soviet Union was a workers' state deformed by bureaucracy, not socialism."
- "The crisis of humanity is the crisis of revolutionary leadership."
- "A revolution that doesn't spread across borders will die."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Stalinism:** its antagonist: socialism in one country, bureaucratic rule, and the
  leader cult (_Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality_, _Leadership_,
  _Representation vs. participation_).
- **Marxism–Leninism:** Trotskyism keeps the vanguard party and Lenin's strategy;
  what sets it apart is in Reasons (permanent revolution and the critique of the
  bureaucracy).
- **Council communism:** rejects the vanguard party Trotskyists keep (_Popular judgment
  vs. expertise_; Reasons).
- **Maoism:** builds the revolution on the peasantry and protracted war, in stages;
  Trotskyism on the industrial working class and permanent revolution (Reasons).
- **Democratic socialism:** works through elections and gradual change (_Channels of
  change_, _Tempo of change_).

**Sources.** Leon Trotsky, _Results and Prospects_ (1906), _The Permanent Revolution_
(1930), _The Revolution Betrayed_ (1936), _The Death Agony of Capitalism and the Tasks
of the Fourth International_ ("The Transitional Program", 1938); Isaac Deutscher, _The
Prophet Armed_, _The Prophet Unarmed_, _The Prophet Outcast_ (1954–63); Ernest Mandel,
_Trotsky as Alternative_ (1992; trans. 1995); Robert J. Alexander, _International
Trotskyism, 1929–1985_ (1991).

## Maoism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism–Leninism
- **Also called:** Mao Zedong Thought (its Chinese official name);
  Marxism–Leninism–Maoism (its later international form)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                      | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Tempo of change                | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_                                                            | Yes     |
| Force in domestic politics     | Strongly _Permissible force against injustice_                                                   | Yes     |
| Pluralism vs. unity            | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_                               | Yes     |
| Channels of change             | Leans _Beyond institutions_ – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                                     | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise | Middle – Strongly _Equal popular judgment_                                                       | No      |
| Social hierarchy               | Strongly _Equality of status_ – Leans _Equality of status_                                       | No      |
| Economic coordination          | Strongly _Public direction_                                                                      | No      |
| Property in capital and goods  | Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_                                                        | No      |
| Land and natural resources     | Strongly _Common inheritance_ – Leans _Common inheritance_                                       | No      |
| Trade and economic openness    | Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_ – Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_ | No      |
| Leadership                     | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_                     | No      |
| Human nature                   | Leans _Malleable and improvable_ – Strongly _Malleable and improvable_                           | No      |
| Tradition or reason            | Leans _Reason should audit inheritance_ – Strongly _Reason should audit inheritance_             | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** In semi-colonial, peasant societies the revolution's main force is
the peasantry, encircling the cities from the countryside in a protracted people's war.
The party must follow the mass line, "from the masses, to the masses", learning from the
people and returning their ideas to them in concentrated form. Winning power is not the
end. New bourgeois elites grow inside the party and the state itself, so the revolution
has to continue under socialism, with the masses mobilised to criticise and overthrow
those taking the capitalist road.

**Key concepts.**

- **Mass line:** leadership that gathers the scattered ideas of the masses, systematises
  them, and takes them back to the masses to test in practice.
- **People's war:** protracted, rural-based armed struggle that builds base areas and
  encircles the cities.
- **Contradiction:** every situation contains contradictions; politics means
  identifying the principal one and handling "contradictions among the people"
  differently from those with the enemy.
- **Continuing revolution:** class struggle goes on under socialism, against a new
  bourgeoisie inside the party.
- **Self-reliance:** relying on one's own forces, in war and in development.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Political power grows out of the barrel of a gun."
- "It is right to rebel."
- "From the masses, to the masses."
- "The countryside will encircle the cities."
- "Even after the revolution, a new bourgeoisie grows inside the party."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Marxism–Leninism:** Maoism rests the revolution on the peasantry and continues class
  struggle against the party's own elites (_Popular judgment vs. expertise_, _Social
  hierarchy_; Reasons).
- **Socialism with Chinese characteristics:** puts development, markets, and stability
  ahead of class struggle (_Economic coordination_, _Tempo of change_).
- **Stalinism:** Maoism honours Stalin, but mobilises the masses against bureaucracy
  rather than relying on the security apparatus (_Popular judgment vs. expertise_).
- **Trotskyism:** centres the industrial working class and permanent revolution, where
  Maoism centres the peasantry and revolution by stages (Reasons).
- **Fanonist anti-colonial revolution:** shares rural, anti-colonial armed struggle, but
  frames it by the colonial relation rather than by Marxist–Leninist class analysis
  (Reasons).

**Sources.** Mao Zedong, "Report on an Investigation of the Peasant Movement in Hunan"
(1927), "On Contradiction" (1937), "On Protracted War" (1938), "On New Democracy" (1940),
"Some Questions Concerning Methods of Leadership" (1943), _Quotations from Chairman Mao
Tse-tung_ (1964); Stuart Schram, _The Thought of Mao Tse-tung_ (1989); Maurice Meisner,
_Mao's China and After_ (3rd ed., 1999); Julia Lovell, _Maoism: A Global History_ (2019).

## Socialism with Chinese characteristics

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism–Leninism
- **Also called:** Deng Xiaoping Theory; Xi Jinping Thought on Socialism with Chinese
  Characteristics for a New Era (its current official form); critics: "Dengism", "state
  capitalism"
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                                      | Central |
| --------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Pluralism vs. unity               | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_                         | Yes     |
| Economic coordination             | Leans _Public direction_ – Leans _Market coordination_                                     | Yes     |
| Growth                            | Leans _Growth and abundance_ – Strongly _Growth and abundance_                             | Yes     |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise    | Strongly _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_                   | No      |
| Leadership                        | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_ – Leans _Strong, decisive leadership_               | No      |
| Security and civil liberties      | Leans _Collective security and order_ – Strongly _Collective security and order_           | No      |
| Speech and expression             | Leans _Protection from harmful expression_ – Strongly _Protection from harmful expression_ | No      |
| Property in capital and goods     | Middle – Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_                                            | No      |
| Land and natural resources        | Strongly _Common inheritance_ – Leans _Common inheritance_                                 | No      |
| Trade and economic openness       | Leans _Open exchange_ – Middle                                                             | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                   | No      |
| Foreign-policy aims               | Leans _National interest_ – Strongly _National interest_                                   | No      |
| Tempo of change                   | Leans _Incremental reform_ – Strongly _Incremental reform_                                 | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** China must build socialism from where it actually stands: the
primary stage of socialism, in a once-poor, populous country. Seek truth from facts:
what proves itself in practice is what serves the people, and developing the productive
forces comes first. Markets, foreign investment, and private enterprise are tools that
socialism can use, as long as the Communist Party leads, the commanding heights stay
public, and stability holds. This path has lifted hundreds of millions out of poverty
and is restoring the Chinese nation to its rightful place.

**Key concepts.**

- **Primary stage of socialism:** a long period in which developing the productive
  forces takes priority, and in which markets and private ownership have a role.
- **Seek truth from facts:** practice, not dogma, is the test of policy; reform proceeds
  by experiment ("crossing the river by feeling for the stones").
- **Socialist market economy:** markets allocate most goods, with public ownership
  dominant and the party directing strategy.
- **Party leadership:** the Communist Party's leadership is the defining feature of the
  system and the guarantee of its socialist direction.
- **Stability and common prosperity:** order is the precondition of development, and
  growth must eventually be shared.

**Signature reasons.**

- "It doesn't matter whether a cat is black or white, so long as it catches mice."
- "Poverty is not socialism."
- "Development is the hard truth."
- "Without stability, nothing can be achieved."
- "The Party leads everything."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Marxism–Leninism:** classical Marxism–Leninism plans the economy centrally; this
  tradition uses markets and opening as means (_Economic coordination_, _Growth_; Reasons:
  the primary stage).
- **Maoism:** put class struggle and mass campaigns first; this tradition puts
  development and stability first (_Tempo of change_, _Popular judgment vs. expertise_).
- **Market socialism:** also pairs markets with public ownership, but within multiparty
  democracy and often with worker ownership (_Pluralism vs. unity_, _Workplace
  governance_).
- **Developmental state:** a non-communist, usually capitalist model of
  bureaucrat-guided growth (_Property in capital and goods_; Reasons).
- **Asian values:** grounds order and development in Confucian tradition, not in
  Marxism (_Tradition or reason_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Deng Xiaoping, "Build Socialism with Chinese Characteristics" (1984) and
the Southern Tour talks (1992), in _Selected Works of Deng Xiaoping_, vol. 3 (1993);
Constitution of the Communist Party of China; Xi Jinping, _The Governance of China_
(2014–); Barry Naughton, _The Chinese Economy_ (2nd ed., 2018); Daniel A. Bell, _The
China Model_ (2015).

## Juche

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism–Leninism
- **Also called:** the Juche idea; Kimilsungism–Kimjongilism (its official name since 2012)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                        | Central |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Trade and economic openness       | Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_                             | Yes     |
| Leadership                        | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_                                       | Yes     |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                                         | Yes     |
| Pluralism vs. unity               | Strongly _Unified common purpose_                                            | Yes     |
| Economic coordination             | Strongly _Public direction_                                                  | No      |
| Property in capital and goods     | Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_                                    | No      |
| Security and civil liberties      | Strongly _Collective security and order_                                     | No      |
| Speech and expression             | Strongly _Protection from harmful expression_                                | No      |
| Use of force                      | Strongly _Readiness to use force_                                            | No      |
| Basis of nationhood               | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_ | No      |
| Individuals or wholes             | Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                                     | No      |
| Self-interest or duty to others   | Leans _Owing yourself to others_ – Strongly _Owing yourself to others_       | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A people must be master of its own destiny. Korea, long dominated
by larger powers, can be free only through self-reliance: independence in politics,
self-sufficiency in the economy, and self-defence in national security, applying
Marxism–Leninism to Korean conditions instead of copying any foreign model. The masses
are the masters of history but need correct guidance, so the nation lives as one
socio-political body, united around the leader and the party, and in a dangerous world
it puts the army first.

**Key concepts.**

- **Juche:** being the subject, not the object, of one's own history; "man is the master
  of everything and decides everything".
- **Self-reliance:** independence (chaju) in politics, self-sufficiency (charip) in the
  economy, and self-defence (chawi) in national security.
- **The leader (suryong):** the brain of the socio-political organism, through whom the
  masses become a conscious force.
- **Socio-political organism:** leader, party, and masses as one living body.
- **Songun:** military-first politics; the army as the backbone of independence.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Man is the master of everything and decides everything."
- "Self-reliance in politics, economy, and defence, or we become someone's colony again."
- "Don't copy any foreign model. Apply the idea to our own conditions."
- "The leader, the party, and the masses are one living body."
- "A strong army is what keeps the nation independent."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Marxism–Leninism:** Juche demotes material conditions and class in favour of
  national self-reliance and the leader's guidance (_Trade and economic openness_,
  _Sovereignty vs. pooled governance_; Reasons).
- **Stalinism:** shares the leader-centred party-state; Juche puts national independence
  where Stalinism puts class struggle (_Basis of nationhood_; Reasons).
- **Maoism:** shares self-reliance, but trusts the mass line and campaigns against the
  party's own elites, where Juche concentrates guidance in the leader (_Popular judgment
  vs. expertise_, _Social hierarchy_).
- **Ethnic nationalism:** some scholars read Juche as chiefly ethnic nationalism; its own
  self-description is socialist, with public ownership and planning (_Property in capital
  and goods_, _Economic coordination_).

**Sources.** Kim Il-sung, "On Eliminating Dogmatism and Formalism and Establishing Juche
in Ideological Work" (1955); Kim Jong-il, _On the Juche Idea_ (1982); Dae-Sook Suh, _Kim
Il Sung: The North Korean Leader_ (1988); Charles K. Armstrong, _The North Korean
Revolution, 1945–1950_ (2003); Bruce Cumings, _North Korea: Another Country_ (2004);
B. R. Myers, _The Cleanest Race_ (2010) and _North Korea's Juche Myth_ (2015).

## Council communism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism
- **Also called:** left communism (its wider family, with the Italian current); the
  Dutch-German communist left; Luxemburgism (a near neighbour)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                        | Range                                                              | Central |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Workplace governance             | Strongly _Worker voice and self-management_                        | Yes     |
| Representation vs. participation | Strongly _Direct and participatory self-rule_                      | Yes     |
| Channels of change               | Leans _Beyond institutions_ – Strongly _Beyond institutions_       | Yes     |
| Property in capital and goods    | Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_                          | No      |
| Economic coordination            | Strongly _Public direction_ – Leans _Public direction_             | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise   | Leans _Equal popular judgment_ – Strongly _Equal popular judgment_ | No      |
| Leadership                       | Strongly _Dispersed, checked authority_                            | No      |
| Political authority              | Middle – Leans _Voluntary order_                                   | No      |
| Tempo of change                  | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_                              | No      |
| Direction of history             | Strongly _History is progressing_ – Leans _History is progressing_ | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The emancipation of the working class must be the work of the
working class itself. Parliaments and trade unions belong to capitalism and tie workers
to it, and a vanguard party that takes power in workers' name becomes a new ruling
class, as Russia showed. Power belongs in workers' councils: assemblies in every
workplace, with delegates mandated and recallable at any moment, federated to run
production and society directly.

**Key concepts.**

- **Workers' councils:** assemblies of workers at the point of production, the organs of
  both revolution and the new society.
- **Self-emancipation:** only workers' own mass action can create socialism; no party
  can do it for them.
- **The party:** at most a tendency that argues within the class, never its ruler.
- **State capitalism:** the Soviet system replaced private owners with a party
  bureaucracy, not with workers' power.
- **Anti-parliamentarism:** electoral politics and union bargaining integrate workers
  into capitalism.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The emancipation of the working class must be the work of the working class itself."
- "The Soviet Union was state capitalism, not socialism."
- "All power to the workers' councils, not to any party."
- "Delegates should be mandated and recallable at any moment."
- "Unions and parliaments bind workers to capitalism."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Marxism–Leninism:** puts power in the vanguard party; council communism puts it in
  the councils (_Representation vs. participation_, _Popular judgment vs. expertise_).
- **Anarcho-syndicalism:** also wants workers to run production, but through permanent
  revolutionary unions and without Marxism's theory of history (Reasons).
- **Libertarian socialism:** the wider family; council communism is its Marxist wing,
  holding to historical materialism and class rule in the transition (Reasons).
- **Autonomism:** shares hostility to parties and unions, but aims at the refusal of work
  rather than workers' management of it (_Work_).
- **Trotskyism:** keeps the vanguard party (_Popular judgment vs. expertise_).

**Sources.** Rosa Luxemburg, _The Russian Revolution_ (1918); Herman Gorter, _Open Letter
to Comrade Lenin_ (1920); Otto Rühle, _The Revolution Is Not a Party Affair_ (1920);
Anton Pannekoek, _Lenin as Philosopher_ (1938) and _Workers' Councils_ (1946); Paul
Mattick, _Anti-Bolshevik Communism_ (1978); Philippe Bourrinet, _The Dutch and German
Communist Left (1900–68)_ (2017).

## Autonomism (operaismo)

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Marxism
- **Also called:** workerism (operaismo, its 1960s Italian form); Autonomia (its 1970s
  movement); post-operaismo (its later theory)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                        | Range                                                                              | Central |
| -------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Work                             | Leans _Work is a means to living_ – Strongly _Work is a means to living_           | Yes     |
| Channels of change               | Leans _Beyond institutions_ – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                       | Yes     |
| Reciprocity in support           | Strongly _Unconditional support_ – Leans _Unconditional support_                   | No      |
| Representation vs. participation | Strongly _Direct and participatory self-rule_                                      | No      |
| Political authority              | Leans _Voluntary order_ – Strongly _Voluntary order_                               | No      |
| Property in capital and goods    | Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_ – Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_ | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise   | Leans _Equal popular judgment_ – Strongly _Equal popular judgment_                 | No      |
| Tempo of change                  | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_ – Leans _Fundamental transformation_         | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Workers are not passive victims of capital. Their struggles come
first, and capital's restructurings are responses to them. Change comes from workers'
own self-activity, in the factory and across the whole of social life, not from parties
and unions that negotiate the terms of their exploitation. The goal is not dignity in
work but freedom from it: refusing work, demanding income as a right rather than a wage,
and taking back time and the wealth that society produces in common.

**Key concepts.**

- **Autonomy:** the working class's independence from capital, and from the parties and
  unions that claim to represent it.
- **Refusal of work:** the struggle against wage labour itself, not for better terms
  within it.
- **Class composition:** how workers' technical position and political organisation
  shape what struggles are possible.
- **Social factory:** capital organises all of social life, including unpaid domestic
  work, not only the workplace.
- **The common:** wealth produced in common by social cooperation, which capital
  captures (post-operaismo).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Workers' struggle comes first; capital only reacts."
- "We don't want a fairer job. We want less work."
- "The whole of society has become a factory."
- "Income should be a right, not a reward for having a job."
- "Don't wait for the party. Act for yourselves."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Council communism:** wants workers to manage production through councils; autonomism
  wants to escape work (_Work_).
- **Anarcho-syndicalism:** builds unions to self-manage work; autonomism treats unions as
  part of capital's management of labour (_Work_, _Workplace governance_; Reasons).
- **Insurrectionary anarchism:** shares informal, direct action outside institutions,
  without autonomism's Marxist class analysis (Reasons).
- **Left accelerationism:** also aims at a post-work society, but through automation and
  political strategy, including the state (_Technology and risk_; Reasons).
- **Marxism–Leninism:** the party-led politics autonomism defines itself against
  (_Popular judgment vs. expertise_, _Representation vs. participation_).

**Sources.** Raniero Panzieri, "The Capitalist Use of Machinery" (1961); Mario Tronti,
_Operai e capitale_ (1966; trans. _Workers and Capital_, 2019); Mariarosa Dalla Costa &
Selma James, _The Power of Women and the Subversion of the Community_ (1972); Antonio
Negri, _Marx beyond Marx_ (1979); Michael Hardt & Antonio Negri, _Empire_ (2000); Steve
Wright, _Storming Heaven_ (2002); Kathi Weeks, _The Problem with Work_ (2011).
