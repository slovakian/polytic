# Non-Western and classical traditions: Label cards

Cards for this family, written to the template in `../label-library.md`, in the order
of the list. Every card here is written with the same dispassion as any other: the
self-description is the tradition's own self-understanding, steelmanned, and never
Polytic's verdict (`../principles.md`). These traditions grew up outside the European
vocabulary the Dimensions mostly come from, so their Reasons carry more of the weight:
where a concept has no Dimension (the Mandate of Heaven, musyawarah, satyagraha), it is
named in Key concepts rather than forced onto an axis.

## Political Confucianism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Confucian constitutionalism (Jiang Qing); Confucian perfectionism
  (Chan); Confucian political meritocracy (Bell); Ruism (_rujia_)
- **Variants:** Confucian meritocracy
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                      | Range                                                                                                          | Central |
| ------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Social hierarchy               | Leans _Ordered rank and deference_ – Strongly _Ordered rank and deference_                                     | Yes     |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise | Strongly _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_                                       | Yes     |
| Self-authorship or given role  | Leans _Live up to a given role_ – Strongly _Live up to a given role_                                           | Yes     |
| Moral circle                   | Strongly _Near and dear first_ – Leans _Near and dear first_                                                   | No      |
| Rule of law vs. discretion     | Strongly _Judgment for the case at hand_ – Middle                                                              | No      |
| Neutrality vs. perfectionism   | Leans _State perfectionism_ – Strongly _State perfectionism_                                                   | No      |
| Civil society vs. the state    | Strongly _Family, community, and voluntary association_ – Leans _Family, community, and voluntary association_ | No      |
| Tradition or reason            | Strongly _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ – Leans _Inherited practice carries wisdom_                       | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity            | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                                        | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A good society is held together less by laws and punishments
than by people who have learned, through ritual, education and the family, to treat one
another rightly in their relationships. Those relationships are not between
interchangeable individuals: parent and child, elder and younger, ruler and subject
owe each other different things, and harmony comes from each honouring their role.
Government exists for the people's welfare, and it should be led by the most
cultivated and capable, chosen by merit, whose legitimacy rests on serving the people
well, on continuity with tradition, and on a higher moral order together.

**Key concepts.**

- **Ren (humaneness):** the virtue of caring for others, learned first in the family and
  extended outward by degrees.
- **Li (ritual propriety):** the forms of conduct that cultivate virtue and order
  relationships, better than law at producing willing harmony.
- **Graded love:** obligations are strongest to family and diminish with distance, and
  rightly so (Mencius against Mozi).
- **Meritocracy:** rule by the virtuous and capable, selected by examination and record,
  rather than by birth or popular vote alone.
- **Mandate of Heaven (tianming):** rulers are legitimate only while they govern for the
  people's good; a ruler who oppresses the people loses it.
- **Threefold legitimacy:** in Jiang Qing's version, the way of heaven, the way of the
  earth (cultural continuity) and the way of the people, each with its own chamber.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Lead them with virtue and order them with ritual, and they will have a sense of shame
  and correct themselves." (after the _Analects_)
- "Good government starts with good families."
- "The people are the most important; the ruler is the least." (Mencius)
- "Leaders should be chosen for wisdom and virtue, not for winning a popularity
  contest."
- "Harmony, not sameness."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Legalism:** the classical rival: order through clear laws, rewards and punishments,
  not virtue and ritual (_Rule of law vs. discretion_; _Punishment_).
- **Asian values:** a 1990s pan-Asian political discourse, drawing on Confucianism among
  other traditions, about development and the limits of universal rights; Political
  Confucianism is the philosophical tradition and its contemporary revival (Reasons;
  _Social hierarchy_).
- **Epistocracy:** also weights rule toward the capable, but by measured political
  knowledge, not moral cultivation (Reasons).
- **Communitarianism:** shares the socially embedded self, without Confucian hierarchy of
  roles or meritocratic rule (_Social hierarchy_, a central Dimension).
- **Socialism with Chinese characteristics:** draws on Confucian language, but is a
  Marxist–Leninist party-state (Reasons; _Property in capital and goods_).
- **Role ethics:** the Foundation Label for living up to given roles, which this
  tradition carries as a central Dimension.

**Sources.** Confucius, _Analects_; _Mencius_; _Xunzi_; Dong Zhongshu, _Luxuriant Gems of
the Spring and Autumn_ (attributed); Huang Zongxi, _Waiting for the Dawn_ (1663); Jiang
Qing, _A Confucian Constitutional Order_ (2013); Joseph Chan, _Confucian Perfectionism_
(2014); Daniel A. Bell, _The China Model_ (2015); Tongdong Bai, _Against Political
Equality_ (2019); Roger T. Ames, _Confucian Role Ethics_ (2011).

## Legalism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** _fajia_ ("the school of standards" or "of law", a Han-dynasty
  bibliographers' category, not a self-name); Chinese Legalism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                      | Range                                                                                | Central |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Rule of law vs. discretion     | Strongly _Rule by general, fixed rules_                                              | Yes     |
| Punishment                     | Strongly _Desert and deterrence_                                                     | Yes     |
| Leadership                     | Strongly _Strong, decisive leadership_                                               | Yes     |
| Human nature                   | Strongly _Fixed and flawed_ – Leans _Fixed and flawed_                               | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity            | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_                   | No      |
| Tradition or reason            | Leans _Reason should audit inheritance_ – Strongly _Reason should audit inheritance_ | No      |
| Citizenship: rights vs. duties | Strongly _Citizenship as shared obligation_                                          | No      |
| Security and civil liberties   | Leans _Collective security and order_ – Strongly _Collective security and order_     | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** People seek benefit and avoid harm; that is how they are, and good
government starts from it rather than from hopes of making them virtuous. A ruler who
waits for sages and relies on persuasion will lose his state. Order comes from clear,
public laws that apply to noble and commoner alike, backed by rewards and punishments so
certain that no one dares to break them, and from a ruler who holds power firmly and
judges officials by results. Times change, so institutions must change with them, not
copy the ancient kings. A rich state and a strong army follow.

**Key concepts.**

- **Fa (standards, law):** clear, publicly promulgated rules applied impartially to all
  below the ruler.
- **The two handles:** reward and punishment, the ruler's instruments for directing
  conduct.
- **Shi (positional power):** authority comes from the ruler's position, not from his
  personal virtue.
- **Shu (techniques):** methods for controlling officials, matching their words to their
  results.
- **Rich state, strong army:** agriculture and war as the foundations of a state's
  survival among rivals.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Make the laws clear and the punishments certain, and no one will dare offend."
- "Don't wait for virtuous people. Build a system that works with ordinary ones."
- "The law does not bend for the noble." (after Han Feizi)
- "Different times call for different measures; don't imitate the ancients."
- "Heavy penalties for light offences mean that there are no offences."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Political Confucianism:** order through virtue, ritual and moral example rather than
  law and punishment (_Rule of law vs. discretion_, a central Dimension; _Punishment_).
- **Realism:** a school of interstate power politics; Legalism is mainly domestic
  statecraft, though both stress power and survival among rival states (Reasons).
- **Classical liberalism:** also prizes general, known rules, but as limits on rulers to
  protect individual liberty; in Legalism the law is the ruler's instrument, and the
  ruler stands above it (_Leadership_; _Security and civil liberties_).

**Sources.** _The Book of Lord Shang_ (_Shangjunshu_; trans. Yuri Pines 2017); _Han
Feizi_ (trans. Burton Watson, _Han Feizi: Basic Writings_, 2003); Herrlee Creel, _Shen
Pu-hai_ (1974); Sima Tan, "On the Essentials of the Six Schools", in Sima Qian, _Records
of the Grand Historian_, ch. 130; Benjamin Schwartz, _The World of Thought in Ancient
China_ (1985); Yuri Pines, "Legalism in Chinese Philosophy", _Stanford Encyclopedia of
Philosophy_.

## Three Principles of the People

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** _Sanmin zhuyi_; Tridemism; Sun Yat-sen thought
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                        | Central |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Land and natural resources        | Strongly _Common inheritance_ – Leans _Common inheritance_                   | Yes     |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_     | Yes     |
| Economic coordination             | Leans _Public direction_ – Middle                                            | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise    | Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Middle                                   | No      |
| Representation vs. participation  | Leans _Direct and participatory self-rule_ – Middle                          | No      |
| Basis of nationhood               | Middle – Leans _Shared heritage and culture_                                 | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity               | Leans _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_ – Leans _Unified common purpose_ | No      |
| Distribution                      | Middle – Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_                                  | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** China was humiliated and carved up because its people were "a sheet
of loose sand", without national unity, popular power or economic security. Three
principles answer this: nationalism, so the nation stands free of foreign domination;
democracy, so the people hold sovereignty; and the people's livelihood, so land rights
are equalised and private capital is regulated before great inequality can take root.
A people long ruled by emperors must pass through stages, military unification and then
political tutelage, before full constitutional government, in which the people hold
power and capable officials exercise it.

**Key concepts.**

- **Minzu (nationalism):** national independence and the equality of China's peoples,
  against imperialism and unequal treaties.
- **Minquan (people's rights):** the people's four powers of election, recall,
  initiative and referendum.
- **Minsheng (people's livelihood):** equalisation of land rights, taxing the
  unearned increase in land value, and regulation of capital.
- **Power and ability:** the people hold sovereignty (_quan_), while the government's
  capable officials exercise administration (_neng_).
- **Five-power constitution:** executive, legislative and judicial branches plus
  examination (merit selection) and control (oversight) branches.
- **Three stages:** military government, political tutelage, then constitutional
  government.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The nation belongs to all." (_tianxia wei gong_)
- "Land to the tiller, and the rising value of land to the community."
- "The people hold the power; the able do the governing."
- "A free nation first, then free citizens."
- "Regulate private capital before it becomes a new aristocracy."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Georgism:** Sun adopted Henry George's land-value principle, but within a nationalist,
  developmental and constitutional programme; Georgism has no view on nationhood or
  tutelage (Reasons; _Sovereignty vs. pooled governance_).
- **Socialism with Chinese characteristics:** a Marxist–Leninist party-state; the Three
  Principles explicitly reject class struggle and aim at constitutional democracy
  (Reasons; _Pluralism vs. unity_).
- **Kemalism:** also nation-building and modernisation from above in a post-imperial
  state, but laicist and without the land programme (_Religion and state_; _Land and
  natural resources_).
- **Developmental state:** Taiwan's later growth model, an economic strategy rather than a
  founding political philosophy (Reasons).
- **Political Confucianism:** Sun drew on Confucian virtues and the examination
  tradition, but the Three Principles are a modern republican programme (_Social
  hierarchy_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Sun Yat-sen, _The International Development of China_ (1922), _San Min Chu
I: The Three Principles of the People_ (lectures 1924; trans. Frank W. Price 1927) and
_Fundamentals of National Reconstruction_ (1924); Constitution of the Republic of China
(1947), preamble and art. 1; Marie-Claire Bergère, _Sun Yat-sen_ (1994; trans. 1998);
Audrey Wells, _The Political Thought of Sun Yat-sen_ (2001).

## Asian values

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** the Asian values thesis; the Singapore model; "Shared Values"
  (Singapore's 1991 white paper)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                      | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Security and civil liberties   | Leans _Collective security and order_ – Strongly _Collective security and order_                 | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes          | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                 | Yes     |
| Pluralism vs. unity            | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                          | No      |
| Civil society vs. the state    | Leans _Family, community, and voluntary association_ – Middle                                    | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance   | Strongly _Self-reliance and voluntary provision_ – Leans _Self-reliance and voluntary provision_ | No      |
| Growth                         | Leans _Growth and abundance_ – Strongly _Growth and abundance_                                   | No      |
| Popular judgment vs. expertise | Strongly _Rule by the knowledgeable_ – Leans _Rule by the knowledgeable_                         | No      |
| Punishment                     | Strongly _Desert and deterrence_ – Leans _Desert and deterrence_                                 | No      |
| Foreign-policy aims            | Leans _National interest_ – Strongly _National interest_                                         | No      |
| Rights: natural or man-made    | Middle – Strongly _Rights are created by society_                                                | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Societies in East and Southeast Asia achieved in a generation what
took the West a century, and they did it by putting the community before the
individual: strong families, respect for authority and learning, hard work, thrift and
social harmony. Western-style liberal democracy, with its adversarial politics, welfare
dependence and absolute individual rights, is one historical path, not a universal
standard to be imposed. A people first needs order, stability and development; rights
must be balanced against duties and fitted to each society's culture and stage of
development.

**Key concepts.**

- **Community over self:** the nation and family come before the individual, and
  individual rights are balanced against social duties.
- **Family:** the basic unit of society and of welfare, rather than the state.
- **Consensus:** decisions reached by consultation and harmony, not by confrontation.
- **Development first:** economic growth and social stability are preconditions for
  wider freedoms.
- **Cultural relativity of rights:** human rights are real, but their content and
  priority depend on national culture and history (Bangkok Declaration).
- **Good governance:** honest, competent, meritocratic government judged by results.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The family is the building block of society."
- "Order and stability come before Western-style freedoms."
- "We won't be lectured on human rights by countries that colonised us."
- "Society comes first, the individual second."
- "Good government matters more than the form of government."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Political Confucianism:** the philosophical tradition; Asian values is a 1990s
  political discourse, pan-Asian (Malay and Islamic as well as Confucian), centred on
  development and on the limits of universal rights (Reasons; _Social hierarchy_).
- **Developmental state:** the economic strategy that often accompanied it; Asian values
  is a case about culture, rights and order (Reasons).
- **Communitarianism:** a Western critique of individualism that keeps liberal rights and
  democratic contestation (_Security and civil liberties_, a central Dimension;
  _Pluralism vs. unity_).
- **Liberalism:** treats individual rights as universal and prior (_Rights: natural or
  man-made_; _Security and civil liberties_).
- **Socialism with Chinese characteristics:** a Marxist–Leninist party-state, sometimes
  defended in similar terms (Reasons; _Property in capital and goods_).

**Sources.** Lee Kuan Yew, in Fareed Zakaria, "Culture Is Destiny: A Conversation with Lee
Kuan Yew" (1994), and _From Third World to First_ (2000); Government of Singapore, _Shared
Values_ white paper (1991); Bangkok Declaration (1993); Bilahari Kausikan, "Asia's
Different Standard" (1993); Mahathir Mohamad & Shintaro Ishihara, _The Voice of Asia_
(1995); Kishore Mahbubani, _Can Asians Think?_ (1998); Joanne Bauer & Daniel A. Bell
(eds.), _The East Asian Challenge for Human Rights_ (1999); Amartya Sen, "Human Rights and
Asian Values" (1997) (a critic).

## Pancasila

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** the Five Principles; Pancasila democracy (_Demokrasi Pancasila_, the
  New Order's usage)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                  | Range                                                                                    | Central |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Religion and state         | Leans _Public role for religion_ – Middle                                                | Yes     |
| Majoritarian vs. consensus | Leans _Power-sharing and broad consensus_ – Strongly _Power-sharing and broad consensus_ | Yes     |
| Basis of nationhood        | Strongly _Shared civic principles_ – Middle                                              | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity        | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                  | No      |
| Individuals or wholes      | Middle – Leans _Groups and relations are real_                                           | No      |
| Economic coordination      | Leans _Public direction_ – Middle                                                        | No      |
| Distribution               | Middle – Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_                                              | No      |
| Transcendence              | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_ – Leans _A transcendent order exists_             | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A nation of thousands of islands, hundreds of peoples and many
faiths needs a common foundation that belongs to none of them in particular and to all
of them together. Pancasila is that foundation: belief in the One and Only God, a just
and civilised humanity, the unity of Indonesia, democracy guided by wisdom through
deliberation and representation, and social justice for all. It keeps Indonesia
neither a religious state nor a godless one, and it seeks decisions by consensus in the
spirit of mutual help, so that no community is simply outvoted.

**Key concepts.**

- **Ketuhanan Yang Maha Esa (belief in the One and Only God):** the state honours
  religion and recognises several faiths, but establishes none.
- **Musyawarah-mufakat:** deliberation toward consensus, rather than decision by bare
  majority.
- **Gotong royong (mutual cooperation):** the communal spirit of working together, which
  Sukarno called the essence of the five principles.
- **Bhinneka Tunggal Ika (Unity in Diversity):** one nation made of many peoples, bound
  by shared principles.
- **Family principle (kekeluargaan):** the economy organised as a common endeavour, with
  key sectors under state control (1945 Constitution, art. 33).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Unity in diversity."
- "Indonesia is neither a religious state nor a secular one."
- "We decide by deliberation, not by defeating each other."
- "Every Indonesian, whatever their faith or island, belongs."
- "Social justice for all the people of Indonesia."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Islamism:** would make sharia the law of the state; Pancasila's founders dropped the
  Jakarta Charter's clause obliging Muslims to follow sharia (_Religion and state_, a
  central Dimension).
- **Muslim democracy:** pursues Islamic values through pluralist electoral politics;
  Pancasila is a multi-faith national foundation, not a Muslim party's programme
  (Reasons).
- **French republicanism and Kemalism:** keep religion out of public institutions
  (_Religion and state_).
- **Asian values:** shares consensus and community, but Pancasila is one nation's
  constitutional philosophy, with a monotheist first principle and a social-justice
  economy (Reasons).
- **Ubuntu:** also prizes consensus and communal personhood, from African rather than
  Indonesian sources (Reasons).

**Sources.** Sukarno, "The Birth of Pancasila" (speech of 1 June 1945); Preamble to the
1945 Constitution of the Republic of Indonesia; Michael Morfit, "Pancasila: The
Indonesian State Ideology According to the New Order Government" (1981); Eka Darmaputera,
_Pancasila and the Search for Identity and Modernity in Indonesian Society_ (1988);
Douglas Ramage, _Politics in Indonesia: Democracy, Islam and the Ideology of Tolerance_
(1995); Yudi Latif, _Negara Paripurna_ (2011).

## Gandhism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Gandhian thought; Sarvodaya ("the welfare of all", its post-1947
  movement); satyagraha (its method)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                        | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Force in domestic politics       | Strongly _Principled nonviolence_                                                                | Yes     |
| Local vs. central                | Strongly _Local and regional self-rule_ – Leans _Local and regional self-rule_                   | Yes     |
| Economic scale and concentration | Leans _Dispersed ownership and small scale_ – Strongly _Dispersed ownership and small scale_     | Yes     |
| Channels of change               | Middle – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                                                          | No      |
| Growth                           | Strongly _Sufficiency and limits_ – Leans _Sufficiency and limits_                               | No      |
| Trade and economic openness      | Leans _Economic protection and self-reliance_ – Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_ | No      |
| Property in capital and goods    | Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_ – Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_               | No      |
| Use of force                     | Leans _Reluctance to use force_ – Strongly _Reluctance to use force_                             | No      |
| Political authority              | Middle – Leans _Voluntary order_                                                                 | No      |
| Transcendence                    | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_ – Leans _A transcendent order exists_                     | No      |
| Self-interest or duty to others  | Leans _Owing yourself to others_ – Strongly _Owing yourself to others_                           | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Real freedom, swaraj, is self-rule in both senses: a people
governing itself, and each person governing their own desires. It cannot be won by
violence, because the means shape the ends, and it cannot be built by copying the West's
machine civilisation, which multiplies wants and concentrates power. Truth and love,
pressed through nonviolent resistance that accepts suffering rather than inflicts it,
can move any opponent. The good society is a federation of self-reliant villages
producing for their own needs, where the wealthy hold their riches in trust for all and
every person's welfare counts.

**Key concepts.**

- **Satyagraha ("truth-force"):** nonviolent resistance that seeks to convert the
  opponent through self-suffering, not to defeat them.
- **Ahimsa:** nonviolence in thought, word and deed, as the law of our species.
- **Swaraj:** self-rule, personal as well as national.
- **Swadeshi:** serving and buying from one's immediate neighbourhood; village
  self-reliance, symbolised by homespun khadi.
- **Trusteeship:** the rich hold wealth beyond their needs in trust for society,
  voluntarily, rather than having it seized.
- **Sarvodaya:** the welfare of all, including the last and least.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The means are the ends in the making."
- "An eye for an eye only ends up making the whole world blind." (attributed)
- "The earth provides enough for everyone's need, but not for everyone's greed."
- "India lives in her villages."
- "Recall the face of the poorest person you have seen, and ask whether your next step
  will help them." (Gandhi's talisman)

**Telling it apart.**

- **Christian anarchism:** Tolstoy's Gospel-based rejection of the state, which Gandhi
  admired; Gandhism is rooted in Hindu and Jain ethics, and accepts a minimal,
  decentralised polity (_Political authority_; _Transcendence_ and Reasons).
- **Pacifism:** a Thin Label for refusing war and violence, which can ride on any host;
  Gandhism adds an economics of village self-reliance and a method of mass resistance
  (_Local vs. central_; _Economic scale and concentration_).
- **Ambedkarism:** Ambedkar rejected Gandhi's early defence of _varna_ and his village
  ideal, and sought equality through the constitutional state and modern economy
  (_Social hierarchy_; _Local vs. central_).
- **Engaged Buddhism:** shares nonviolence and right livelihood, from Buddhist sources
  (Reasons).
- **Distributism and Degrowth:** share small scale and sufficiency, from Catholic or
  ecological rather than Gandhian grounds (Reasons).

**Sources.** M. K. Gandhi, _Hind Swaraj_ (1909), _Satyagraha in South Africa_ (1928), _An
Autobiography, or The Story of My Experiments with Truth_ (1927–29) and _Constructive
Programme_ (1941); J. C. Kumarappa, _Economy of Permanence_ (1945); Vinoba Bhave, writings
on Sarvodaya and Bhoodan; Joan Bondurant, _Conquest of Violence_ (1958); Bhikhu Parekh,
_Gandhi's Political Philosophy_ (1989); Anthony Parel (ed.), _Hind Swaraj and Other
Writings_ (1997).

## Ubuntu

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Ubuntu philosophy; _botho_ (Sotho–Tswana); _hunhu_ (Shona); African
  humanism (Kaunda, a close neighbour)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                       | Range                                                                                                          | Central |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Majoritarian vs. consensus      | Leans _Power-sharing and broad consensus_ – Strongly _Power-sharing and broad consensus_                       | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes           | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                               | Yes     |
| Punishment                      | Leans _Rehabilitation and restoration_ – Strongly _Rehabilitation and restoration_                             | No      |
| Self-interest or duty to others | Leans _Owing yourself to others_ – Strongly _Owing yourself to others_                                         | No      |
| Civil society vs. the state     | Strongly _Family, community, and voluntary association_ – Leans _Family, community, and voluntary association_ | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity             | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                                        | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** A person is a person through other persons (_umuntu ngumuntu
ngabantu_). We become fully human in relationships of care, respect and sharing, so
harming another diminishes oneself, and the goal of a community is harmony, not the
victory of one side over another. Politics in this spirit seeks decisions everyone can
live with, through patient discussion until consensus is reached, and answers wrongdoing
by restoring broken relationships rather than only punishing. This is how South Africa
chose truth and reconciliation over vengeance.

**Key concepts.**

- **Personhood:** something achieved in community, through how one treats others, not a
  given of the isolated individual.
- **Harmony:** the central good of social life, sustained by sharing, solidarity and
  identification with others (Metz).
- **Consensus:** decision by discussion until all can agree, as in the traditional
  village assembly (_imbizo_, _lekgotla_).
- **Restorative justice:** repairing relationships between offender, victim and
  community, rather than retribution.
- **Interdependence:** my well-being is bound up with yours, which grounds hospitality
  and generosity.

**Signature reasons.**

- "I am because we are."
- "A person is a person through other persons."
- "My humanity is caught up, inextricably bound up, in yours." (Tutu)
- "We should talk until we agree, not vote until someone loses."
- "Justice should heal, not just punish."

**Telling it apart.**

- **African socialism:** an economic programme of communal land and village
  self-reliance that drew on the same communal ethic; Ubuntu is an ethic and a theory of
  personhood, with no fixed economic programme (_Economic coordination_; Reasons).
- **Communitarianism:** a Western critique of the unencumbered self, with similar
  conclusions from different sources (Reasons; _Majoritarian vs. consensus_).
- **Consociationalism:** shares power among segments by elite bargain; Ubuntu seeks
  consensus as a moral ideal of harmony (Reasons).
- **Pancasila:** also prizes deliberation to consensus and mutual help, from Indonesian
  sources and with a monotheist first principle (_Transcendence_; Reasons).
- **Political Confucianism:** also a relational self, but ordered by graded roles and
  meritocratic hierarchy (_Social hierarchy_).

**Sources.** John Mbiti, _African Religions and Philosophy_ (1969); Kenneth Kaunda, _A
Humanist in Africa_ (1966); Kwasi Wiredu, "Democracy and Consensus in African Traditional
