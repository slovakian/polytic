# Religious political traditions: Label cards

Cards for this family, written to the template in `../label-library.md`, in the order
of the list. A religion is not a Label (`../label-library.md`); these are the political
traditions that grow out of one. Every card here is written with the same dispassion as
any other: the self-description is the tradition's own self-understanding, steelmanned,
and never Polytic's verdict (`../principles.md`).

## Catholic social teaching

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Catholic social doctrine; Catholic social thought (its wider scholarly
  tradition)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                                          | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Transcendence                       | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_                                                                         | Yes     |
| Civil society vs. the state         | Strongly _Family, community, and voluntary association_ – Leans _Family, community, and voluntary association_ | Yes     |
| Property in capital and goods       | Middle – Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_                                                                | Yes     |
| Life and body                       | Strongly _Protection of human life and dignity_                                                                | Yes     |
| Grounds of moral status             | Strongly _Life itself is inviolable_                                                                           | No      |
| Moral objectivity                   | Strongly _Objective moral truth_                                                                               | No      |
| Source of moral authority           | Strongly _Given from beyond us_ – Leans _Given from beyond us_                                                 | No      |
| Individuals or wholes               | Middle – Leans _Groups and relations are real_                                                                 | No      |
| Distribution                        | Middle – Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_                                                                    | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance        | Middle – Leans _Public social insurance_                                                                       | No      |
| Workplace governance                | Leans _Worker voice and self-management_ – Middle                                                              | No      |
| Moral limits of markets             | Strongly _Protected non-market spheres_ – Leans _Protected non-market spheres_                                 | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_                                 | No      |
| Environmental protection            | Middle – Leans _Ecological protection first_                                                                   | No      |
| Punishment                          | Middle – Leans _Rehabilitation and restoration_                                                                | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Every human person bears an inviolable dignity as made in the image
of God, and society exists for persons, not persons for society. The economy and the
state must serve the common good: the conditions in which every person and family can
flourish. Higher bodies should not absorb what families, communities and associations
can do for themselves, but they owe help where those fall short. Private property is
legitimate, yet the goods of the earth are destined for all, and labour comes before
capital.

**Key concepts.**

- **Human dignity:** given by God to every person from conception to natural death, not
  conferred by the state or earned by capacity.
- **Common good:** the sum of social conditions that let people and groups reach their
  own fulfilment, not the greatest good of the greatest number.
- **Subsidiarity:** a higher body should not take over what a lower one (family, parish,
  association, town) can do, but should support it where it cannot.
- **Solidarity:** a firm commitment to the good of all, because all are responsible for
  all.
- **Property:** a natural right with a "social mortgage": the universal destination of
  goods comes first.
- **Preferential option for the poor:** policy is judged first by how it affects the
  poorest and most vulnerable.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The economy exists for the person, not the person for the economy."
- "What a family or a local community can do, a higher body should not take over."
- "Private property carries a social mortgage."
- "A worker is owed a wage that can support a family."
- "Human life is sacred from conception to natural death."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Christian democracy:** the political movement built on this teaching, which accepts
  pluralist party politics, works ecumenically, and often compromises on _Life and body_
  and _Legal moralism_ (Reasons).
- **Catholic integralism:** a branch that wants the state to recognise the Church and
  order law to the spiritual end (_Religion and state_, _Neutrality vs.
  perfectionism_).
- **Liberation theology:** a branch that reads the option for the poor through structural
  analysis and calls for transforming society, not reforming it (_Tempo of change_,
  _Causes of fortune_).
- **Distributism:** a branch whose answer is widely held small property (_Economic scale
  and concentration_).
- **Neo-Calvinism:** the Protestant parallel; its spheres are coordinate and each
  answers directly to God, where subsidiarity ranks bodies as higher and lower (Reasons).
- **Social democracy:** a similar welfare state, built by the state rather than through
  families and intermediate bodies, on secular grounds (_Civil society vs. the state_,
  _Transcendence_).

**Sources.** Leo XIII, _Rerum Novarum_ (1891); Pius XI, _Quadragesimo Anno_ (1931);
Second Vatican Council, _Gaudium et Spes_ (1965); John Paul II, _Laborem Exercens_
(1981) and _Centesimus Annus_ (1991); Benedict XVI, _Caritas in Veritate_ (2009);
Francis, _Laudato si'_ (2015); Pontifical Council for Justice and Peace, _Compendium of
the Social Doctrine of the Church_ (2004).

## Christian democracy

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Catholic social teaching
- **Also called:** Christian personalism in politics; _popolarismo_ (Sturzo's term)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                        | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Civil society vs. the state         | Leans _Family, community, and voluntary association_ – Middle                                | Yes     |
| Welfare and social insurance        | Middle – Leans _Public social insurance_                                                     | Yes     |
| Pluralism vs. unity                 | Strongly _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_ – Leans _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_ | Yes     |
| Economic coordination               | Middle – Leans _Market coordination_                                                         | No      |
| Workplace governance                | Leans _Worker voice and self-management_ – Middle                                            | No      |
| Religion and state                  | Leans _Public role for religion_ – Middle                                                    | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance   | Strongly _Pooled, rule-bound international governance_ – Middle                              | No      |
| Majoritarian vs. consensus          | Leans _Power-sharing and broad consensus_ – Strongly _Power-sharing and broad consensus_     | No      |
| Individuals or wholes               | Middle – Leans _Groups and relations are real_                                               | No      |
| Life and body                       | Middle – Strongly _Protection of human life and dignity_                                     | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Middle                                                | No      |
| Tempo of change                     | Leans _Incremental reform_ – Strongly _Incremental reform_                                   | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Politics should serve the human person, understood not as an
isolated individual but as a being in relation, in family, community and nation, with a
dignity no state confers. Christian democrats bring that Christian image of humanity into
pluralist democracy: a social market economy that joins enterprise with solidarity, a
welfare state that supports families and lets churches, unions and associations do their
work, and a Europe of reconciled nations bound by law. They seek the centre by
conviction, not by calculation.

**Key concepts.**

- **Personalism:** the person is neither the isolated individual of liberalism nor a cell
  of the collective; politics exists to serve persons in community.
- **Subsidiarity:** the state supports families, churches, unions and associations rather
  than replacing them; welfare is often delivered through them.
- **Social market economy:** free enterprise within a framework of social protection and
  partnership between capital and labour.
- **Reconciliation:** between classes at home and nations abroad, the founding purpose of
  postwar European integration.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The person comes before the state, and the family before the market."
- "A market economy needs a social conscience."
- "Europe was built so that our nations would never go to war with each other again."
- "Capital and labour are partners, not enemies."
- "We are a party of Christian values, open to everyone who shares them."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Catholic social teaching:** the parent doctrine; Christian democracy is its
  expression in pluralist, often ecumenical, party politics, and accepts compromises on
  _Life and body_ and _Legal moralism_ that the teaching itself does not (Reasons).
- **Liberal conservatism:** also centre-right and market-based, but with a smaller welfare
  state and without subsidiarity and personalism (_Welfare and social insurance_, _Civil
  society vs. the state_).
- **Social democracy:** a similar welfare state, built through the state on secular,
  egalitarian grounds (_Civil society vs. the state_; Reasons).
- **Ordoliberalism:** shares the social market economy, which Christian democracy
  grounds in personalism and extends with family and welfare policy (_Welfare and social
  insurance_; Reasons).
- **Neo-Calvinism:** the Protestant tradition that joined it in the Netherlands, with
  sphere sovereignty in place of subsidiarity (Reasons).
- **Muslim democracy:** the same shape, religious values in pluralist democracy, drawn
  from Islam (Reasons).

**Sources.** Luigi Sturzo, _Church and State_ (1939); Jacques Maritain, _Integral
Humanism_ (1936), _Christianity and Democracy_ (1943) and _Man and the State_ (1951);
Emmanuel Mounier, _Personalism_ (1949); Michael Fogarty, _Christian Democracy in Western
Europe, 1820–1953_ (1957); Kees van Kersbergen, _Social Capitalism_ (1995); Stathis
Kalyvas, _The Rise of Christian Democracy in Europe_ (1996); Carlo Invernizzi Accetti,
_What Is Christian Democracy?_ (2019).

## Neo-Calvinism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Kuyperianism; Reformational philosophy (Dooyeweerd's school);
  principled pluralism (its American form)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                                          | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Transcendence                       | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_                                                                         | Yes     |
| Civil society vs. the state         | Strongly _Family, community, and voluntary association_ – Leans _Family, community, and voluntary association_ | Yes     |
| Group difference                    | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_                         | No      |
| Religion and state                  | Leans _Public role for religion_ – Middle                                                                      | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                 | Strongly _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_ – Leans _Legitimate pluralism and opposition_                   | No      |
| Political authority                 | Strongly _Legitimate state authority_ – Leans _Legitimate state authority_                                     | No      |
| Source of moral authority           | Strongly _Given from beyond us_                                                                                | No      |
| Grounds for belief                  | Leans _Belief rightly held on other grounds_ – Strongly _Belief rightly held on other grounds_                 | No      |
| Individuals or wholes               | Middle – Leans _Groups and relations are real_                                                                 | No      |
| Life and body                       | Leans _Protection of human life and dignity_ – Strongly _Protection of human life and dignity_                 | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Middle                                                                  | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Christ is Lord over every part of creation, and God has given each
sphere of life (family, church, school, business, science, art and the state) its own
task and its own authority under him. No sphere rules another: the state's calling is
public justice among the spheres, not running them, and the church's calling is not to
govern the state. A society of many faiths should give each community equal room to live
out its convictions in public, including in its own schools, rather than pretend to a
neutrality that is itself a worldview.

**Key concepts.**

- **Sphere sovereignty:** each social sphere has an authority of its own, received from
  God, not delegated by the state or the church.
- **Public justice:** the state's distinct task, to protect the spheres and adjudicate
  between them, not to absorb them.
- **Common grace:** God restrains evil and enables good across humanity, so Christians
  can build a shared public life with others.
- **Antithesis:** every worldview rests on religious commitments, so no public order is
  neutral.
- **Principled pluralism:** equal public standing for every faith community and its
  institutions (confessional pluralism) and for the different spheres (structural
  pluralism).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Not one square inch of life lies outside Christ's lordship."
- "The state's job is public justice, not running the family, the church or the school."
- "Nobody is neutral, so fund every community's schools on equal terms."
- "The church shouldn't run the state, and the state shouldn't run the church."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Catholic social teaching:** subsidiarity ranks bodies as higher and lower, with the
  higher helping the lower; sphere sovereignty makes the spheres coordinate, each
  answerable directly to God (Reasons).
- **Christian democracy:** the party tradition Dutch neo-Calvinists later joined;
  neo-Calvinism is the confessional doctrine beneath it (_Transcendence_; Reasons).
- **Christian reconstructionism (theonomy):** also puts all of life under God's law, but
  wants the state to enforce biblical civil law; neo-Calvinism gives every faith equal
  public standing (_Legal moralism_, _Group difference_).
- **Catholic integralism:** wants the state ordered to the Church; neo-Calvinism denies
  any church jurisdiction over the state (_Religion and state_; Reasons).
- **Multicultural liberalism:** also recognises groups in public life, but grounds it in
  individual autonomy, not in God-given spheres (_Transcendence_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Abraham Kuyper, "Sphere Sovereignty" (1880) and _Lectures on Calvinism_
(1898); Herman Dooyeweerd, _A New Critique of Theoretical Thought_ (1953–58) and _Roots
of Western Culture_ (1959); James Skillen and Rockne McCarthy (eds.), _Political Order
and the Plural Structure of Society_ (1991); Jonathan Chaplin, _Herman Dooyeweerd:
Christian Philosopher of State and Civil Society_ (2011); James Bratt, _Abraham Kuyper:
Modern Calvinist, Christian Democrat_ (2013).

## Catholic integralism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Catholic social teaching
- **Also called:** integralism; the confessional state (its classical form); critics:
  "Catholic theocracy"
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                    | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Public role for religion_                                                      | Yes     |
| Neutrality vs. perfectionism        | Strongly _State perfectionism_                                                           | Yes     |
| Transcendence                       | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_                                                   | Yes     |
| Legal moralism                      | Leans _Enforcement of shared morality_ – Strongly _Enforcement of shared morality_       | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                 | Leans _Unified common purpose_ – Strongly _Unified common purpose_                       | No      |
| Source of moral authority           | Strongly _Given from beyond us_                                                          | No      |
| Moral objectivity                   | Strongly _Objective moral truth_                                                         | No      |
| What makes a life go well           | Strongly _Some things are good for anyone_                                               | No      |
| Life and body                       | Strongly _Protection of human life and dignity_                                          | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_           | No      |
| Individuals or wholes               | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_         | No      |
| Tradition or reason                 | Strongly _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ – Leans _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ | No      |
| Speech and expression               | Middle – Leans _Protection from harmful expression_                                      | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Human beings have two ends, a natural one in this life and a
supernatural one in eternity, and the higher must order the lower. As the soul rules the
body, temporal power should be ordered to the spiritual end the Church serves: the state
should seek the whole common good, recognise the true religion, and shape law and
culture toward virtue, not stand neutral among ways of life. Liberalism's neutrality was
always a pretence, and it has left a people unable to say what is good.

**Key concepts.**

- **The two powers:** temporal and spiritual authority are distinct, but the temporal is
  subordinate to the spiritual in matters touching salvation.
- **Common good:** a real, shared good that includes the eternal good of souls, not an
  aggregate of private preferences.
- **Freedom:** the capacity to choose the good, not an empty right to choose anything.
- **Neutrality:** impossible; every regime promotes some idea of the good.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The state cannot be neutral about what is good for human beings."
- "Temporal power should be ordered to the spiritual, as the body to the soul."
- "Freedom is the power to choose the good, not a right to choose anything."
- "A political order that refuses to name God has already chosen another god."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Catholic social teaching:** the parent; integralism takes the maximal reading of
  Church and state, against the liberal reading of the Council's declaration on religious
  liberty (_Religion and state_, _Neutrality vs. perfectionism_).
- **Post-liberalism:** shares the common-good critique of liberalism without requiring
  the state to recognise the Church (_Religion and state_; Reasons).
- **Counter-revolutionary traditionalism:** also unites throne and altar, but demands
  monarchy and a society of estates; integralism is indifferent to the form of government
  (_Leadership_, _Social hierarchy_).
- **Christian nationalism:** centers a nation's Christian people and heritage, often
  Protestant; integralism centers a universal Church, not a nation (centered group;
  Reasons).
- **Guardianship of the Jurist:** gives clerics direct political rule; integralism keeps
  the two powers distinct, with the Church's authority indirect (_Popular judgment vs.
  expertise_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Leo XIII, _Immortale Dei_ (1885); Pius IX, _Quanta Cura_ (1864); Thomas
Crean and Alan Fimister, _Integralism: A Manual of Political Philosophy_ (2020); Edmund
Waldstein, "Integralism in Three Sentences" (_The Josias_, 2016); Thomas Pink,
"Conscience and Coercion" (_First Things_, 2012); Kevin Vallier, _All the
Kingdoms of the World_ (2023).

## Christian nationalism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Christian America (its US form); critics: "theocracy",
  "dominionism" (also used for its reconstructionist branch)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** the nation's Christian people, in whichever nation the adherent
  belongs to

### Positions

| Dimension                                   | Range                                                                                          | Central |
| ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Religion and state                          | Strongly _Public role for religion_                                                            | Yes     |
| Basis of nationhood                         | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_                   | Yes     |
| Transcendence                               | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_ – Middle                                                | No      |
| Neutrality vs. perfectionism                | Leans _State perfectionism_ – Strongly _State perfectionism_                                   | No      |
| Legal moralism                              | Leans _Enforcement of shared morality_ – Strongly _Enforcement of shared morality_             | No      |
| Life and body                               | Leans _Protection of human life and dignity_ – Strongly _Protection of human life and dignity_ | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life         | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_                 | No      |
| Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality | Strongly _Special obligations to compatriots_ – Leans _Special obligations to compatriots_     | No      |
| Immigration and borders                     | Strongly _Community control over membership_ – Leans _Community control over membership_       | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance           | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                       | No      |
| Pluralism vs. unity                         | Middle – Leans _Unified common purpose_                                                        | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Every nation has a character formed by its history, and a
Christian nation's was formed by Christianity: its law, its moral sense, its calendar and
its idea of human dignity. A nation that forgets the faith that made it loses the ground
of its freedoms. Christian nationalists want their nation to acknowledge that heritage
openly, with laws on life, marriage and family that reflect Christian morality, a public
life free to honour God, and a people confident in handing its faith and culture on.

**Key concepts.**

- **Nation:** a people with a shared history and a Christian inheritance, not only the
  holders of a passport; loving it is a Christian duty.
- **Separation of church and state:** keeps the institutions distinct, but was never
  meant to remove God from public life.
- **Religious freedom:** the freedom of Christians to shape public life by their faith,
  not confinement of faith to private life.
- **Heritage:** law and morality inherited from Christianity, which a secular state lives
  off without renewing.

**Signature reasons.**

- "This nation was built on Christian principles, and it should say so."
- "Separation of church and state was meant to protect the church, not to drive God out
  of public life."
- "Our freedoms come from God, not from government."
- "Every nation's laws reflect someone's morality; ours should reflect the faith that
  made us."
- "Loving your own nation is a Christian duty, not a sin."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Christian reconstructionism (theonomy):** a branch that wants biblical civil law
  enforced by a minimal, decentralised state, and treats nationhood as secondary
  (_Legal moralism_, _Local vs. central_; Reasons).
- **Catholic integralism:** centers a universal Church, not a nation (centered group;
  Reasons).
- **National conservatism:** defends the nation-state with religion as one part of its
  heritage; Christian nationalism makes the nation's Christian identity its core
  (_Religion and state_; centered group).
- **Social conservatism:** shares the moral positions without claiming the nation is
  Christian (_Basis of nationhood_, _Religion and state_).
- **Hindutva:** the same shape, centering Hindus (centered group).
- **Religious Zionism:** the same shape, centering the Jewish people and the Land of
  Israel (centered group).

**Sources.** David Barton, _The Myth of Separation_ (1989); Stephen Wolfe, _The Case for
Christian Nationalism_ (2022); Andrew Torba and Andrew Isker, _Christian Nationalism: A
Biblical Guide for Taking Dominion and Discipling Nations_ (2022); Andrew Whitehead and
Samuel Perry, _Taking America Back for God_ (2020); Philip Gorski and Samuel Perry, _The
Flag and the Cross_ (2022).

## Christian reconstructionism (theonomy)

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Christian nationalism
- **Also called:** theonomy; theonomic postmillennialism; critics: "dominionism",
  "theocracy"
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                          | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Legal moralism                      | Strongly _Enforcement of shared morality_                                      | Yes     |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Public role for religion_                                            | Yes     |
| Source of moral authority           | Strongly _Given from beyond us_                                                | Yes     |
| Transcendence                       | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_                                         | No      |
| Grounds for belief                  | Strongly _Belief rightly held on other grounds_                                | No      |
| Civil society vs. the state         | Strongly _Family, community, and voluntary association_                        | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance        | Strongly _Self-reliance and voluntary provision_                               | No      |
| Local vs. central                   | Strongly _Local and regional self-rule_ – Leans _Local and regional self-rule_ | No      |
| Economic coordination               | Leans _Market coordination_ – Strongly _Market coordination_                   | No      |
| Money and public finance            | Leans _Rules and fiscal discipline_ – Strongly _Rules and fiscal discipline_   | No      |
| Punishment                          | Strongly _Desert and deterrence_                                               | No      |
| Life and body                       | Strongly _Protection of human life and dignity_                                | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Distinct roles for the sexes_                                        | No      |
| Channels of change                  | Strongly _Within institutions_ – Leans _Within institutions_                   | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** God's law, revealed in Scripture, is the only just standard for
every sphere of life, civil government included; there is no neutral, merely human law.
The civil laws of the Old Testament still bind wherever the New Testament has not set
them aside, so a just state would enforce God's law and do nothing beyond it, leaving
welfare, education and most of life to family, church and free enterprise. That order
will come not by force or revolution but as the gospel spreads and generation after
generation is converted and rebuilds society.

**Key concepts.**

- **Theonomy:** "God's law"; the abiding validity of biblical law, including its civil
  penalties, for every society.
- **Dominion mandate:** the command of Genesis 1:28 to bring every area of life under
  God's rule.
- **Postmillennialism:** Christ's kingdom will grow through conversion until it fills the
  earth, before his return.
- **Limited state:** the state's only legitimate task is to punish what God's law calls
  crime; most of government belongs to family and church.
- **No neutrality:** every legal order rests on some religious authority (presuppositional
  apologetics).

**Signature reasons.**

- "It's God's law or man's law; there is no neutral third option."
- "Every civil law in the Bible still binds unless God has repealed it."
- "The state's only job is to punish evil as God defines it; the rest belongs to family
  and church."
- "Christ's kingdom will fill the earth by conversion, not by the sword."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Christian nationalism:** the parent, which centers a nation's Christian heritage;
  reconstructionism wants biblical law in every nation and treats nationhood as
  secondary (_Legal moralism_; Reasons).
- **Neo-Calvinism:** also Reformed, but gives every faith equal public standing and does
  not apply Old Testament civil law (_Legal moralism_, _Group difference_).
- **Catholic integralism:** a Church-guided state reasoning from natural law;
  reconstructionism reasons from Scripture alone and wants a minimal, decentralised
  state (_Local vs. central_; Reasons).
- **Paleolibertarianism:** shares decentralisation, hard money and a minimal welfare
  state, without biblical law (_Legal moralism_, _Religion and state_).
- **Islamism:** the same claim that revealed law should govern society, from the Qur'an
  and Sunna rather than the Bible (Reasons).

**Sources.** R. J. Rushdoony, _The Institutes of Biblical Law_ (1973); Greg Bahnsen,
_Theonomy in Christian Ethics_ (1977); Gary DeMar, _Ruler of the Nations_ (1987); Gary
North, _Political Polytheism_ (1989); Julie Ingersoll, _Building God's Kingdom_ (2015);
Michael McVicar, _Christian Reconstruction: R. J. Rushdoony and American Religious
Conservatism_ (2015).

## Christian socialism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Socialism
- **Also called:** religious socialism; the Social Gospel (its US Protestant form)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                       | Range                                                                                  | Central |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Property in capital and goods   | Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_ – Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_     | Yes     |
| Distribution                    | Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_ – Strongly _Egalitarian redistribution_             | Yes     |
| Transcendence                   | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_ – Leans _A transcendent order exists_           | Yes     |
| Economic coordination           | Strongly _Public direction_ – Middle                                                   | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance    | Leans _Public social insurance_ – Strongly _Public social insurance_                   | No      |
| Workplace governance            | Strongly _Worker voice and self-management_ – Leans _Worker voice and self-management_ | No      |
| Moral limits of markets         | Strongly _Protected non-market spheres_ – Leans _Protected non-market spheres_         | No      |
| Self-interest or duty to others | Leans _Owing yourself to others_ – Strongly _Owing yourself to others_                 | No      |
| Hierarchy or equality           | Leans _Equality is basic_ – Strongly _Equality is basic_                               | No      |
| Source of moral authority       | Strongly _Given from beyond us_ – Middle                                               | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The Gospel announces good news to the poor and a kingdom in which
the last are first, and it cannot be squared with an economy organised around
competition and private gain. The earth belongs to God and its wealth is meant for all,
so the economy should be organised cooperatively, with common ownership, fair work and
care for the weakest. Faith is not only personal salvation but the work of making
society reflect God's justice and love of neighbour.

**Key concepts.**

- **Kingdom of God:** a social reality to be built, in part, within history, not only a
  hope beyond it.
- **Fellowship:** co-operation is the law of Christian life; competition sets brothers
  and sisters against each other.
- **Sin:** social and structural as well as personal; institutions can embody greed.
- **Stewardship:** property is held in trust from God for the common good, not owned
  absolutely.

**Signature reasons.**

- "You cannot serve God and Mammon."
- "The first Christians held all things in common, and that is still the model."
- "Love of neighbour has to be built into how we organise work and wealth."
- "Salvation is social as well as personal."
- "Co-operation, not competition, is the law of Christ."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Socialism:** the parent, with the same economic shape, which Christian socialism
  grounds in the Gospel (_Transcendence_; Reasons).

- **Christian democracy:** a social market economy, not common ownership (_Property in
  capital and goods_, _Economic coordination_).
- **Christian anarchism:** rejects the state as an instrument of the kingdom (_Political
  authority_).
- **Blue Labour (left conservatism):** draws on the same Christian socialist roots, but
  emphasises family, place and reciprocity and is socially conservative (_Sex roles in
  family and public life_, _Reciprocity in support_).

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Islam is not only a private faith but a complete way of life, with
guidance for law, economy, family and government. Sovereignty belongs to God, and a just
society is one whose laws rest on the sharia rather than on imported Western models that
have brought Muslim societies dependency, corruption and moral decay. Islamists work,
through preaching, social service and political action, to restore an Islamic order in
which Muslim peoples govern themselves by their own values.

**Key concepts.**

- **Sharia:** God's law, a body of principles and rules the state should apply, not only
  a guide to private worship.
- **God's sovereignty (_hakimiyya_):** legislation ultimately belongs to God; human
  rulers apply and interpret it.

- "Islam is a complete way of life, not a religion of the mosque alone."

- "The Muslim world is weak because it abandoned its faith, not because of it."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Muslim democracy:** a branch that pursues Islamic values through pluralist elections
  and civil liberties rather than a sharia state (_Pluralism vs. unity_, _Religion and
  state_).
- **Guardianship of the Jurist:** a Shi'i branch that gives supreme authority to a
  qualified jurist; Sunni Islamism has been mostly lay-led (_Popular judgment vs.
  expertise_; Reasons).
- **Quietist Salafism:** a branch that rejects political activism and counsels obedience
  to rulers (_Channels of change_).
- **Salafi-jihadism:** a branch that makes armed jihad against rulers obligatory (_Force
  in domestic politics_).
- **Christian reconstructionism (theonomy):** the same claim that revealed law should
  govern society, from the Bible (Reasons).
- **Pan-Arabism:** unites Arabs as a nation, often on secular grounds; Islamism unites
  Muslims as an umma under sharia (_Religion and state_; centered group).

## Quietist Salafism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Islamism
- **Also called:** purist Salafism (scholars' term); scholarly Salafism (_al-salafiyya

**Self-description.** True Islam is the religion as the Prophet and the first three

knowledge, teaching and personal reform, not through parties, protests or revolts, which
divide the community and bring bloodshed. A Muslim owes obedience to the Muslim ruler in

**Key concepts.**

- **The Salaf:** the first generations of Muslims, whose understanding of the Qur'an and
  Sunna is the standard.
- **Innovation (_bid'a_):** any addition to the religion, including modern political

- **Purification and education (_tasfiya wa tarbiya_):** reform of belief and practice
  must precede any political change.
- **Strife (_fitna_):** rebellion against a Muslim ruler causes more harm than his
  injustice.
- **Partisanship (_hizbiyya_):** political parties and movements, including Islamist
  ones, split the umma.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Follow the Qur'an and the Sunna as the first generations understood them."
- "Purify belief first; politics comes later, if at all."
- "Rebelling against a Muslim ruler brings more harm than his injustice."
- "Parties and demonstrations are innovations that divide the umma."
- "Advise the ruler in private, not on the street."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Islamism:** the parent; quietists share the aim of a sharia-governed order but reject
  political activism as the way to reach it, and reject Islamist movements themselves as
  partisan innovation (_Channels of change_; Reasons).
- **Salafi-jihadism:** the same creed, with the opposite answer on rebellion and force
  (_Force in domestic politics_, _Channels of change_).
- **Muslim democracy:** takes part in elections and accepts party pluralism (_Pluralism
  vs. unity_).
- **Guardianship of the Jurist:** Shi'i and revolutionary, with clerics in power
  (_Popular judgment vs. expertise_; Reasons).

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Rulers who govern by man-made law instead of God's have left Islam,
and the order they uphold, propped up by foreign powers, keeps Muslims humiliated and
divided. Preaching alone has not changed this, and elections only legitimise the
usurpation of God's sovereignty. Salafi-jihadists hold that armed jihad against these
rulers and their foreign backers is an obligation on every capable Muslim, until a
caliphate governing by sharia unites the umma.

**Key concepts.**

- **God's sole right to legislate (_tawhid al-hakimiyya_):** making law is an act of
  worship due to God alone, so democracy is idolatry.
- **Excommunication (_takfir_):** rulers who replace the sharia are judged apostates and
  lose any claim to obedience.
- **Jihad:** an individual duty (_fard 'ayn_) when Muslim lands are occupied or ruled by
  apostates.
- **Loyalty and disavowal (_al-wala' wa-l-bara'_):** loyalty is owed to believers, and
  disavowal to unbelief and its supporters.
- **Caliphate:** the single Islamic polity that should unite the umma under sharia.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Whoever does not rule by what God has revealed, those are the unbelievers." (Qur'an
  5:44, as they read it)
- "Democracy puts the people's law above God's."
- "When Muslim lands are occupied, jihad is a duty on every Muslim."
- "These rulers serve foreign powers, not the umma."
- "Without a caliphate, the umma has no shield."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Quietist Salafism:** the same creed, but forbids rebellion against Muslim rulers
  (_Force in domestic politics_, _Channels of change_).
- **Islamism:** the parent, whose mainstream seeks an Islamic order through preaching,
  social work and elections (_Channels of change_, _Force in domestic politics_).
- **Guardianship of the Jurist:** Shi'i and state-led; Salafi-jihadists are Sunni and
  reject Shi'ism (Reasons).
- **Fanonist anti-colonial revolution:** also legitimises violence against foreign
  domination, on secular and national grounds (_Transcendence_; Reasons).
- **Insurrectionary anarchism:** also direct attack, but against all authority, including
  religious law (_Political authority_, _Religion and state_).

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The Buddha's teaching on suffering and its causes applies not only
to individual minds but to the societies they build: greed, hatred and delusion are
built into economies of endless consumption, into war and into injustice. Engaged
Buddhists bring mindfulness and compassion into action on poverty, war, ecology and
human rights, working without hatred for their opponents. Buddhist economics seeks the
most well-being from the least consumption, through right livelihood, simplicity and
non-harm to all sentient beings.

**Key concepts.**

- **Interbeing:** all things arise together (dependent origination), so harming others or
  the earth harms oneself.
- **Institutional poisons:** greed, hatred and delusion embodied in economic, military
  and media institutions, not only in individuals.
- **Right livelihood:** work that does no harm and develops the worker, from the Noble
  Eightfold Path.
- **Non-harm (_ahimsa_):** toward all sentient beings, in means as well as ends.
- **The middle way:** sufficiency between deprivation and indulgence.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Inner peace and social justice are one practice."
- "Greed, hatred and delusion are built into institutions, not only into people."
- "Aim at the most well-being with the least consumption."
- "You can oppose injustice without hating the people who commit it."
- "We inter-are: what we do to the earth, we do to ourselves."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Gandhism:** shares nonviolence and village-scale economics, grounded in Hindu and
  plural traditions and in a programme of national self-rule (Reasons).
- **Degrowth:** shares sufficiency and limits on secular ecological and social grounds
  (_Transcendence_; Reasons).
- **Green politics:** shares ecology and nonviolence as a secular party programme
  (Reasons).
- **Pacifism:** a Thin Label for refusing war or violence, on any grounds (Reasons).
- **Ambedkarism:** also Buddhist, but centred on annihilating caste through constitutional
  democracy (_Social hierarchy_; Reasons).
