# Critical, liberation, and postcolonial traditions: Label cards

Cards for this family, written to the template in `../label-library.md`, in the order
of the list. Queer liberation and Disability rights are Thin, so their cards list only
the Dimensions their thin core commits them to. Every card here is written with the same
dispassion as any other: the self-description is the tradition's own
self-understanding, steelmanned, and never Polytic's verdict (`../principles.md`).
Where a tradition is defined partly by violent strategy, the card describes it, and
probes stay historical or abstract, never calls to action (`../label-library.md`).

## Postcolonialism

- **Type:** Ideology (usually Area)
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** postcolonial theory; decoloniality or decolonial thought (its Latin
  American strand)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                                                                                    | Central |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Historical injustice              | Leans _Rectification_ – Strongly _Rectification_                                                                                         | Yes     |
| Zero-sum or positive-sum          | Strongly _One's gain is another's loss_ – Middle                                                                                         | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Middle – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                                                                                            | No      |
| Trade and economic openness       | Middle – Strongly _Economic protection and self-reliance_                                                                                | No      |
| Group difference                  | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_                                                   | No      |
| Race and ethnicity                | Leans _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ – Strongly _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ | No      |
| Moral objectivity                 | Middle – Strongly _Human-made morality_                                                                                                  | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Formal empire ended, but colonialism did not simply stop. It lives
on in the global economy, in borders drawn by empires, in which languages and
knowledge count as serious, and in how the West still describes the rest of the world
and the formerly colonised describe themselves. To be free, colonised peoples must
recover their own histories and ways of knowing, and the wealth and power built on
conquest must be named and undone. Claims to universal values often carry a particular
civilisation's interests.

**Key concepts.**

- **Coloniality:** the patterns of power, knowledge, and identity that outlast formal
  colonial rule (Quijano, Mignolo).
- **Orientalism:** the West's way of knowing and representing the East as its inferior
  Other, which served and justified domination (Said).
- **The subaltern:** those excluded from the colonial and national structures that speak
  for them (Spivak).
- **Decolonising the mind:** reclaiming one's own languages, histories, and knowledge
  from colonial ones (Ngũgĩ).
- **Provincialising Europe:** treating European thought as one tradition among many, not
  the universal measure (Chakrabarty).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Colonialism didn't end; it changed form."
- "The wealth of the West was built on the colonies."
- "Who gets to tell the story matters as much as what happened."
- "Universal values have usually meant European ones."
- "Decolonise the curriculum."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Fanonist anti-colonial revolution:** a branch that makes revolutionary struggle,
  violence included, the path to liberation (_Force in domestic politics_, _Channels of
  change_).
- **Anti-imperialism:** a Thin Label opposing imperial domination in foreign affairs;
  postcolonialism adds the critique of culture and knowledge (Reasons).
- **Dependency theory:** an economic doctrine about unequal exchange between core and
  periphery; postcolonialism is broader and cultural (Reasons).
- **Critical race theory:** analyses race in law and institutions, mainly in the United
  States; postcolonialism analyses empire and its legacies globally (Reasons).

**Sources.** Aimé Césaire, _Discourse on Colonialism_ (1950); Edward W. Said,
_Orientalism_ (1978) and _Culture and Imperialism_ (1993); Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o,
_Decolonising the Mind_ (1986); Gayatri Chakravorty Spivak, "Can the Subaltern Speak?"
(1988); Homi K. Bhabha, _The Location of Culture_ (1994); Dipesh Chakrabarty,
_Provincializing Europe_ (2000); Robert J. C. Young, _Postcolonialism: A Very Short
Introduction_ (2003); Walter D. Mignolo, _The Darker Side of Western Modernity_ (2011).

## Fanonist anti-colonial revolution

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Postcolonialism
- **Also called:** Fanonism; revolutionary anti-colonialism; national liberation
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                         | Range                                                                                                                                    | Central |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Force in domestic politics        | Strongly _Permissible force against injustice_ – Leans _Permissible force against injustice_                                             | Yes     |
| Channels of change                | Leans _Beyond institutions_ – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                                                                             | Yes     |
| Tempo of change                   | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_                                                                                                    | Yes     |
| Historical injustice              | Strongly _Rectification_                                                                                                                 | No      |
| Land and natural resources        | Strongly _Common inheritance_ – Leans _Common inheritance_                                                                               | No      |
| Property in capital and goods     | Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_ – Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_                                                       | No      |
| Sovereignty vs. pooled governance | Leans _Sovereign self-government_ – Strongly _Sovereign self-government_                                                                 | No      |
| Representation vs. participation  | Strongly _Direct and participatory self-rule_ – Leans _Direct and participatory self-rule_                                               | No      |
| Race and ethnicity                | Leans _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ – Strongly _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Colonialism is violence. It was imposed by force, it is kept in
place by force, and it reaches inside the colonised, teaching them to see themselves
through the coloniser's eyes. It will not leave because it is asked politely. The
colonised reclaim their humanity and their nation by rising against it, and the
struggle itself, carried by the peasant masses, forges a new people and a new consciousness.
Independence must not simply hand power to a national bourgeoisie that copies the old
masters. It must transform land, economy, and society from the bottom up.

**Key concepts.**

- **Colonial violence:** the colonial order is founded and maintained by force, so its
  overthrow cannot be wholly peaceful (_The Wretched of the Earth_, ch. 1).
- **Psychology of the colonised:** colonialism produces inferiority and alienation in
  the colonised, which struggle overcomes (_Black Skin, White Masks_).
- **The national bourgeoisie:** a local elite that inherits the colonial state and serves
  foreign capital; Fanon's warning against "false decolonisation".
- **Political education:** the masses, not a party elite, must understand and own the
  revolution.
- **New humanism:** the goal is not revenge but a new humanity beyond the colonial
  divide.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Decolonisation is always a violent phenomenon." (Fanon)
- "The colonial order was built by force, and it won't be undone by petitions."
- "Independence that only changes the flag changes nothing."
- "Each generation must discover its mission, fulfil it, or betray it." (Fanon)

**Telling it apart.**

- **Postcolonialism:** the parent; most postcolonial thinkers focus on culture and
  knowledge and do not make armed struggle part of their programme (_Force in domestic
  politics_).
- **Gandhism:** also anti-colonial, but holds to principled nonviolence (_Force in
  domestic politics_, the central Dimension).
- **Maoism:** shares the peasant-based armed struggle, but organises it under a vanguard
  party and Marxist–Leninist doctrine; Fanon distrusted the party elite (_Representation
  vs. participation_, _Leadership_; Reasons).
- **Pan-Africanism:** shares African liberation as an aim but is defined by unity across
  the continent and diaspora, not by a theory of revolution (centered group; Reasons).

**Sources.** Frantz Fanon, _Black Skin, White Masks_ (1952), _A Dying Colonialism_
(1959), and _The Wretched of the Earth_ (1961, with a preface by Jean-Paul Sartre);
Albert Memmi, _The Colonizer and the Colonized_ (1957); Amílcar Cabral, "The Weapon of
Theory" (1966) and _Return to the Source_ (1973); David Macey, _Frantz Fanon: A
Biography_ (2000); Peter Hudis, _Frantz Fanon: Philosopher of the Barricades_ (2015).

## Critical race theory

- **Type:** Ideology (usually Area)
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** CRT; critical race studies
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension             | Range                                                                                  | Central |
| --------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Race and ethnicity    | Strongly _Group differences are largely made by history and society_                   | Yes     |
| Group difference      | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_ | Yes     |
| Causes of fortune     | Leans _Luck and structure_ – Strongly _Luck and structure_                             | No      |
| Historical injustice  | Leans _Rectification_ – Strongly _Rectification_                                       | No      |
| Individuals or wholes | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_       | No      |
| Speech and expression | Middle – Strongly _Protection from harmful expression_                                 | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Racism is not mainly a matter of individual bigots. It is built into
law, institutions, and ordinary practices, and it persists even when the rules are
formally neutral, because they operate on a history of unequal starting points. Race
itself is a social creation, but one with real and lasting effects. Civil-rights gains
have come when they also served the interests of the powerful, and colour-blind rules
can lock in the inequalities they claim to ignore. The stories and experience of people
of colour are evidence the law has long overlooked.

**Key concepts.**

- **Structural racism:** racial inequality reproduced by institutions and laws, with or
  without racist intent.
- **Social construction of race:** race is a category made by law and society, not a
  biological kind, though it shapes lives profoundly.
- **Interest convergence:** racial progress happens when it aligns with the interests of
  the White majority (Bell).
- **Whiteness as property:** law has treated White identity as a valuable, protected
  status (Harris).
- **Counter-storytelling:** the narratives of people of colour challenge accounts that
  present the law as neutral.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Racism is built into the system, not just in people's hearts."
- "Colour-blind rules can keep in place the inequalities that racism created."
- "Race isn't biological, but its effects are real."
- "Civil rights advanced when it suited those in power."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Identity-conscious progressivism:** a broader political programme that draws on CRT,
  but CRT itself is a body of legal scholarship (Reasons).
- **Intersectional feminism:** grew from CRT; it centres women at the crossing of race,
  sex, and class (Reasons).
- **Multicultural liberalism:** grounds group recognition in liberal autonomy and
  cultural membership, not structural racism (Reasons).
- **Black nationalism:** seeks self-determination for people of African descent through
  their own institutions; CRT critiques and reforms existing law (centered group;
  Reasons).

**Sources.** Derrick Bell, "_Brown v. Board of Education_ and the Interest-Convergence
Dilemma" (_Harvard Law Review_, 1980) and _Faces at the Bottom of the Well_ (1992);
Patricia J. Williams, _The Alchemy of Race and Rights_ (1991); Mari J. Matsuda et al.,
_Words That Wound_ (1993); Cheryl I. Harris, "Whiteness as Property" (_Harvard Law
Review_, 1993); Kimberlé Crenshaw et al. (eds.), _Critical Race Theory: The Key Writings
That Formed the Movement_ (1995); Richard Delgado & Jean Stefancic, _Critical Race
Theory: An Introduction_ (2001; 3rd ed. 2017).

## Identity-conscious progressivism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** Progressivism / Critical race theory
- **Also called:** antiracism (Kendi's sense); social justice politics; critics: "woke",
  "the successor ideology", "the identity synthesis"
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                                                                    | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Group difference                    | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_                                                                                              | Yes     |
| Causes of fortune                   | Leans _Luck and structure_ – Strongly _Luck and structure_                                                                               | Yes     |
| Race and ethnicity                  | Leans _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ – Strongly _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ | No      |
| Historical injustice                | Leans _Rectification_ – Strongly _Rectification_                                                                                         | No      |
| Speech and expression               | Middle – Strongly _Protection from harmful expression_                                                                                   | No      |
| Sex and gender identity             | Leans _Law follows gender identity_ – Strongly _Law follows gender identity_                                                             | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_ – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_                                                     | No      |
| Distribution                        | Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_ – Strongly _Egalitarian redistribution_                                                               | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance        | Leans _Public social insurance_ – Strongly _Public social insurance_                                                                     | No      |
| Immigration and borders             | Middle – Strongly _Freedom of movement_                                                                                                  | No      |
| Individuals or wholes               | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_                                                         | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Society is structured by systems of advantage and disadvantage
based on race, gender, sexuality, disability, and other identities, and these
systems interlock. Treating everyone "the same" in a society that is not equal keeps
those disparities in place. Justice means recognising who is marginalised, listening to
their experience, and actively working to dismantle the policies and norms that produce
unequal outcomes, in institutions, in language, and in ourselves. Being neutral in the
face of inequity is not neutral.

**Key concepts.**

- **Antiracism:** actively opposing racist policies and their effects; on this view there
  is no neutral middle ground between racist and antiracist (Kendi).
- **Equity:** fair outcomes, which may require treating groups differently, as distinct
  from formal equality of treatment.
- **Privilege:** unearned advantages that come with belonging to a dominant group,
  often invisible to those who have them.
- **Lived experience:** the testimony of marginalised people is a key source of knowledge
  about injustice.
- **Harm:** words and norms, not only actions, can harm marginalised people and sustain
  their exclusion.

**Signature reasons.**

- "It's not enough to be non-racist. You have to be antiracist."
- "Equality treats everyone the same; equity gives people what they need."
- "Disparities in outcomes are evidence of discrimination in the system."
- "Listen to marginalised voices."
- "Silence is complicity."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Progressivism:** a parent; older progressivism centres class, expertise, and
  economic reform, while this branch centres identity-based oppression (_Group
  difference_; Reasons).
- **Critical race theory:** the academic tradition this draws on; identity-conscious
  progressivism is a wider political and institutional programme spanning many
  identities (Reasons).
- **Social liberalism:** supports equal rights and welfare but prefers difference-blind
  rules and individual opportunity (_Group difference_, the central Dimension).
- **Intersectional feminism:** shares the framework but keeps sex and gender at its
  centre (Reasons).

**Sources.** Iris Marion Young, _Justice and the Politics of Difference_ (1990);
Kimberlé Crenshaw, "Mapping the Margins" (1991); Robin DiAngelo, _White Fragility_
(2018); Ijeoma Oluo, _So You Want to Talk About Race_ (2018); Ibram X. Kendi, _How to Be
an Antiracist_ (2019). Critics' terms: Wesley Yang ("the successor ideology", 2019);
Yascha Mounk, _The Identity Trap_ (2023).

## Indigenous resurgence

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Indigenous sovereignty; Land Back; Red Power (its 1960s–70s North
  American form)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** Indigenous peoples, each a distinct nation with its own land, law,
  and way of life

### Positions

| Dimension                  | Range                                                                                    | Central |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Historical injustice       | Strongly _Rectification_                                                                 | Yes     |
| Group difference           | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_                                              | Yes     |
| Land and natural resources | Strongly _Common inheritance_ – Leans _Common inheritance_                               | No      |
| Local vs. central          | Strongly _Local and regional self-rule_ – Leans _Local and regional self-rule_           | No      |
| Standing of nature         | Leans _Nature-centered_ – Strongly _Nature-centered_                                     | No      |
| Tradition or reason        | Strongly _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ – Leans _Inherited practice carries wisdom_ | No      |
| Individuals or wholes      | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_         | No      |
| Basis of nationhood        | Leans _Shared heritage and culture_ – Strongly _Shared heritage and culture_             | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Indigenous peoples were nations with their own lands, laws, and
governments long before settler states arrived, and they never gave up that sovereignty.
It was taken by force, fraud, and broken treaties. Justice means more than inclusion or
recognition granted by the settler state on its terms. It means returning land,
honouring treaties, and Indigenous nations governing themselves by their own laws,
languages, and relationships with the land, renewed from within their own traditions.

**Key concepts.**

- **Inherent sovereignty:** Indigenous self-government comes from the peoples
  themselves, not from any grant by a settler state.
- **Land:** a web of relationships and responsibilities, not property; its return is the
  core of justice ("Land Back").
- **Resurgence:** renewing Indigenous ways of life, languages, and governance from within,
  rather than seeking the state's recognition (L. B. Simpson, Coulthard).
- **Treaty:** a nation-to-nation agreement whose terms bind the settler state.
- **Refusal:** declining the settler state's terms of recognition and citizenship (A.
  Simpson).

**Signature reasons.**

- "We are nations, not minorities."
- "Land back."
- "Our sovereignty was never ceded."
- "Honour the treaties."
- "We don't need the state's recognition to know who we are."

**Centered group.** Indigenous peoples, each as a distinct nation; a person matches if
they center their own Indigenous nation, or Indigenous peoples generally, in the way the
card describes.

**Telling it apart.**

- **Multicultural liberalism:** offers group rights within a liberal state; resurgence
  rejects the settler state's authority to grant or define them (Reasons).
- **Buen vivir:** shares Indigenous roots, but is a general ecological and communal ethic
  rather than a claim of sovereignty for particular nations (centered group; Reasons).
- **Stateless-nation nationalism:** minority nations seeking autonomy or independence
  within an existing state; Indigenous resurgence grounds its claim in prior,
  never-ceded sovereignty and land relations (_Historical injustice_; Reasons).
- **Postcolonialism:** analyses colonial legacies broadly; Indigenous resurgence focuses
  on settler colonialism and land (centered group).

**Sources.** Vine Deloria Jr., _Custer Died for Your Sins_ (1969); Taiaiake Alfred,
_Wasáse_ (2005); UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples (2007); Glen Sean
Coulthard, _Red Skin, White Masks_ (2014); Audra Simpson, _Mohawk Interruptus_ (2014);
Leanne Betasamosake Simpson, _As We Have Always Done_ (2017).

## Queer liberation

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** gay liberation (its 1969–70s form); queer radicalism;
  anti-assimilationist queer politics
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                        | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_                                   | Yes     |
| Tempo of change                     | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_ – Leans _Fundamental transformation_   | Yes     |
| Legal moralism                      | Strongly _Liberty of self-regarding conduct_                                 | No      |
| Sex and gender identity             | Leans _Law follows gender identity_ – Strongly _Law follows gender identity_ | No      |
| Channels of change                  | Middle – Strongly _Beyond institutions_                                      | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The goal is not for gay, lesbian, bisexual, trans, and queer people
to be let into institutions built around heterosexuality: marriage, the nuclear family,
the military, respectability. It is to be free of the norms that made queerness a
problem in the first place, and to change society so that no one has to fit them.
Inclusion on straight terms leaves the most marginalised queer people (poor, trans, of
colour, sex workers) behind. Liberation means transforming the norms, not joining them.

**Key concepts.**

- **Heteronormativity:** the social order that treats heterosexuality, the gender binary,
  and the nuclear family as natural and normal.
- **Assimilation:** gaining acceptance by conforming to straight norms, which
  liberationists reject.
- **Chosen family:** kinship built by choice and care rather than by blood or marriage.
- **Pride:** visibility and refusal of shame as political acts.

**Signature reasons.**

- "We're here, we're queer, get used to it."
- "Liberation, not assimilation."
- "Marriage was never the goal."
- "The first Pride was a riot."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Social liberalism:** seeks equal rights and inclusion within existing institutions
  (its LGBT equality variant); queer liberation seeks to transform them (_Tempo of
  change_).
- **Queer feminism:** a branch of feminism built on queer theory of gender (_Sex and
  gender_); queer liberation is the movement politics of sexual and gender minorities
  (Reasons).
- **Sex-positive feminism:** shares the defence of sexual freedom, but as part of women's
  liberation (Reasons).

**Sources.** Carl Wittman, "A Gay Manifesto" (1970); Dennis Altman, _Homosexual:
Oppression and Liberation_ (1971); Gay Liberation Front, _Manifesto_ (London, 1971); Guy
Hocquenghem, _Homosexual Desire_ (1972; trans. 1978); Queer Nation, "Queers Read This"
(1990); Michael Warner, _The Trouble with Normal_ (1999); Ryan Conrad (ed.), _Against
Equality_ (2014).

## Ambedkarism

- **Type:** Ideology
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** Ambedkarite politics; Navayana Buddhism (its religious strand)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                                         | Range                                                                                            | Central |
| ------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Social hierarchy                                  | Strongly _Equality of status_                                                                    | Yes     |
| Group difference                                  | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_           | Yes     |
| Hierarchy or equality                             | Strongly _Equality is basic_                                                                     | No      |
| Historical injustice                              | Leans _Rectification_ – Strongly _Rectification_                                                 | No      |
| Popular sovereignty vs. constitutional constraint | Strongly _Entrenched rights and judicial review_ – Leans _Entrenched rights and judicial review_ | No      |
| Channels of change                                | Strongly _Within institutions_ – Leans _Within institutions_                                     | No      |
| Religion and state                                | Leans _Secular public order_ – Strongly _Secular public order_                                   | No      |
| Tradition or reason                               | Leans _Reason should audit inheritance_ – Strongly _Reason should audit inheritance_             | No      |
| Basis of nationhood                               | Strongly _Shared civic principles_ – Leans _Shared civic principles_                             | No      |
| Distribution                                      | Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_ – Strongly _Egalitarian redistribution_                       | No      |
| Economic coordination                             | Strongly _Public direction_ – Leans _Public direction_                                           | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Caste is not a division of labour but a division of labourers: a
graded hierarchy, sanctioned by scripture, that assigns each person a rank by birth and
denies the lowest even basic humanity. It cannot be reformed from within the religion
that upholds it; it must be annihilated. The path is liberty, equality, and fraternity
secured by a democratic constitution, reservations that guarantee the oppressed a share
of power, education, and organisation, and for many, leaving Hinduism for a Buddhism
reinterpreted as a religion of reason and equality.

**Key concepts.**

- **Graded inequality:** caste ranks every group above some and below others, so each
  has a stake in the system and resists its abolition.
- **Annihilation of caste:** caste must be destroyed, not softened, including the
  scriptural authority that sanctions it.
- **Constitutional morality:** loyalty to the constitution's principles and methods,
  which a democracy must cultivate.
- **Reservations:** guaranteed representation in legislatures, education, and public
  employment for Scheduled Castes and Tribes.
- **Educate, agitate, organise:** Ambedkar's charge to the oppressed.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Caste is not a division of labour; it is a division of labourers." (Ambedkar)
- "Educate, agitate, organise."
- "Political democracy cannot last unless it rests on social democracy."
- "Reservations aren't charity; they're a share of power that was denied."
- "I was born a Hindu, but I will not die a Hindu." (Ambedkar)

**Telling it apart.**

- **Gandhism:** sought to reform caste within Hinduism and opposed separate electorates;
  Ambedkarism holds caste must be annihilated (_Social hierarchy_; Reasons).
- **Hindutva:** defines the nation by shared Hindu civilisation; Ambedkarism treats that
  heritage as the source of caste oppression (_Basis of nationhood_, _Religion and
  state_).
- **Marxism:** treats class as the primary division; Ambedkarism holds caste cannot be
  reduced to class (Reasons).
- **Engaged Buddhism:** shares the Buddhist ethics of social justice; Ambedkarism's
  Buddhism is a political rejection of caste (Reasons).

**Sources.** B. R. Ambedkar, _Annihilation of Caste_ (1936), _Who Were the Shudras?_
(1946), _States and Minorities_ (1947), speech to the Constituent Assembly (25 November
1949), and _The Buddha and His Dhamma_ (1957); Gail Omvedt, _Dalits and the Democratic
Revolution_ (1994); Christophe Jaffrelot, _Dr Ambedkar and Untouchability_ (2005).

## Disability rights

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** the disability rights movement; the independent living movement;
  disability justice (its intersectional strand)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                    | Range                                                                                  | Central |
| ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Group difference             | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_ | Yes     |
| Paternalism                  | Leans _Choice_ – Strongly _Choice_                                                     | Yes     |
| Welfare and social insurance | Leans _Public social insurance_ – Strongly _Public social insurance_                   | No      |
| Causes of fortune            | Leans _Luck and structure_ – Strongly _Luck and structure_                             | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** People are disabled less by their bodies or minds than by a society
built without them in mind: stairs, inaccessible information, discrimination at work, and
institutions that decide for them. Remove those barriers, and much of what is called
disability disappears. Disabled people are the experts on their own lives and must
control the decisions that affect them, live in the community rather than in
institutions, and have the support they need as a right, not as charity.

**Key concepts.**

- **Social model:** impairment is a feature of a body or mind; disability is the
  exclusion society builds around it (UPIAS, Oliver).
- **Nothing about us without us:** disabled people must represent themselves and lead
  the decisions that concern them.
- **Independent living:** choice and control over one's own life and support, in the
  community.
- **Reasonable accommodation:** adjustments that give disabled people equal access, owed
  as a matter of rights.
- **Ableism:** discrimination and prejudice that treats non-disabled bodies and minds as
  the norm.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Nothing about us without us."
- "It's the stairs that disable me, not my wheelchair."
- "Access is a right, not a favour."
- "Disabled people are experts on our own lives."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Social liberalism:** supports welfare and anti-discrimination law, but often with a
  medical, provision-centred view of disability; disability rights insists on
  self-determination and the social model (_Paternalism_; Reasons).
- **Identity-conscious progressivism:** includes disability among interlocking identities;
  disability rights is a single-movement Thin Label that rides on many hosts, left and
  right (Reasons).

**Sources.** Union of the Physically Impaired Against Segregation, _Fundamental
Principles of Disability_ (1976); Michael Oliver, _The Politics of Disablement_ (1990);
Americans with Disabilities Act (1990); James I. Charlton, _Nothing About Us Without Us_
(1998); Tom Shakespeare, _Disability Rights and Wrongs_ (2006); UN Convention on the
Rights of Persons with Disabilities (2006); Sins Invalid, _Skin, Tooth, and Bone_ (2016);
Elizabeth Barnes, _The Minority Body_ (2016).
