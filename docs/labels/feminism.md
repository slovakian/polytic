# Feminism and gender politics: Label cards

Cards for this family, written to the template in `../label-library.md`, in the order
of the list. Feminism itself heads the family. Every Label here is Thin: it rides on a
host Ideology, so each card lists only the Dimensions its thin core commits it to and
leaves the rest to the host. Every card is written with the same dispassion as any
other: the self-description is the tradition's own self-understanding, steelmanned, and
never Polytic's verdict (`../principles.md`).

## Feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** the women's movement; women's liberation (its 1960s–70s form)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                    | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Middle – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_      | Yes     |
| Hierarchy or equality               | Leans _Equality is basic_ – Strongly _Equality is basic_ | No      |
| Causes of fortune                   | Middle – Strongly _Luck and structure_                   | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Women have been, and in many ways still are, disadvantaged because
they are women: excluded from power, paid less, burdened with unpaid care, and exposed
to violence and control by men. None of this follows from what women are. It is the
work of laws, customs, and institutions, and so it can be changed. Feminism is the
conviction that women are full and equal human beings, and the effort to make the world
treat them that way.

**Key concepts.**

- **Patriarchy:** a social order in which men as a group hold power over women, in the
  family, the economy, and the state; feminists differ on how deep it runs.
- **Gender:** the social meanings and roles laid on sex, which can be questioned and
  remade.
- **The personal is political:** private life (marriage, housework, sexuality) is shaped
  by power and is a proper subject of politics.
- **Equality:** equal standing and freedom for women; feminists differ on whether that
  means sameness of treatment or recognition of difference.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Feminism is the radical notion that women are people."
- "The personal is political."
- "One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman."
- "Equal work deserves equal pay."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Men's rights:** also concerned with sex-based disadvantage, but holds that men now
  bear neglected disadvantages and that feminism often overlooks or causes them
  (Reasons).
- **Social conservatism:** upholds distinct roles for men and women in family and public
  life (_Sex roles in family and public life_, the central Dimension).
- **Liberal feminism** and the other branches: feminism alone says only that women's
  disadvantage is real and should end; the branches disagree about its causes and cure.

**Sources.** Mary Wollstonecraft, _A Vindication of the Rights of Woman_ (1792); John
Stuart Mill, _The Subjection of Women_ (1869); Simone de Beauvoir, _The Second Sex_
(1949); bell hooks, _Feminism Is for Everybody_ (2000); Chris Beasley, _What Is
Feminism?_ (1999); Sally Haslanger, Nancy Tuana & Peg O'Connor, "Topics in Feminism",
_Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_.

## Liberal feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism
- **Also called:** mainstream feminism; equal-rights feminism
- **Variants:** Equity feminism
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                  | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_ – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_   | Yes     |
| Channels of change                  | Strongly _Within institutions_ – Leans _Within institutions_                           | Yes     |
| Tempo of change                     | Leans _Incremental reform_ – Strongly _Incremental reform_                             | No      |
| Individuals or wholes               | Strongly _Only individuals are real_ – Middle                                          | No      |
| Paternalism                         | Middle – Strongly _Choice_                                                             | No      |
| Life and body                       | Strongly _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_ – Leans _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_ | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Women are individuals with the same capacity for reason and the
same right to shape their own lives as men. What holds them back are unequal laws,
discrimination, and customs that close doors to them, and these can be removed through
the ordinary means of a liberal democracy: legislation, courts, education, and
persuasion. The goal is a society in which a person's sex no longer limits her
opportunities, and in which each woman is free to choose her own path.

**Key concepts.**

- **Equal rights:** the same legal and civil rights for women as for men, in voting,
  property, work, education, and marriage.
- **Equal opportunity:** removing barriers so that women can compete and succeed on
  equal terms.
- **Choice:** each woman decides how to live, including whether to work, marry, or have
  children; the state protects that choice rather than prescribing one.
- **Individual:** women are wronged as individuals denied fair treatment, rather than as
  members of an oppressed class.

**Signature reasons.**

- "A woman should be free to be anything a man can be."
- "Equal pay for equal work."
- "Change the law, open the doors, and let women choose."
- "My body, my choice."
- "Feminism is about equal rights, not special treatment." (its equity strand)

**Telling it apart.**

- **Radical feminism:** holds that patriarchy runs far deeper than law and opportunity,
  and seeks to uproot it (_Tempo of change_; Reasons).
- **Socialist feminism:** ties women's oppression to capitalism and unpaid care work
  (_Property in capital and goods_).
- **Difference feminism:** seeks recognition of women's distinct values rather than
  equal access to male-defined roles (_Sex roles in family and public life_; Reasons).
- **Social liberalism:** a host Ideology many liberal feminists share; this Label marks
  the feminist commitment within it.

**Sources.** Wollstonecraft (1792); Harriet Taylor Mill, "The Enfranchisement of Women"
(1851); Mill (1869); Betty Friedan, _The Feminine Mystique_ (1963); Susan Moller Okin,
_Justice, Gender, and the Family_ (1989); Christina Hoff Sommers, _Who Stole Feminism?_
(1994); Martha Nussbaum, _Sex and Social Justice_ (1999); Amy R. Baehr, "Liberal
Feminism", _Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy_.

## Radical feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism
- **Also called:** women's liberation (its radical wing)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                            | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_                                       | Yes     |
| Tempo of change                     | Strongly _Fundamental transformation_ – Leans _Fundamental transformation_       | Yes     |
| Individuals or wholes               | Leans _Groups and relations are real_ – Strongly _Groups and relations are real_ | No      |
| Sex and gender                      | Leans _Gender is made by society_ – Strongly _Gender is made by society_         | No      |
| Moral limits of markets             | Strongly _Protected non-market spheres_ – Leans _Protected non-market spheres_   | No      |
| Speech and expression               | Middle – Strongly _Protection from harmful expression_                           | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Male dominance is the oldest and deepest form of power, and it is
rooted in sex itself: in men's control of women's bodies, sexuality, and reproduction.
It shapes the family, law, work, culture, and even desire, so it cannot be fixed by
giving women equal access to institutions men built. Women are oppressed as a class, and
liberation means uprooting the whole system, including prostitution, pornography, and
male violence, rather than learning to succeed within it.

**Key concepts.**

- **Patriarchy:** a system of male power over women that runs through every
  institution and is the model for other hierarchies.
- **Sex class:** women are oppressed as women, as a class, not only as individuals.
- **Sexuality:** a central site of male dominance, so sexual practices and industries
  are political questions.
- **Gender:** a hierarchy imposed on the sexes; feminism aims to abolish it, not to
  celebrate it.
- **Consciousness-raising:** women sharing their experience to see the structure behind
  personal suffering.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The personal is political."
- "Patriarchy isn't a few bad laws; it's the whole system."
- "Prostitution and pornography are not work or speech. They are male power over
  women's bodies."
- "Gender isn't an identity; it's a hierarchy."
- "You can't dismantle the master's house by joining the board."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Liberal feminism:** reformist and individualist, working through equal rights and
  choice (_Tempo of change_, _Individuals or wholes_).
- **Socialist feminism:** traces women's oppression to capitalism as well as patriarchy;
  radical feminism takes patriarchy as primary (Reasons).
- **Sex-positive feminism:** sees sexual freedom, including sex work and pornography, as
  part of liberation (_Moral limits of markets_, _Speech and expression_).
- **Gender-critical feminism:** a branch that makes sex-based law its central cause
  (_Sex and gender identity_).

**Sources.** Carol Hanisch, "The Personal Is Political" (1970); Shulamith Firestone,
_The Dialectic of Sex_ (1970); Kate Millett, _Sexual Politics_ (1970); Andrea Dworkin,
_Pornography: Men Possessing Women_ (1981); Marilyn Frye, _The Politics of Reality_
(1983); Catharine A. MacKinnon, _Toward a Feminist Theory of the State_ (1989).

## Socialist feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism / Socialism
- **Also called:** Marxist feminism (its more orthodox strand); social reproduction
  feminism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                  | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_ – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_   | Yes     |
| Property in capital and goods       | Leans _Socially conditioned ownership_ – Strongly _Socially conditioned ownership_     | Yes     |
| Welfare and social insurance        | Leans _Public social insurance_ – Strongly _Public social insurance_                   | No      |
| Reciprocity in support              | Strongly _Unconditional support_ – Middle                                              | No      |
| Workplace governance                | Strongly _Worker voice and self-management_ – Leans _Worker voice and self-management_ | No      |
| Distribution                        | Leans _Egalitarian redistribution_ – Strongly _Egalitarian redistribution_             | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Women's oppression cannot be understood apart from the economy.
Capitalism depends on an enormous amount of unpaid work, mostly done by women: raising
children, caring for the sick and old, keeping households running, and so producing the
workforce itself. That dependence keeps women poorer, less free, and tied to men's
wages. Ending patriarchy and ending capitalism are bound together, and both require
collective provision of care and democratic control of the economy.

**Key concepts.**

- **Social reproduction:** the unpaid work of making and sustaining people, without which
  no economy could run.
- **Dual systems:** patriarchy and capitalism are two systems that reinforce each other
  (Hartmann); unitary theorists see one system (Vogel).
- **Care:** care work should be valued, shared, and supported publicly, not left to
  women's unpaid labour.
- **Class:** women's interests differ by class; liberation for a few women at the top is
  not liberation for women as a whole.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Capitalism runs on women's unpaid work."
- "They say it's love. We say it's unwaged work."
- "Childcare should be a public service, like schools."
- "Feminism for the 99 per cent, not the 1 per cent."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Liberal feminism:** seeks equality within a market economy (_Property in capital and
  goods_).
- **Radical feminism:** takes patriarchy as the primary system, with capitalism secondary
  (Reasons).
- **Intersectional feminism:** adds race and other systems to sex and class; many
  socialist feminists accept this, but class and capital stay central here (Reasons).
- **Socialism:** a host this Label rides on; socialist feminism adds the analysis of
  unpaid care and gender (_Sex roles in family and public life_).

**Sources.** Juliet Mitchell, "Women: The Longest Revolution" (1966); Silvia Federici,
_Wages Against Housework_ (1975) and _Caliban and the Witch_ (2004); Heidi Hartmann,
"The Unhappy Marriage of Marxism and Feminism" (1979); Zillah Eisenstein (ed.),
_Capitalist Patriarchy and the Case for Socialist Feminism_ (1979); Lise Vogel, _Marxism
and the Oppression of Women_ (1983); Nancy Fraser, _Fortunes of Feminism_ (2013); Tithi
Bhattacharya (ed.), _Social Reproduction Theory_ (2017).

## Difference feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism
- **Also called:** cultural feminism; care feminism; maternalist feminism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                  | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Distinct roles for the sexes_ – Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_         | Yes     |
| Sex and gender                      | Leans _Gender follows from sex_ – Middle                                               | No      |
| Welfare and social insurance        | Leans _Public social insurance_ – Strongly _Public social insurance_                   | No      |
| Reciprocity in support              | Strongly _Unconditional support_ – Middle                                              | No      |
| Group difference                    | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_ | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Equality should not mean women becoming like men. Women have, by
nature or by long experience, developed distinct ways of relating to others: an ethic of
care, attention to relationships, and the work of mothering. A world built around
competition and abstract rules treats those values as lesser. Feminism should demand
that they be honoured in public life, and that the care women give be recognised and
supported, rather than asking women to fit into roles designed by and for men.

**Key concepts.**

- **Ethic of care:** moral reasoning rooted in relationships and responsibility, as
  against an ethic of abstract rights and justice (Gilligan, Noddings).
- **Difference:** women's distinct experience and values are a source of strength, not
  a deficit to be overcome.
- **Maternal thinking:** the practices and wisdom of caring for children, with lessons
  for politics and peace (Ruddick).
- **Wollstonecraft's dilemma:** women are asked either to become like men or to accept
  being unequal; difference feminists seek equal worth for different contributions
  (Pateman).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Equality shouldn't mean women becoming like men."
- "Care is work, and it's the most important work there is."
- "A world run on women's values would be more peaceful."
- "Mothers deserve support, not just a place in the workforce."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Liberal feminism:** seeks equal access to the same roles; difference feminism seeks
  recognition of women's distinct ones (_Sex roles in family and public life_).
- **Social conservatism:** also values distinct roles, but wants law to uphold a
  traditional family form; difference feminists want women's distinct values honoured
  and their care supported on equal terms (Reasons).
- **Ecofeminism:** shares the valuation of care, and links it to nature (_Standing of
  nature_).
- **Queer feminism:** treats gender difference as performed and contestable, not a
  source of distinct values (_Sex and gender_).

**Sources.** Luce Irigaray, _Speculum of the Other Woman_ (1974; trans. 1985); Carol
Gilligan, _In a Different Voice_ (1982); Nel Noddings, _Caring_ (1984); Carole Pateman,
_The Sexual Contract_ (1988); Sara Ruddick, _Maternal Thinking_ (1989); Joan Tronto,
_Moral Boundaries_ (1993); Virginia Held, _The Ethics of Care_ (2006).

## Intersectional feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism
- **Also called:** Black feminism (its originating tradition); intersectionality
- **Variants:** Postcolonial feminism
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                                                                    | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_ – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_                                                     | Yes     |
| Group difference                    | Strongly _Group-differentiated recognition_ – Leans _Group-differentiated recognition_                                                   | Yes     |
| Causes of fortune                   | Leans _Luck and structure_ – Strongly _Luck and structure_                                                                               | No      |
| Race and ethnicity                  | Leans _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ – Strongly _Group differences are largely made by history and society_ | No      |
| Historical injustice                | Middle – Strongly _Rectification_                                                                                                        | No      |
| Sex and gender identity             | Middle – Strongly _Law follows gender identity_                                                                                          | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Women do not all experience oppression the same way. A Black woman,
a poor woman, a disabled woman, or a woman in the Global South faces sexism bound up with
racism, class, and other systems of power, and those combinations produce harms that
neither feminism nor anti-racism alone can see. A feminism that speaks only for the
women with the most privilege leaves most women out. Liberation has to address these
systems together, centring the women at their intersections.

**Key concepts.**

- **Intersectionality:** race, sex, class, sexuality, and disability interlock, producing
  distinct experiences of disadvantage that are not just the sum of their parts
  (Crenshaw).
- **Matrix of domination:** these systems are organised together at every level of
  society (Collins).
- **Standpoint:** those at the margins see structures of power that those at the centre
  miss.
- **Solidarity:** coalitions across difference, without one group's experience standing
  for all.

**Signature reasons.**

- "There is no such thing as a single-issue struggle, because we do not live
  single-issue lives."
- "If your feminism isn't intersectional, it isn't feminism."
- "You can't fight sexism and ignore racism."
- "Listen to the women who are most affected."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Liberal feminism:** treats women's disadvantage mainly as sex discrimination against
  individuals (_Group difference_, _Causes of fortune_).
- **Socialist feminism:** puts class and capital first; intersectional feminism refuses
  to rank systems of power (Reasons).
- **Identity-conscious progressivism:** a wider Ideology built on the same idea of
  interlocking oppressions; intersectional feminism keeps sex and gender at its centre
  (Reasons).
- **Critical race theory:** the legal movement where intersectionality was first named;
  it centres race, intersectional feminism centres women (Reasons).

**Sources.** Combahee River Collective, "A Black Feminist Statement" (1977); bell hooks,
_Ain't I a Woman_ (1981); Audre Lorde, _Sister Outsider_ (1984); Chandra Talpade Mohanty,
"Under Western Eyes" (1984); Kimberlé Crenshaw, "Demarginalizing the Intersection of
Race and Sex" (1989) and "Mapping the Margins" (1991); Patricia Hill Collins, _Black
Feminist Thought_ (1990); Patricia Hill Collins & Sirma Bilge, _Intersectionality_
(2016).

## Gender-critical feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism / Radical feminism
- **Also called:** sex-based rights feminism; critics: "TERF" (trans-exclusionary radical
  feminist), a term adherents regard as a slur
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Sex and gender identity             | Strongly _Law follows sex_                                                           | Yes     |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_ – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_ | Yes     |
| Sex and gender                      | Strongly _Gender follows from sex_ – Leans _Gender is made by society_               | No      |
| Speech and expression               | Strongly _Maximal expressive freedom_ – Leans _Maximal expressive freedom_           | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Sex is real, binary, and immutable, and it matters: women are
oppressed because of their sex, not because of an inner sense of identity. Women's
rights, single-sex spaces, sports, and data were won on the basis of sex, and redefining
"woman" by gender identity dissolves them. Gender stereotypes are the problem
feminism exists to fight, not an identity to be affirmed. Women must be free to say so
without losing their jobs.

**Key concepts.**

- **Sex:** a material, biological category that cannot be changed and remains the basis
  of women's oppression.
- **Gender:** a set of oppressive stereotypes laid on the sexes, to be abolished rather
  than adopted as an identity.
- **Sex-based rights:** protections for women (spaces, sports, prisons, shortlists, data)
  that only work if they are defined by sex.
- **Belief:** the view that sex is real is a protected belief, and women must be free to
  state it (_Forstater_).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Sex is real, and it matters."
- "Women's rights are based on sex, not identity."
- "You can't fight sexism by redefining what a woman is."
- "Gender stereotypes are the problem, not the solution."
- "Women must be able to say this without losing their jobs."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Queer feminism:** holds that gender is performed and that law should follow gender
  identity (_Sex and gender identity_, _Sex and gender_).
- **Radical feminism:** the tradition the gender-critical view grew from; not every
  radical feminist makes sex-based law the central cause, and some gender-critical
  feminists argue from liberal premises instead (_Sex and gender identity_; Reasons).
- **Social conservatism:** also holds that law should follow sex, but wants distinct
  roles for the sexes, which gender-critical feminists reject (_Sex roles in family and
  public life_).

**Sources.** Janice Raymond, _The Transsexual Empire_ (1979); Sheila Jeffreys, _Gender
Hurts_ (2014); _Forstater v CGD Europe_ (Employment Appeal Tribunal, 2021); Helen Joyce,
_Trans: When Ideology Meets Reality_ (2021); Kathleen Stock, _Material Girls_ (2021);
Holly Lawford-Smith, _Gender-Critical Feminism_ (2022); _For Women Scotland v Scottish
Ministers_ (UK Supreme Court, 2025).

## Queer feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism
- **Also called:** transfeminism (its trans-centred strand); queer-feminist theory
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                    | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex and gender                      | Leans _Gender is made by society_ – Strongly _Gender is made by society_                 | Yes     |
| Sex and gender identity             | Leans _Law follows gender identity_ – Strongly _Law follows gender identity_             | Yes     |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_                                               | No      |
| Legal moralism                      | Strongly _Liberty of self-regarding conduct_ – Leans _Liberty of self-regarding conduct_ | No      |
| Given or made                       | Middle – Strongly _Made by society_                                                      | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Gender is not a fixed fact that sex hands down. It is something done
and redone, through repeated acts and social norms that make some bodies and lives seem
natural and others deviant. The binary of man and woman, and the assumption that
everyone is heterosexual, are part of how power works. Feminism should not defend a
fixed category of "woman" but loosen these norms for everyone, and its liberation must
include trans, non-binary, and gender-nonconforming people.

**Key concepts.**

- **Performativity:** gender is produced by repeated acts and norms, not expressed from an
  inner essence (Butler).
- **Heteronormativity:** the assumption that heterosexuality and a gender binary are
  natural and normal.
- **Gender identity:** a person's own sense of their gender, which deserves recognition in
  law and life.
- **Coalition:** feminism and trans liberation share an enemy in rigid gender norms.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Trans women are women."
- "Gender is something we do, not something we are born with."
- "Feminism that excludes trans people isn't feminism."
- "No one should be forced into a box they didn't choose."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Gender-critical feminism:** holds that sex is real and that law should follow it
  (_Sex and gender identity_, _Sex and gender_).
- **Queer liberation:** shares the critique of heteronormativity, but is a movement of
  sexual and gender minorities against assimilation rather than a branch of feminism
  (_Tempo of change_; Reasons).
- **Difference feminism:** treats women's distinct values as real and worth honouring
  (_Sex and gender_).
- **Liberal feminism:** may support trans inclusion, but works with a stable category of
  women and does not question gender as such (_Sex and gender_).

**Sources.** Gayle Rubin, "Thinking Sex" (1984); Judith Butler, _Gender Trouble_ (1990)
and _Undoing Gender_ (2004); Eve Kosofsky Sedgwick, _Epistemology of the Closet_ (1990);
Emi Koyama, "The Transfeminist Manifesto" (2003); Julia Serano, _Whipping Girl_ (2007);
Talia Mae Bettcher, "Trapped in the Wrong Theory" (_Signs_, 2014).

## Sex-positive feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism
- **Also called:** pro-sex feminism; sex-radical feminism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Legal moralism                      | Strongly _Liberty of self-regarding conduct_                                         | Yes     |
| Moral limits of markets             | Leans _Markets without fixed limits_ – Strongly _Markets without fixed limits_       | Yes     |
| Speech and expression               | Strongly _Maximal expressive freedom_ – Leans _Maximal expressive freedom_           | No      |
| Paternalism                         | Leans _Choice_ – Strongly _Choice_                                                   | No      |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Leans _Equal and interchangeable roles_ – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_ | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Women's sexual freedom is part of women's liberation, not a
distraction from it. For centuries women's desire has been policed by men, the church,
and the law, and a feminism that treats pornography and sex work as inherently degrading
repeats that policing. Women can choose what to do with their own bodies, including
selling sexual services or making sexual images. What they need are rights, safety, and
freedom from stigma, not rescue or censorship.

**Key concepts.**

- **Sexual autonomy:** women decide for themselves what sex they have and on what terms.
- **Sex work:** work like other work, which should be decriminalised and protected by
  labour rights.
- **Censorship:** laws against pornography and sexual speech are used against women and
  sexual minorities more than for them.
- **Stigma:** the "whore stigma" that marks some women as unworthy, and that harms all
  women.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Sex work is work."
- "My body, my rules, including what I do with it for money."
- "Censorship has never protected women."
- "Women's desire is not the problem."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Radical feminism:** sees pornography and prostitution as male power over women and
  wants them abolished (_Moral limits of markets_, _Speech and expression_).
- **Liberal feminism:** often shares the defence of choice, but doesn't make sexual
  freedom central (Reasons).
- **Queer feminism:** shares the critique of sexual norms, but centres gender identity
  and the gender binary (_Sex and gender_).

**Sources.** Ellen Willis, "Lust Horizons: Is the Women's Movement Pro-Sex?" (1981);
Gayle Rubin, "Thinking Sex" (1984); Carole S. Vance (ed.), _Pleasure and Danger_ (1984);
Feminist Anti-Censorship Taskforce, brief in _American Booksellers v. Hudnut_ (1985);
Nadine Strossen, _Defending Pornography_ (1995); Wendy McElroy, _XXX: A Woman's Right to
Pornography_ (1995); Juno Mac & Molly Smith, _Revolting Prostitutes_ (2018).

## Ecofeminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism / Ecologism
- **Also called:** ecological feminism
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                                                          | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Middle – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_                                            | Yes     |
| Standing of nature                  | Leans _Nature-centered_ – Strongly _Nature-centered_                                           | Yes     |
| Environmental protection            | Leans _Ecological protection first_ – Strongly _Ecological protection first_                   | Yes     |
| Growth                              | Strongly _Sufficiency and limits_ – Leans _Sufficiency and limits_                             | No      |
| Animals                             | Strongly _Legal protection of animal interests_ – Leans _Legal protection of animal interests_ | No      |
| Technology and risk                 | Leans _Precaution_ – Strongly _Precaution_                                                     | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The domination of women and the domination of nature come from
the same root: a way of thinking that splits reason from emotion, mind from body, and
man from nature, and ranks the first above the second. Women and the natural world have
both been treated as resources to be used. They are freed together or not at all. A
politics of care, interdependence, and respect for life, often led by the women who feed
families and tend land, is the way out of both forms of domination.

**Key concepts.**

- **Dualism:** paired hierarchies (reason/nature, man/woman, mind/body) that justify
  domination (Plumwood).
- **Logic of domination:** the reasoning that turns difference into a licence to rule
  (Warren).
- **Subsistence:** the life-sustaining work of women and peasants, ignored by growth
  economics (Mies, Shiva).
- **Care:** an ethic of relationship extended to the living world.

**Signature reasons.**

- "The exploitation of women and of the Earth are linked."
- "There's no women's liberation on a dying planet."
- "Women who grow food and gather water know what 'development' destroys."
- "Care for people and care for the Earth are the same work."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Deep ecology:** shares ecocentrism, but does not trace domination of nature to
  patriarchy (Reasons).
- **Difference feminism:** values women's care, but without the link to nature (_Standing
  of nature_).
- **Eco-socialism:** traces ecological crisis to capitalism rather than patriarchal
  dualism (Reasons).

**Sources.** Françoise d'Eaubonne, _Le Féminisme ou la mort_ (1974); Carolyn Merchant,
_The Death of Nature_ (1980); Vandana Shiva, _Staying Alive_ (1988); Carol J. Adams,
_The Sexual Politics of Meat_ (1990); Maria Mies & Vandana Shiva, _Ecofeminism_ (1993);
Val Plumwood, _Feminism and the Mastery of Nature_ (1993); Karen J. Warren, _Ecofeminist
Philosophy_ (2000).

## Islamic feminism

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** Feminism
- **Also called:** Muslim feminism; gender jihad (Wadud)
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension                           | Range                                                              | Central |
| ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------- |
| Transcendence                       | Strongly _A transcendent order exists_                             | Yes     |
| Sex roles in family and public life | Middle – Strongly _Equal and interchangeable roles_                | Yes     |
| Source of moral authority           | Strongly _Given from beyond us_ – Middle                           | No      |
| Religion and state                  | Strongly _Public role for religion_ – Leans _Secular public order_ | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** The Qur'an affirms the equal worth of women and men before God,
who created both from a single soul. The patriarchy found in Muslim societies and laws
comes from centuries of male interpretation shaped by the cultures of the time, not from
revelation itself. Muslim women can and should read the sources for themselves, with the
tools of Islamic scholarship, and reform family law and religious practice from within
the faith, without having to choose between their religion and their equality.

**Key concepts.**

- **Tawhid:** the unity of God, which rules out any man standing above a woman as a
  lesser god over her (Wadud, Barlas).
- **Ijtihad:** independent interpretive reasoning, which women may exercise and which
  can reopen settled rulings.
- **Fiqh vs. sharia:** human jurisprudence is fallible and changeable; the divine path
  it tries to grasp is just.
- **Justice ('adl):** the Qur'an's overriding aim, by which inherited rulings must be
  tested.

**Signature reasons.**

- "Islam gave women rights; men took them away."
- "The Qur'an is egalitarian. The patriarchy came from its interpreters."
- "I don't have to choose between my faith and my equality."
- "Women can read the sacred texts for themselves."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Liberal feminism:** grounds equality in secular rights and individual autonomy;
  Islamic feminism grounds it in revelation (_Transcendence_, _Source of moral
  authority_).
- **Islamism:** many Islamists also invoke the Qur'an, but uphold distinct, complementary
  roles for the sexes in law (_Sex roles in family and public life_).
- **Muslim democracy:** a host many Islamic feminists share; this Label marks the
  feminist re-reading of the sources (Reasons).

**Sources.** Fatima Mernissi, _The Veil and the Male Elite_ (1987; trans. 1991); Leila
Ahmed, _Women and Gender in Islam_ (1992); Amina Wadud, _Qur'an and Woman_ (1992) and
_Inside the Gender Jihad_ (2006); Ziba Mir-Hosseini, _Islam and Gender_ (1999); Asma
Barlas, _"Believing Women" in Islam_ (2002); Musawah, _Framework for Action_ (2009);
Margot Badran, _Feminism in Islam_ (2009).

## Men's rights

- **Type:** Thin
- **Parent:** —
- **Also called:** the men's rights movement; men's issues advocacy; masculism; critics:
  part of "the manosphere"
- **Variants:** —
- **Centered group:** —

### Positions

| Dimension        | Range                                             | Central |
| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------- | ------- |
| Group difference | Middle – Strongly _Difference-blind common rules_ | Yes     |
| Sex and gender   | Strongly _Gender follows from sex_ – Middle       | No      |

### Reasons

**Self-description.** Men face serious disadvantages that politics ignores because they
don't fit the story that society favours men. Men die younger, take their own lives far
more often, make up most of the homeless and the dead at work, receive harsher
sentences, lose custody of their children, and are the only ones conscripted. Boys are
falling behind in school. Equality should mean equal treatment under the law and equal
concern for men's suffering, not policies that assume only women can be victims.

**Key concepts.**

- **Male disposability:** the expectation that men take the dangerous jobs, fight the
  wars, and sacrifice themselves, and that their suffering matters less (Farrell).
- **Equal treatment:** sex-neutral law in family courts, sentencing, conscription, and
  services such as domestic-violence shelters.
- **Empathy gap:** society responds to women's suffering more readily than to men's.
- **Second sexism:** discrimination against men, which is real even if less recognised
  (Benatar).

**Signature reasons.**

- "Men take their own lives at three times the rate of women, and nobody talks about it."
- "Fathers deserve an equal presumption of custody."
- "Equality under the law means equal treatment for men too."
- "Only men get drafted."
- "Boys are falling behind, and we pretend not to notice."

**Telling it apart.**

- **Feminism:** holds that women are the disadvantaged sex; men's rights holds that men
  now face neglected disadvantages, often in areas feminism has shaped (Reasons).
- **Liberal feminism:** its equity strand shares the demand for sex-neutral law, but
  focuses on women's opportunities (Reasons).
- **Social conservatism:** some men's rights advocates share its view of distinct roles,
  but men's rights centres men's grievances under current law, and many of its advocates
  want equal roles (_Sex roles in family and public life_; Reasons).

**Sources.** Ernest Belfort Bax, _The Fraud of Feminism_ (1913); Herb Goldberg, _The
Hazards of Being Male_ (1976); Warren Farrell, _The Myth of Male Power_ (1993); David
Benatar, _The Second Sexism_ (2012); Michael A. Messner, _Politics of Masculinities_
(1997).
