# Economic coordination

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Economy_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Public direction ↔ Market coordination
- **Definition:** Should the economy be coordinated mainly through prices and
  competition, or through deliberate collective direction (ownership, planning,
  industrial policy, heavy regulation), whether by the state or by councils, communes,
  and federations?
- **Facets:** Ownership of key sectors; industrial policy; price controls; regulatory
  intensity; public vs. private delivery; non-state planning (councils, federated
  communes).
- **Note:** Whether the direction comes from a state is measured by _Political
  authority_, so anarcho-communists and state socialists share this Pole and part on
  that one (widened in [Author the label
  library](https://github.com/slovakian/polytic/issues/27)).

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

_Public direction_ includes direction by councils, cooperatives, and federations as
well as by the state. Whether the direction comes from a state is recorded on
_Political authority_, never here.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The water company

- **Tests:** Economic coordination: _Public direction_ ↔ _Market coordination_
- **Also touches:** Property in capital and goods, Economic scale and concentration
- **Facet:** Ownership of key sectors
- **Scenario:** The private company that supplies a region's water has paid large
  dividends while leaks and outages grew. One plan is to take water into public
  ownership, run by the state or by a users' cooperative. The other is to keep it
  private under tougher contracts and penalties. Which should the region do?
- **Localise:** The region and the kind of operator. Never change that both plans
  exist and are workable.
- **Case for _Public direction_:** Water is a natural monopoly where competition
  cannot work, and taking profit out of a necessity people cannot do without is wrong.
  Public or common ownership puts the service first and answers to the people who
  depend on it.
- **Case for _Market coordination_:** Public ownership removes the pressure to be
  efficient and puts a vital service at the mercy of budgets and politics. The fix is
  better contracts and real penalties, keeping the discipline of private capital that
  has to answer for its own losses.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Public direction_:** Tougher contracts and fines have been tried three
    times, and the company paid the fines and carried on.
  - **Toward _Market coordination_:** The publicly run water service in the next region
    has worse leaks and higher bills.

### The rent cap

- **Tests:** Economic coordination: _Public direction_ ↔ _Market coordination_
- **Also touches:** Paternalism, Property in capital and goods
- **Facet:** Price controls
- **Scenario:** Rents in a city have doubled in five years. A proposal would cap rent
  increases at the rate of inflation. Should the city adopt it?
- **Localise:** The city and the figures. Never state the cap's effect on supply; that
  is a dial.
- **Case for _Public direction_:** Housing is a basic need, and tenants facing huge
  rises have no real choice. Leaving it to the market means families pushed out of the
  places they belong to, so the public should set fair limits on what can be charged.
- **Case for _Market coordination_:** Prices signal scarcity. Capping rents cuts new
  building and upkeep, and leaves fewer homes for the next tenants. The way to bring
  rents down is to let more homes be built.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Public direction_:** The cap applies only to existing homes, and builders
    say new construction will go on.
  - **Toward _Market coordination_:** A nearby city that capped rents saw its rental
    housing shrink by a fifth.
- **Rules:** —

### The strategic industry

- **Tests:** Economic coordination: _Public direction_ ↔ _Market coordination_
- **Also touches:** Trade and economic openness, Technology and risk
- **Facet:** Industrial policy
- **Scenario:** Private investors find a new industry, say large-scale battery making,
  too risky to build at home. The government is considering spending heavily to build
  it anyway, rather than leaving it to the market to decide where the country competes.
  Should it?
- **Localise:** The industry may be any strategic one debated locally. Never claim the
  project will succeed or fail.
- **Case for _Public direction_:** Markets underinvest in long-term, strategic
  industries. Nearly every rich country built its industries with deliberate public
  backing, and waiting for private capital means handing the future to whoever did not
  wait.
- **Case for _Market coordination_:** Governments are poor at picking winners and good
  at subsidising losers with political friends. Investors risking their own money
  judge better, and public money spent here is money taken from somewhere else.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Public direction_:** The industry is needed for the country's energy
    security, and a rival power already dominates it.
  - **Toward _Market coordination_:** The last two such projects failed after years of
    subsidy.
