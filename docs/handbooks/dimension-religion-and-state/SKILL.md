# Religion and state

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _The state and the good life_.
Copied from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Public role for religion ↔ Secular public order
- **Definition:** Should public institutions be kept free of religion, or should the
  state recognise, support, or be guided by religion?
- **Facets:** Establishment and funding; religious schools; symbols; exemptions;
  blasphemy; religious law in family matters.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The faith school

- **Tests:** Religion and state: _Public role for religion_ ↔ _Secular public order_
- **Also touches:** Pluralism vs. unity, Neutrality vs. perfectionism
- **Facet:** Religious schools and public funding
- **Scenario:** Religious schools that teach the national curriculum alongside their
  faith ask for the same public funding per pupil as state schools. Should they get
  it?
- **Localise:** The faith may be the country's main religion or a minority one. Never
  say the schools' results are better or worse; that is a dial.
- **Case for _Public role for religion_:** Faith is central to many families' lives,
  and parents have a right to schools that share it. Funding them equally respects
  that right and recognises the good that religious communities do for everyone.
- **Case for _Secular public order_:** Public money should fund schools that are open
  to all and teach no faith. Funding religious schools divides children by creed and
  makes every taxpayer pay for other people's religion.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Public role for religion_:** The religious schools get better results and
    take many children of no faith.
  - **Toward _Secular public order_:** The schools may turn away children of other
    faiths.
- **Rules:** —

### The blasphemy charge

- **Tests:** Religion and state: _Public role for religion_ ↔ _Secular public order_
- **Also touches:** Speech and expression, Sacred or tradeable
- **Facet:** Blasphemy
- **Scenario:** A country's law makes it a crime to insult the sacred figures of its
  main religion. A writer has been charged over a novel. Should such a law exist?
- **Localise:** The religion may be the country's main one. Never name a real writer,
  book, or prophet.
- **Case for _Public role for religion_:** Religion is the deepest thing in many
  people's lives. The law protects what a people holds sacred, as it protects people
  from other grave insults, and public peace depends on that respect.
- **Case for _Secular public order_:** The state should take no side on religious
  truth. Protecting one faith's sacred figures makes the law an enforcer of belief and
  turns dissenters and other faiths into criminals.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Public role for religion_:** The novel was written to provoke, and it has
    already led to riots.
  - **Toward _Secular public order_:** The law has mostly been used against members of
    minority religions.
- **Rules:** —

### The religious family court

- **Tests:** Religion and state: _Public role for religion_ ↔ _Secular public order_
- **Also touches:** Pluralism vs. unity, Sex roles in family and public life
- **Facet:** Religious law in family matters
- **Scenario:** A religious community asks that its own courts may decide divorce and
  inheritance for members who choose them, with the state enforcing the rulings. The
  community's law treats men and women differently in these matters. Should the state
  allow it?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never name the religion.
- **Case for _Public role for religion_:** Religious law is part of how believers live
  their faith. Letting consenting members settle family matters by it respects freedom
  of religion and the life of the community, which the state has no business
  replacing.
- **Case for _Secular public order_:** One law for everyone is the basis of equal
  citizenship. A parallel system whose rules treat women unequally will bind those who
  cannot really refuse it, whatever the forms say.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Public role for religion_:** Either party can move the case to the state
    courts at any stage.
  - **Toward _Secular public order_:** Women who refuse the religious court risk being
    cut off by their families.
- **Rules:** —
