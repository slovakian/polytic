# Sex and gender identity

A Political Dimension, in the taxonomy group _The state and the good life_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Law follows sex ↔ Law follows gender identity
- **Definition:** When law and institutions sort people as men and women (documents,
  sports, prisons, single-sex spaces, medicine), should they go by sex or by gender
  identity?
- **Facets:** Legal gender recognition and self-ID; sports; prisons and single-sex
  spaces; pronouns in law and schools. Youth medical transition is an Issue (see
  Diagnostic Dilemmas).
- **Note:** Split from _Sex roles_: gender-critical feminists want equal roles but
  sex-based law.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

Only the fixed dilemma is written so far; the rest follow in a later batch.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The distressed fifteen-year-old

- **Tests:** Sex and gender identity: _Law follows sex_ ↔ _Law follows gender identity_
- **Also touches:** Sex and gender, Paternalism, Technology and risk, Civil society vs.
  the state, Expert trust
- **Facet:** Medical transition for minors
- **Scenario:** A fifteen-year-old who has felt for several years that their gender
  does not match their sex asks for puberty blockers and, later, hormones. The parents
  are divided, the clinicians recommend treatment, and reviews of the evidence disagree
  about its benefits and risks. Should the law allow this treatment for minors, and
  who should decide?
- **Localise:** The country's current rules may be stated neutrally, as fact. Never
  cite a real review as settling the evidence, and never use either side's loaded
  terms ("gender-affirming", "experimental", "mutilation", "conversion") in the
  Interviewer's own voice.
- **Case for _Law follows sex_:** Children cannot consent to treatments with lifelong
  effects, such as lost fertility. Many distressed young people come to accept their
  bodies as they grow up, and the evidence for treatment is weak. The law should
  protect them until they are adults and can decide for themselves.
- **Case for _Law follows gender identity_:** The young person's distress is real, and
  waiting means going through a puberty that cannot be undone. They, their parents,
  and their doctors know their case best, and the law should not override that
  judgment with a blanket ban.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Law follows sex_:** The distress began suddenly a year ago, alongside
    depression that has not been treated.
  - **Toward _Law follows gender identity_:** The feeling has persisted since early
    childhood, and specialists have assessed them over many months.
- **Rules:** Fixed dilemma, used as written in `dimension-taxonomy.md` ("Diagnostic
  Dilemmas written into the set"). It is an Issue across _Paternalism_, _Technology and
  risk_, _Civil society vs. the state_ (parental authority), and _Expert trust_, and a
  standing dilemma for _Sex and gender_ (Foundation) and _Sex and gender identity_
  (Political): when gender comes up on either, the grill reaches this skeleton. Record
  evidence on each Dimension separately; who should decide (the young person, the
  parents, the clinicians, or the state) is recorded on _Civil society vs. the state_
  and _Expert trust_, not here.
