# Human nature

A Foundation Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Human nature_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Fixed and flawed ↔ Malleable and improvable
- **Definition:** Is human nature essentially fixed, with permanent limits of
  selfishness, knowledge, and virtue that institutions must work around, or largely
  shaped by circumstance, so better institutions can make people substantially better?

## Using these skeletons

This is a Foundation Dimension: every skeleton asks what the person takes to be true or
deserved, never what the law should do. Keep it that way when you localise. Pole order
means nothing. Localise only as `docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic
institutions, places, and amounts for local ones; never change the collision, a fact
either side relies on, or either Case. For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial**
variant that pushes against the person's answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The sharing community

- **Tests:** Human nature: _Fixed and flawed_ ↔ _Malleable and improvable_
- **Also touches:** Design vs. evolved order, Self-interest or duty to others
- **Facet:** Whether better arrangements make better people
- **Scenario:** A group founds a community where everything is shared and no one is
  paid more than anyone else. A generation later it still runs, but some members do
  much less than others, and a quiet pecking order has formed. One founder says this
  proves people cannot be remade; another says it only shows how deep old habits go,
  and that children raised wholly inside it will be different. Who reads it better?
- **Localise:** The kind of community (village, farm, workshop). Never change that it
  has lasted a generation, or that both drift and endurance are real.
- **Case for _Fixed and flawed_:** Self-interest and the wish for status come back
  wherever people live together, because they are part of us, not taught. Wise
  arrangements work with that and channel it. Arrangements that expect to transform
  people end up blaming, and then forcing, those who do not change.
- **Case for _Malleable and improvable_:** Much of what we call human nature is
  learned. People raised under different arrangements behave very differently, as
  history shows with literacy, cruelty, and trust. One generation is too short to
  judge, and expecting the worst of people helps produce it.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Fixed and flawed_:** The same pecking order formed in every such
    community that has been studied.
  - **Toward _Malleable and improvable_:** The children raised wholly inside share far
    more readily than their parents ever did.
- **Rules:** —

### The quieter centuries

- **Tests:** Human nature: _Fixed and flawed_ ↔ _Malleable and improvable_
- **Also touches:** Direction of history
- **Facet:** Moral progress over history
- **Scenario:** Over centuries, many societies have become far less violent: fewer
  killings, less cruel punishment, less acceptance of beating children. Does that show
  people becoming better, or the same nature held in check by restraints that could
  fail?
- **Localise:** Examples of the change may be drawn from the person's own region's
  history. Never claim the change was universal or complete.
- **Case for _Fixed and flawed_:** The capacity for cruelty has not gone; it returns
  quickly when order breaks down, as wars and riots show. What changed are the
  restraints: law, policing, prosperity, habit. They have to be kept up, precisely
  because what lies beneath them has not changed.
- **Case for _Malleable and improvable_:** People raised in gentler societies do not
  merely hold back; they find cruelty repugnant in a way their ancestors did not. Norms
  become part of who people are. That is real improvement in people, and it can go
  further.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Fixed and flawed_:** In a neighbouring region where order collapsed,
    ordinary people committed atrocities within months.
  - **Toward _Malleable and improvable_:** Children of families who left violent
    places for peaceful ones are as gentle as anyone born there.
- **Rules:** —

### The honest newcomer

- **Tests:** Human nature: _Fixed and flawed_ ↔ _Malleable and improvable_
- **Also touches:** Leadership
- **Facet:** Power and character
- **Scenario:** A young idealist, known for honesty, takes a powerful post. Two of her
  friends disagree about what twenty years will do to her. One says power wears down
  everyone in the end; the other says that a person of well-formed character can stay
  who she is. Which is closer to the truth about people?
- **Localise:** The kind of post (office, firm, institution). Never make her a real or
  recognisable figure.
- **Case for _Fixed and flawed_:** Flattery, self-interest, and the wish to keep power
  work on everyone. The reformers who became what they replaced were not unusually
  weak; they were ordinary. Counting on virtue to hold is the mistake that lets abuse
  grow.
- **Case for _Malleable and improvable_:** Character is formed by upbringing and
  surroundings, and some people are formed well enough to stay honest under pressure;
  many have. Assuming that everyone can be bought breeds a cynicism that keeps good
  people from serving.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Fixed and flawed_:** She has already begun to excuse small favours as
    necessary.
  - **Toward _Malleable and improvable_:** She comes from a tradition of public service
    that has produced honest officials for generations.
