# Expert trust

A Foundation Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Knowledge_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Defer to expert institutions ↔ Judge for yourself
- **Definition:** When you cannot check something yourself, should you defer to experts
  and their institutions, or keep final judgment for yourself and your community?

## Using these skeletons

This is a Foundation Dimension: every skeleton asks what the person takes to be true or
deserved, never what the law should do. Keep it that way when you localise. Pole order
means nothing. Localise only as `docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic
institutions, places, and amounts for local ones; never change the collision, a fact
either side relies on, or either Case. For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial**
variant that pushes against the person's answer.

Every scenario here asks whom the person would trust, never what the state should do
with expert advice (that is _Popular judgment vs. expertise_). Medical transition for
minors also touches this Dimension; it is the fixed skeleton _The distressed
fifteen-year-old_ under _Sex and gender identity_ (`dimension-sex-and-gender-identity`).

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The new treatment

- **Tests:** Expert trust: _Defer to expert institutions_ ↔ _Judge for yourself_
- **Also touches:** Grounds for belief, Technology and risk
- **Facet:** Medicine and personal decisions
- **Scenario:** Your doctor and the national health authority recommend a new
  treatment for your child. A friend whose judgment you respect has read the trial
  data and thinks the risks are understated. You cannot check the evidence yourself.
  Whose judgment should decide what you do?
- **Localise:** The health authority. Never name a real treatment or illness.
- **Case for _Defer to expert institutions_:** Expert institutions pool far more
  knowledge, data, and checking than any one person can. When you cannot weigh the
  evidence yourself, your friend's reading is also an amateur's, and trusting a system
  with a long record is the rational choice, not the lazy one.
- **Case for _Judge for yourself_:** Institutions have incentives and blind spots, and
  they have been wrong before. You know your child and your situation, and a
  responsible adult weighs the experts as one voice among several. Handing your final
  judgment to anyone, however qualified, is giving up a duty that is yours.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Defer to expert institutions_:** Independent authorities in several other
    countries reached the same recommendation.
  - **Toward _Judge for yourself_:** The treatment's maker paid for every trial.
- **Rules:** —

### The economists' warning

- **Tests:** Expert trust: _Defer to expert institutions_ ↔ _Judge for yourself_
- **Also touches:** Grounds for belief, Popular judgment vs. expertise
- **Facet:** Expert consensus against lived experience
- **Scenario:** Nearly all economists in your country warn that a proposed change will
  make most people poorer. Many ordinary people, including those who work in the
  affected trade, say from experience that it will not. Whose judgment should you
  trust?
- **Localise:** The change may be any economic policy debated locally, described
  without saying which side proposes it. Never say who turns out to be right.
- **Case for _Defer to expert institutions_:** Economists study effects across many
  cases with methods that correct for what personal experience cannot see. When they
  broadly agree, that agreement is strong evidence, and experience from inside one
  trade is real but partial.
- **Case for _Judge for yourself_:** Expert models have failed before, often for the
  very people they were about. Those who live inside a trade see what the models miss,
  and a professional consensus can reflect a field's shared assumptions as much as the
  evidence.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Defer to expert institutions_:** The economists include many who usually
    disagree with each other about politics.
  - **Toward _Judge for yourself_:** The same economists confidently predicted the
    opposite about a similar change a decade ago and were wrong.
- **Rules:** —

### The reversed advice

- **Tests:** Expert trust: _Defer to expert institutions_ ↔ _Judge for yourself_
- **Also touches:** Grounds for belief
- **Facet:** What an expert reversal shows
- **Scenario:** For years, experts told the public that a common food was dangerous;
  then the advice reversed. One friend says this shows you should judge for yourself.
  Another says it shows science correcting itself, which is exactly why you should
  keep trusting it. Who is right?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never name a real food or controversy.
- **Case for _Defer to expert institutions_:** Changing the advice when the evidence
  changes is the system working. People judging alone would make more mistakes and
  rarely notice or correct them. A record of correction is a reason for trust, not
  against it.
- **Case for _Judge for yourself_:** The old advice was presented as settled, which
  shows how a consensus can run ahead of its evidence. That is a reason to treat
  authority as one source among several and to keep your own judgment in play.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Defer to expert institutions_:** The reversal came from a large, careful
    new study the experts themselves ran.
  - **Toward _Judge for yourself_:** The original advice was traced to studies paid for
    by a rival industry.
