# Popular judgment vs. expertise

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Who decides_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Rule by the knowledgeable ↔ Equal popular judgment
- **Definition:** Should political decisions, or the choice of who makes them, give
  special weight to knowledge and competence, or should every citizen's judgment count
  equally?
- **Facets:** Independent central banks and regulators; weighted franchise; meritocratic
  selection; scientific advice.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The central bank

- **Tests:** Popular judgment vs. expertise: _Rule by the knowledgeable_ ↔ _Equal
  popular judgment_
- **Also touches:** Expert trust, Money and public finance
- **Facet:** Independent central banks and regulators
- **Scenario:** A country's interest rates are set by an independent central bank,
  run by economists whom elected politicians cannot remove. A movement wants rate
  decisions handed back to the elected government. Should they be?
- **Localise:** The central bank and the country's current arrangement, stated as
  fact. Never name the movement as a real party.
- **Case for _Rule by the knowledgeable_:** Interest rates need technical judgment and
  long horizons that politicians facing the next election lack. Putting them beyond
  politics has kept inflation down, and inflation hurts savers and the poor most.
- **Case for _Equal popular judgment_:** Decisions that shape people's jobs and homes
  should answer to the people. When unelected economists make them, the public loses
  its say over its own economy, and experts carry biases of their own that no one can
  vote out.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Rule by the knowledgeable_:** Before the bank was independent,
    governments cut rates before every election, and inflation followed.
  - **Toward _Equal popular judgment_:** The bank's last policy hit workers hard while
    protecting those who own property, and no one answered for it.

### The extra vote

- **Tests:** Popular judgment vs. expertise: _Rule by the knowledgeable_ ↔ _Equal
  popular judgment_
- **Also touches:** Hierarchy or equality, Citizenship: rights vs. duties
- **Facet:** Weighted franchise
- **Scenario:** A political thinker proposes that citizens who pass a basic test of
  political knowledge get a second vote. Should a country adopt it?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising.
- **Case for _Rule by the knowledgeable_:** A vote affects everyone, and casting it in
  ignorance harms others. Giving more weight to informed voters would produce better
  decisions without taking anyone's vote away.
- **Case for _Equal popular judgment_:** Every citizen lives under the law and so
  deserves an equal say in it. Any test will be written by those in power and favour
  the educated, and ordinary people know things about their own lives that tests
  cannot measure.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Rule by the knowledgeable_:** The test is set by an independent body, and
    free preparation is offered to everyone.
  - **Toward _Equal popular judgment_:** In the past, literacy tests were used to stop
    particular groups from voting.
- **Rules:** —

### The examined officials

- **Tests:** Popular judgment vs. expertise: _Rule by the knowledgeable_ ↔ _Equal
  popular judgment_
- **Also touches:** Representation vs. participation
- **Facet:** Meritocratic selection
- **Scenario:** A country is considering choosing its senior officials, and part of
  its legislature, by rigorous examination and record of performance rather than by
  election. Should it?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never tie the idea to a real country's
  system.
- **Case for _Rule by the knowledgeable_:** Governing is a skill. Choosing rulers for
  proven competence and character, as some long traditions have done, gives wiser
  policy than campaigns that reward charm and money.
- **Case for _Equal popular judgment_:** Rulers chosen by examination answer to no
  one, and power without accountability serves itself. The people's right to choose
  and remove those who govern them is what makes that power legitimate.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Rule by the knowledgeable_:** The elected legislators rarely read the
    laws they pass.
  - **Toward _Equal popular judgment_:** Nearly all the examined officials come from
    wealthy families.
