# Immigration and borders

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Membership and the world_.
Copied from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Community control over membership ↔ Freedom of movement
- **Definition:** Should a political community decide who and how many may enter and
  settle, or do people have a strong claim to move and live where they choose?
- **Facets:** Levels; selection criteria; asylum; enforcement; path to citizenship.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The cap on numbers

- **Tests:** Immigration and borders: _Community control over membership_ ↔ _Freedom
  of movement_
- **Also touches:** Basis of nationhood, Compatriot priority vs. global impartiality
- **Facet:** Levels
- **Scenario:** Immigration to a country has tripled in a decade. A proposal would cap
  it at the level of ten years ago. Should it pass?
- **Localise:** The figures, scaled to the country. Never name a nationality or
  religion among the newcomers.
- **Case for _Community control over membership_:** A political community has the
  right to decide who joins it and how fast. Rapid change strains housing, wages, and a
  shared way of life, and citizens are entitled to set its pace through their votes.
- **Case for _Freedom of movement_:** People who move to work and build a better life
  harm no one by coming. The accident of where someone was born should not decide
  their prospects, and newcomers contribute to the economy and to the society they
  join.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Community control over membership_:** Waiting lists for housing have
    doubled in the same decade.
  - **Toward _Freedom of movement_:** The country's workforce is shrinking, and most
    newcomers work in caring for the old.

### The boat

- **Tests:** Immigration and borders: _Community control over membership_ ↔ _Freedom
  of movement_
- **Also touches:** Moral circle, Rule of law vs. discretion
- **Facet:** Asylum and enforcement
- **Scenario:** A boat arrives without permission, carrying people who say they are
  fleeing persecution. Some may have strong claims; others may be looking for work. The
  government wants to return every boat to the country it sailed from and take refugees
  only through official channels. Should it?
- **Localise:** The route may be by land or sea. Never state how many on board are
  genuine refugees.
- **Case for _Community control over membership_:** A country that cannot control its
  borders cannot keep public support for taking in refugees. Orderly channels are fairer
  to those waiting in camps, and returning boats removes the reason to risk a deadly
  crossing.
- **Case for _Freedom of movement_:** People fleeing persecution cannot wait in line.
  Returning them without hearing their claims risks sending them back into danger, and
  the right to seek asylum does not depend on how a person arrives.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Community control over membership_:** Smugglers charge huge fees, and
    dozens drown on the crossing every month.
  - **Toward _Freedom of movement_:** The country they sailed from routinely sends
    people back to the place they fled.
- **Rules:** —

### The twenty-year resident

- **Tests:** Immigration and borders: _Community control over membership_ ↔ _Freedom
  of movement_
- **Also touches:** Rule of law vs. discretion, Citizenship: rights vs. duties
- **Facet:** Path to citizenship
- **Scenario:** A woman came into a country illegally as a child and has lived there
  for twenty years. She works and pays taxes. Should she be deported, or allowed to
  stay and become a citizen?
- **Localise:** The country's current rules may be stated neutrally, as fact. Never
  give her a nationality.
- **Case for _Community control over membership_:** Membership rules have to mean
  something. Rewarding those who broke them is unfair to those who waited their turn,
  and it encourages more people to come illegally in the hope of the same.
- **Case for _Freedom of movement_:** She has done nothing wrong as an adult and knows
  no other home. Deporting her serves nothing but enforcement for its own sake, and
  twenty years of work and life have made her a member in every way but on paper.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Community control over membership_:** A previous amnesty was followed by
    a large rise in illegal entry.
  - **Toward _Freedom of movement_:** Her own children were born there and are
    citizens.
