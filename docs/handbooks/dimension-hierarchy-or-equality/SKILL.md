# Hierarchy or equality

A Foundation Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _The social world_. Copied
from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Hierarchy is natural ↔ Equality is basic
- **Definition:** Are people naturally ranked (by ability, virtue, role, or strength),
  so that well-ordered hierarchy is part of the good, or fundamentally equal in worth,
  so that every hierarchy needs justification? Asked as what is true, not what society
  should do (that is _Social hierarchy_).

## Using these skeletons

This is a Foundation Dimension: every skeleton asks what the person takes to be true or
deserved, never what the law should do. Keep it that way when you localise. Pole order
means nothing. Localise only as `docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic
institutions, places, and amounts for local ones; never change the collision, a fact
either side relies on, or either Case. For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial**
variant that pushes against the person's answer.

The taxonomy asks this as what is true, not what society should do (that is _Social
hierarchy_). None of these scenarios asks for a law.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The orchestra

- **Tests:** Hierarchy or equality: _Hierarchy is natural_ ↔ _Equality is basic_
- **Also touches:** Social hierarchy
- **Facet:** Rank by excellence
- **Scenario:** An orchestra has a conductor, principal players, and section players.
  A new member argues that every rank has to be justified from scratch; an old hand
  replies that the ranks reflect real differences in mastery, and that everyone plays
  better because of them. Whose view of people is closer to the truth?
- **Localise:** Any skilled group with ranks (a kitchen, a crew, a workshop). Never
  turn it into pay or law.
- **Case for _Hierarchy is natural_:** People really do differ in skill, wisdom, and
  virtue, and recognising the better is accuracy, not injustice. A well-ordered rank
  lets the best lead and gives everyone a place and a standard to rise to. Pretending
  otherwise wrongs the excellent and helps no one.
- **Case for _Equality is basic_:** Ranks can be useful for a task, but they say
  nothing about whose life matters more. Every hierarchy begins as some people's claim
  over others, and it has to keep earning its place, because no one is born to command
  another.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Hierarchy is natural_:** The orchestra tried playing without ranks and
    played noticeably worse.
  - **Toward _Equality is basic_:** The ranks were set decades ago, and the present
    principals are no longer the best players.

### The born rulers

- **Tests:** Hierarchy or equality: _Hierarchy is natural_ ↔ _Equality is basic_
- **Also touches:** Tradition or reason, Leadership
- **Facet:** Rank by birth
- **Scenario:** In an old society, a noble line has governed its lands for centuries,
  raising each child for the duty from birth, and many say it governs well. Is there
  something fitting about some people being born to rule and others to serve, or is it
  wrong in principle, whatever the results?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never make it a real dynasty.
- **Case for _Hierarchy is natural_:** Societies have long understood that people have
  different stations, and that forming a family for a role over generations produces
  real excellence. A fitting order, where each knows their place and its duties, can
  be more humane than a scramble in which everyone competes for status.
- **Case for _Equality is basic_:** No one is born to rule another. Equal worth means
  that authority has to be justified to those who live under it, and good results do
  not make an unchosen rank legitimate.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Hierarchy is natural_:** The nobles live under strict duties, and the
    people prefer them to rulers they could choose.
  - **Toward _Equality is basic_:** Commoners of equal ability can never rise, whatever
    they do.
- **Rules:** —

### The elders

- **Tests:** Hierarchy or equality: _Hierarchy is natural_ ↔ _Equality is basic_
- **Also touches:** Self-authorship or given role, Tradition or reason
- **Facet:** Rank by station in natural relations
- **Scenario:** In many traditions, the young owe their elders honour and obedience
  because of who they are, not only because of what they know. Is that fitting, or
  should respect between adults follow only from what each person has earned?
- **Localise:** The relationship may be any traditional one in the person's culture
  (parents, elders, teachers). Never involve the sexes; that is _Sex roles in family
  and public life_.
- **Case for _Hierarchy is natural_:** Some relations carry real rank by nature:
  parent and child, elder and young, teacher and student. Honouring them is fitting
  gratitude, and it holds a community together across generations. Reducing every
  bond to earned respect leaves the old and the past without a claim on anyone.
- **Case for _Equality is basic_:** Age gives no adult authority over another. Basic
  respect is owed to everyone equally, and anything beyond it, including deference,
  has to be earned. Obedience owed by birth order is still a rank no one chose.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Hierarchy is natural_:** The elders made great sacrifices to raise the
    young.
  - **Toward _Equality is basic_:** An elder demands obedience on a matter they know
    nothing about.
