# Speech and expression

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _The state and the good life_.
Copied from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Maximal expressive freedom ↔ Protection from harmful expression
- **Definition:** Should expression be protected even when offensive, hateful, or false,
  or may the law restrict speech that harms dignity, equality, or public order?
- **Facets:** Hate speech; misinformation and platform moderation; campaign finance;
  academic freedom.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The march through the neighbourhood

- **Tests:** Speech and expression: _Maximal expressive freedom_ ↔ _Protection from
  harmful expression_
- **Also touches:** Security and civil liberties, Pluralism vs. unity
- **Facet:** Hate speech
- **Scenario:** A small group wants to march through a neighbourhood where many
  members of a minority live, carrying banners saying the minority does not belong in
  the country. It plans no violence. Should the law let the march go ahead?
- **Localise:** The minority may be any group targeted in the person's country. Never
  name the marching group as a real movement.
- **Case for _Maximal expressive freedom_:** Freedom of speech exists for the speech
  we hate. If the state can ban this march, it can ban the next unpopular cause, and
  the answer to hateful views is argument and counter-protest, not silence enforced by
  the police.
- **Case for _Protection from harmful expression_:** Speech that tells people they do
  not belong attacks their standing as equal citizens and frightens them out of public
  life. A decent society need not put up with intimidation aimed at people's own
  streets.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Maximal expressive freedom_:** The banners say nothing that newspapers
    are not already allowed to print.
  - **Toward _Protection from harmful expression_:** After a similar march last year,
    attacks on the minority rose in the weeks that followed.

### The false health claim

- **Tests:** Speech and expression: _Maximal expressive freedom_ ↔ _Protection from
  harmful expression_
- **Also touches:** Expert trust, Security and civil liberties
- **Facet:** Misinformation and platform moderation
- **Scenario:** During an outbreak, false claims that a treatment is dangerous spread
  widely online. A proposed law would require large platforms to remove content that
  health authorities judge to be false. Should it pass?
- **Localise:** The health authority and the platforms in generic form. Never name a
  real outbreak or treatment.
- **Case for _Maximal expressive freedom_:** Authorities have been wrong before, and
  requiring platforms to remove what officials call false makes officials the judges
  of truth. Open debate corrects errors; censorship protects them.
- **Case for _Protection from harmful expression_:** Lies that lead people to refuse
  treatment cost lives. Platforms already decide what spreads, and requiring them to
  remove clear falsehoods in an emergency protects public health, not officials.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Maximal expressive freedom_:** One claim that was removed later turned
    out to be true.
  - **Toward _Protection from harmful expression_:** The claims are being spread by a
    coordinated campaign of fake accounts.
- **Rules:** —

### The lecturer's article

- **Tests:** Speech and expression: _Maximal expressive freedom_ ↔ _Protection from
  harmful expression_
- **Also touches:** Group difference, Causes of fortune
- **Facet:** Academic freedom
- **Scenario:** A lecturer at a public university publishes an article arguing that a
  disadvantaged group's problems are mostly of its own making. Students demand that
  the lecturer be dismissed. Should the university keep the lecturer on?
- **Localise:** The group may be any disadvantaged group in the person's country.
  Never state the article's argument in more detail or say whether it is right.
- **Case for _Maximal expressive freedom_:** Universities exist to test ideas,
  including offensive ones. Dismissing a scholar for a conclusion turns academic
  freedom into permission to agree, and a bad argument should be answered in print,
  not punished.
- **Case for _Protection from harmful expression_:** A university owes its students an
  education where they are treated as equals. A teacher who publicly disparages a
  group they teach harms those students, and academic freedom was never meant to
  protect contempt.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Maximal expressive freedom_:** The article passed peer review, and the
    lecturer is known for grading fairly.
  - **Toward _Protection from harmful expression_:** The lecturer has mocked students
    from that group in class.
