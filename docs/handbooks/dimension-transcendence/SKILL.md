# Transcendence

A Foundation Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Reality and history_. Copied
from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** A transcendent order exists ↔ Nature is all there is
- **Definition:** Is there a reality beyond the natural world (God, gods, a divine
  order, an afterlife) that gives existence its meaning and standards, or is nature all
  there is, with meaning made by humans?

## Using these skeletons

This is a Foundation Dimension: every skeleton asks what the person takes to be true or
deserved, never what the law should do. Keep it that way when you localise. Pole order
means nothing. Localise only as `docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic
institutions, places, and amounts for local ones; never change the collision, a fact
either side relies on, or either Case. For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial**
variant that pushes against the person's answer.

These ask what the person takes to be real. The grill asks with the same curiosity
whichever way they answer, and never treats either answer as the default.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The deathbed

- **Tests:** Transcendence: _A transcendent order exists_ ↔ _Nature is all there is_
- **Also touches:** Grounds of moral status
- **Facet:** Death and what follows it
- **Scenario:** A dying woman tells her family she is not afraid, because she is going
  home. Her grandson believes she is going nowhere: when the brain stops, the person
  ends. Is there anything beyond this life?
- **Localise:** Her words may be put in the person's own religious idiom. Never tie
  either view to a named religion as its only form.
- **Case for _A transcendent order exists_:** The longing for more than this life is
  found in every culture, and the order, beauty, and moral law of the world point
  beyond it. Most people who have ever lived have known a reality that death does not
  end, and a purely material account leaves consciousness and meaning unexplained.
- **Case for _Nature is all there is_:** Everything we know about the mind ties it to
  the brain, and no claim of an afterlife has survived testing. Accepting that this
  life is all we have does not empty it of meaning; it makes it precious, and it puts
  meaning in our own hands.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _A transcendent order exists_:** Earlier, when briefly clinically dead,
    she had an experience her medical state cannot explain.
  - **Toward _Nature is all there is_:** Scientists can produce the same sense of
    peace and of going home with a drug.
- **Rules:** —

### The source of the moral law

- **Tests:** Transcendence: _A transcendent order exists_ ↔ _Nature is all there is_
- **Also touches:** Source of moral authority, Moral objectivity
- **Facet:** Whether morality needs a ground beyond nature
- **Scenario:** Two neighbours agree that torturing a child for fun is wrong. One says
  it is wrong because there is a moral order built into reality, which ultimately comes
  from God. The other says it is wrong because of what it does to the child, and that
  needs no God. Is there a standard of right beyond nature?
- **Localise:** "God" may become the person's own word for the divine (gods, Heaven,
  the Dao). Never change the act both agree is wrong.
- **Case for _A transcendent order exists_:** If nature is all there is, morality is a
  product of evolution and culture, and "wrong" can mean no more than "we dislike it".
  The conviction that cruelty is wrong whatever anyone thinks points to a standard
  above us that we did not make.
- **Case for _Nature is all there is_:** Suffering is real and bad without further
  backing, and facts about harm and fairness stand on their own. Adding God explains
  nothing, because we would still have to judge whether God's commands are good.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _A transcendent order exists_:** Every known culture holds that some acts
    stay forbidden even if everyone agreed to them.
  - **Toward _Nature is all there is_:** Other animals show fairness and empathy too,
    with no scripture at all.
- **Rules:** —

### The finely set universe

- **Tests:** Transcendence: _A transcendent order exists_ ↔ _Nature is all there is_
- **Also touches:** Grounds for belief
- **Facet:** Order and design in the cosmos
- **Scenario:** Physicists say that if a few constants of nature were slightly
  different, no stars, planets, or life could exist. One person sees this as a sign of
  a mind behind the universe; another sees it as a puzzle that will be explained, or
  as something we could only ever observe from inside a universe that allows us. Which
  reading is more reasonable?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising.
- **Case for _A transcendent order exists_:** A universe precisely set for life is
  what you would expect if it was meant. Blind chance, or an unseen infinity of other
  universes, is a bigger leap than a mind behind it, and the order of nature is itself
  something that calls for explanation.
- **Case for _Nature is all there is_:** Every gap once filled by gods has since been
  filled by natural explanation. We can only ever find ourselves in a universe that
  allows observers, and "a mind did it" raises more questions than it answers.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _A transcendent order exists_:** No physical theory predicts the values
    of the constants.
  - **Toward _Nature is all there is_:** A promising new theory derives several of the
    constants from deeper principles.
- **Rules:** —
