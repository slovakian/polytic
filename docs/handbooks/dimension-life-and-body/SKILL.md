# Life and body

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _The state and the good life_.
Copied from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Bodily and reproductive autonomy ↔ Protection of human life and dignity
- **Definition:** At the edges of life and about the body, should the law defer to the
  individual's choice, or protect human life and dignity even against that choice?
- **Facets:** Abortion; assisted dying; IVF and embryo research; gene editing.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The unplanned pregnancy

- **Tests:** Life and body: _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_ ↔ _Protection of human
  life and dignity_
- **Also touches:** Grounds of moral status, Transcendence
- **Facet:** Abortion
- **Scenario:** A woman is pregnant and does not want to continue the pregnancy.
  Should the law let her end it, and if so, up to what point in the pregnancy?
- **Localise:** The country's current law may be stated neutrally, as fact. Never
  describe the woman's reasons, and never use either side's slogans ("pro-life",
  "pro-choice", "baby", "clump of cells") in the Interviewer's own voice.
- **Case for _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_:** No one can be required to use their
  body to sustain another life, even to save it. Pregnancy, birth, and motherhood
  transform a woman's life, and only she can weigh what that means for her. Banning
  abortion does not end it; it makes it dangerous and turns women's bodies into public
  property.
- **Case for _Protection of human life and dignity_:** The unborn child is a living
  human being, and the first duty of law is to protect those who cannot protect
  themselves. A hard situation does not make it permissible to end a life; a just
  society supports the mother and protects both.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_:** The pregnancy resulted from rape.
  - **Toward _Protection of human life and dignity_:** She is seven months pregnant,
    and the child could survive outside the womb.
- **Rules:** Fixed dilemma, used as written in `dimension-taxonomy.md` ("Diagnostic
  Dilemmas written into the set"). It is an Issue across _Life and body_ (Political),
  _Grounds of moral status_ and _Transcendence_ (Foundation). Where moral status begins
  (conception, heartbeat, consciousness, viability, birth) is recorded in Reasons; a
  time limit is a Position between the Poles. "It's killing, but it should be legal" is
  a Tension between the Foundation and Political Beliefs, put to the person to
  reconcile or hold.

### The request to die

- **Tests:** Life and body: _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_ ↔ _Protection of human
  life and dignity_
- **Also touches:** Paternalism, Grounds of moral status
- **Facet:** Assisted dying
- **Scenario:** A man with a terminal illness, of sound mind and in great pain, asks
  his doctor to help him die. Should the law allow the doctor to do it?
- **Localise:** The country's current law may be stated neutrally, as fact. Never
  change that he is of sound mind and terminally ill.
- **Case for _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_:** It is his life and his suffering.
  A competent adult should be able to decide how his own life ends, and forcing him to
  endure its last months for the sake of others' principles is cruelty, not respect.
- **Case for _Protection of human life and dignity_:** A life's worth does not depend
  on whether its owner still wants it. Legal assisted death changes what medicine is
  for, and it puts quiet pressure on the old, the disabled, and the poor to see
  themselves as a burden. The answer to suffering is better care.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_:** No available care can relieve his
    pain.
  - **Toward _Protection of human life and dignity_:** He says his main reason is that
    he does not want to be a burden on his family.
- **Rules:** —

### The edited embryo

- **Tests:** Life and body: _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_ ↔ _Protection of human
  life and dignity_
- **Also touches:** Technology and risk, Future generations
- **Facet:** Gene editing
- **Scenario:** A couple who both carry the gene for a severe inherited illness want
  to use a new technique to edit their embryo, so that the child will not have it. The
  change would pass on to the child's own descendants. Should the law permit it?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never name a real illness, and never say
  the technique is unsafe; that would settle it on other grounds.
- **Case for _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_:** Parents already make many choices
  to give their children healthy lives. Preventing terrible suffering with a safe
  technique is no different in kind, and the decision belongs to the family, not the
  state.
- **Case for _Protection of human life and dignity_:** Editing the human line makes
  children into products designed by others, and changes humanity for generations
  without their consent. A line crossed to prevent illness will move, step by step,
  toward designing traits.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Bodily and reproductive autonomy_:** There is no other way for the couple
    to have a healthy child of their own.
  - **Toward _Protection of human life and dignity_:** The same clinic already offers
    to edit for height and intelligence.
- **Rules:** —
