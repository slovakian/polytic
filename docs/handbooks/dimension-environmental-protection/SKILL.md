# Environmental protection

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Nature, technology, and the
future_. Copied from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Prosperity and development first ↔ Ecological protection first
- **Definition:** When protecting ecosystems and the climate costs jobs, growth, or
  cheap energy, which should give way?
- **Facets:** Climate-policy stringency; conservation and land use; energy mix; climate
  burden-sharing; rights of nature.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The mine in the forest

- **Tests:** Environmental protection: _Prosperity and development first_ ↔
  _Ecological protection first_
- **Also touches:** Standing of nature, Growth
- **Facet:** Conservation and land use
- **Scenario:** A company wants to open a mine in an ancient forest. It would bring a
  thousand good jobs to a poor region with high unemployment, and destroy a habitat
  found nowhere else. Should it be allowed?
- **Localise:** The resource, the landscape, and the region. Never change that the
  habitat is unique or that the region is poor.
- **Case for _Prosperity and development first_:** The people of that region need
  work and a future now. A forest, however old, is not worth more than a thousand
  families' livelihoods, and development is how poor regions have always escaped
  poverty.
- **Case for _Ecological protection first_:** Once destroyed, the habitat cannot be
  replaced. Jobs can be created in other ways, but an extinct species is gone for
  good, and the forest serves people far beyond the region and far into the future.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Prosperity and development first_:** The mine would supply a metal needed
    for clean energy, which would otherwise come from a mine abroad with far worse
    practices.
  - **Toward _Ecological protection first_:** The jobs last fifteen years; the forest
    took ten thousand to form.

### The fuel price rise

- **Tests:** Environmental protection: _Prosperity and development first_ ↔
  _Ecological protection first_
- **Also touches:** Future generations, Distribution
- **Facet:** Climate-policy stringency
- **Scenario:** To meet its climate targets, a country would add a tax that noticeably
  raises fuel and heating prices. Rural families and people on low incomes would bear
  most of the cost. Should it go ahead?
- **Localise:** The fuels, the prices, and the country's targets. Never add a rebate;
  that would settle the dilemma rather than test it.
- **Case for _Prosperity and development first_:** Cheap energy is the base of a
  decent life, and making it dearer hits the poorest first. One country's small share
  of global emissions does not justify that burden; it should adapt and innovate
  instead of making its own people poorer.
- **Case for _Ecological protection first_:** Climate change will cost far more than
  the transition, and the poor and future generations will suffer most from it. Delay
  only makes the later cuts steeper, and every country has to do its share or none
  will.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Prosperity and development first_:** The country produces one percent of
    world emissions, and its largest neighbours are increasing theirs.
  - **Toward _Ecological protection first_:** The country has just had its third
    once-in-a-century flood in ten years.
- **Rules:** —

### The coal reserves

- **Tests:** Environmental protection: _Prosperity and development first_ ↔
  _Ecological protection first_
- **Also touches:** Future generations, Historical injustice
- **Facet:** Climate burden-sharing and the right to develop
- **Scenario:** A poor country has large coal reserves. Burning them would lift
  millions out of poverty within a generation, as rich countries once did, but would
  add significantly to global emissions. Should it develop with coal?
- **Localise:** If the person lives in a poor country, it may be their own; if in a
  rich one, the question may be whether their country should accept or oppose it.
  Never name a real country.
- **Case for _Prosperity and development first_:** Rich countries got rich on coal,
  and denying poor countries the same path keeps them poor. The right to develop comes
  first, and the burden of cutting emissions belongs to those who caused the problem.
- **Case for _Ecological protection first_:** The atmosphere does not care who is
  rich. New coal plants lock in emissions for decades, and the poor of that very
  country will be among those hit hardest by a changing climate.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Prosperity and development first_:** Rich countries have refused to help
    pay for a cleaner alternative.
  - **Toward _Ecological protection first_:** The poor country's own farmland is
    already drying up as the rains change.
