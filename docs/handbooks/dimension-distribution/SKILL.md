# Distribution

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Economy_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Market entitlement ↔ Egalitarian redistribution
- **Definition:** Should the law leave the distribution of income and wealth as fair
  exchange produces it, or reshape it toward greater equality?
- **Facets:** Tax progressivity; wealth and inheritance taxes; equality, priority, or
  sufficiency; meritocracy; equality of opportunity.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The family fortune

- **Tests:** Distribution: _Market entitlement_ ↔ _Egalitarian redistribution_
- **Also touches:** Desert, Property in capital and goods
- **Facet:** Wealth and inheritance taxes
- **Scenario:** A woman who built a large business dies and leaves it to her children.
  The country is deciding whether to tax large inheritances heavily, spending the money
  on things that benefit everyone, or to leave them as they are. Which should it do?
- **Localise:** The currency, the size of the estate, and the country's current
  inheritance rules, stated as fact. Never make the heirs idle or the founder a
  cheat.
- **Case for _Market entitlement_:** She earned the fortune and paid tax as she earned
  it. Leaving it to her children is the last use of her own property, and taxing it
  again at death punishes saving, building, and caring for one's family.
- **Case for _Egalitarian redistribution_:** The children did nothing to earn it.
  Large inheritances turn one generation's success into lasting advantage, and taxing
  them is the least unfair way to fund opportunity for children born with nothing.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Market entitlement_:** The tax would force the children to sell the
    family business to pay it.
  - **Toward _Egalitarian redistribution_:** The fortune is so large that none of the
    heirs, or their children, will ever need to work.

### The top rate

- **Tests:** Distribution: _Market entitlement_ ↔ _Egalitarian redistribution_
- **Also touches:** Causes of fortune, Welfare and social insurance
- **Facet:** Tax progressivity
- **Scenario:** A proposal would raise the income tax rate on the top one percent of
  earners from moderate to very high, to fund better public services. Should it pass?
- **Localise:** The rates and the services, scaled to the country's current system.
  Never state how much the tax would raise; that is a dial.
- **Case for _Market entitlement_:** High earners already pay a large share of all tax.
  What people earn in voluntary exchange is theirs, and very high rates penalise the
  work, risk, and enterprise that create the wealth everyone else depends on.
- **Case for _Egalitarian redistribution_:** Incomes at the top reflect luck,
  bargaining power, and public goods as much as effort. A wide gap undermines equal
  citizenship, and an extra unit of money means far less to the rich than to those who
  need the services.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Market entitlement_:** Most top earners would move abroad or shift their
    income, so the higher rate would raise little.
  - **Toward _Egalitarian redistribution_:** The gap between top and middle incomes has
    doubled in a generation.
- **Rules:** —

### The two paths

- **Tests:** Distribution: _Market entitlement_ ↔ _Egalitarian redistribution_
- **Also touches:** Growth, Zero-sum or positive-sum
- **Facet:** Equality against sufficiency
- **Scenario:** A country must choose between two economic paths. On the first,
  everyone becomes better off than today, but the richest gain far more and the gap
  widens sharply. On the second, average growth is slower and the gap narrows; the
  poorest gain exactly as much as on the first. Which path should it choose?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never change that the poorest gain the same
  on both paths.
- **Case for _Market entitlement_:** If everyone gains, no one has been wronged. The
  gains at the top reflect value that people chose to pay for, and holding everyone
  back to narrow a gap gives up real prosperity for a number on a chart.
- **Case for _Egalitarian redistribution_:** Relative position matters, not only
  absolute income. Wide gaps become gaps in political power, health, and respect, and
  a society of rough equals is better to live in, even if it is a little poorer on
  average.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Market entitlement_:** The gains at the top come from inventions
    everyone uses.
  - **Toward _Egalitarian redistribution_:** On the first path, a few families would
    come to own most of the media and fund most election campaigns.
