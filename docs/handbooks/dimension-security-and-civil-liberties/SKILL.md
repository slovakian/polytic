# Security and civil liberties

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Order, security, and justice_.
Copied from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Civil liberties and due process ↔ Collective security and order
- **Definition:** When protecting the public from crime, terrorism, or disorder
  conflicts with individual rights, which should give way?
- **Facets:** Surveillance; policing powers; trial rights; emergency powers; gun rights
  and the state's monopoly of force.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The cameras in the square

- **Tests:** Security and civil liberties: _Civil liberties and due process_ ↔
  _Collective security and order_
- **Also touches:** Technology and risk
- **Facet:** Surveillance
- **Scenario:** The police propose face-recognition cameras across a city's public
  spaces, matching passers-by against a list of wanted suspects. Should the city
  allow them?
- **Localise:** The city and the police force. Never state the system's error rate;
  that is a dial.
- **Case for _Civil liberties and due process_:** Mass surveillance treats everyone
  as a suspect and builds a tool that any future government can turn to its own ends.
  The freedom to move through a city unwatched is worth some crimes going unsolved.
- **Case for _Collective security and order_:** Public spaces are already public.
  Matching faces only against wanted suspects catches dangerous people faster, and
  ordinary citizens have little to fear from a camera looking for someone else.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Civil liberties and due process_:** The system wrongly flags people from
    some groups far more often than others.
  - **Toward _Collective security and order_:** Last year, a wanted man walked past ten
    cameras before killing someone.

### The held suspect

- **Tests:** Security and civil liberties: _Civil liberties and due process_ ↔
  _Collective security and order_
- **Also touches:** Rule of law vs. discretion
- **Facet:** Trial rights and emergency powers
- **Scenario:** After a bombing, the police arrest a man they believe is planning
  another. Their evidence comes from intelligence that cannot be shown in open court.
  Should the law allow him to be held for several weeks without charge?
- **Localise:** The security services. Never make the man's guilt certain or
  impossible.
- **Case for _Civil liberties and due process_:** Holding people without charge, on
  evidence they cannot see or challenge, is the mark of arbitrary power. It will catch
  the innocent and be used beyond its purpose, and the rule of law is exactly what is
  being defended.
- **Case for _Collective security and order_:** The state's first duty is to protect
  its people. When the threat is real and the evidence cannot be shown without
  exposing its sources, limited detention under a judge's eye is a reasonable price
  for preventing a massacre.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Civil liberties and due process_:** In past emergencies, most people held
    this way were later released without charge.
  - **Toward _Collective security and order_:** Intelligence says a second attack is
    planned within days.
- **Rules:** —

### The rifle ban

- **Tests:** Security and civil liberties: _Civil liberties and due process_ ↔
  _Collective security and order_
- **Also touches:** Political authority, Paternalism
- **Facet:** Gun rights and the state's monopoly of force
- **Scenario:** After a mass shooting, a proposal would ban civilians from owning
  semi-automatic rifles and require a licence and training for any gun. Should it
  pass?
- **Localise:** The country's current gun laws may be stated neutrally, as fact. Where
  civilian guns are already rare, the question may be whether to loosen the law
  instead, keeping both Cases.
- **Case for _Civil liberties and due process_:** Ordinary citizens have a right to
  defend themselves and their families, and an armed people is a check on a state that
  might turn on it. Punishing millions of law-abiding owners for one criminal's act is
  unjust.
- **Case for _Collective security and order_:** Public safety depends on the state
  holding a monopoly of lethal force. Weapons built to kill many people quickly have
  no place among civilians, and a licence is a modest price for living alongside guns.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Civil liberties and due process_:** The shooter used a stolen gun and was
    already barred from owning one.
  - **Toward _Collective security and order_:** It is the third mass shooting this
    year with legally bought rifles.
