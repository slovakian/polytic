# Causes of fortune

A Foundation Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Agency and fortune_. Copied
from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Effort and choice ↔ Luck and structure
- **Definition:** When people succeed or fail, what mostly explains it: their own effort
  and choices, or circumstances they did not control (birth, luck, discrimination,
  economic structure)?

## Using these skeletons

This is a Foundation Dimension: every skeleton asks what the person takes to be true or
deserved, never what the law should do. Keep it that way when you localise. Pole order
means nothing. Localise only as `docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic
institutions, places, and amounts for local ones; never change the collision, a fact
either side relies on, or either Case. For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial**
variant that pushes against the person's answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The two siblings

- **Tests:** Causes of fortune: _Effort and choice_ ↔ _Luck and structure_
- **Also touches:** Desert, Free will
- **Facet:** Individual outcomes where upbringing is held constant
- **Scenario:** Two sisters grow up in the same house, with the same parents, at the
  same school. At forty, one runs a successful business; the other has drifted between
  short jobs and is in debt. The first says she worked for it; the second says her
  sister got the breaks: a teacher who took an interest, a first job through a friend,
  better health. What mostly explains the difference between them?
- **Localise:** Occupations, schools, and the kind of debt. Never change that they
  shared the same home and parents.
- **Case for _Effort and choice_:** A shared upbringing removes most of what people
  call structure, and what is left is what each sister did: studying, saving, getting
  up again after failing. Small breaks come to everyone; what people make of them
  compounds over decades. Calling the gap luck takes away the one thing that was
  hers to own.
- **Case for _Luck and structure_:** Even sisters draw different cards: temperament,
  health, birth order, which teacher noticed whom, one early opening that led to the
  next. The capacity to work hard and persist depends on health and encouragement too,
  so it is partly luck as well. The same house is not the same life.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Effort and choice_:** The struggling sister turned down two jobs the other
    arranged for her.
  - **Toward _Luck and structure_:** The struggling sister had a chronic illness that
    was only diagnosed at thirty.

### The two districts

- **Tests:** Causes of fortune: _Effort and choice_ ↔ _Luck and structure_
- **Also touches:** Desert, Group difference
- **Facet:** Gaps between groups and places
- **Scenario:** In one city, children from a poor district are far less likely to
  finish university than children from a wealthy district across the river. Some say
  the gap mostly reflects what families in each district do: reading at home, pushing
  homework, keeping children out of trouble. Others say it mostly reflects schools,
  connections, and money the children never chose. Which explanation is closer to the
  truth?
- **Localise:** The city, the river, and the level of education. Never name an ethnic
  or religious group as either district.
- **Case for _Effort and choice_:** Many children in the poor district, at the same
  schools, do finish, and the difference on the same streets is mostly what their
  families did. An explanation that is all structure overlooks those families and
  tells every child that their own effort will not count.
- **Case for _Luck and structure_:** No child picks their district, school, or parents'
  income, and those predict outcomes strongly. The family habits credited with the
  difference are themselves shaped by stress, long working hours, and what a
  neighbourhood makes possible. A few exceptions do not explain an average.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Effort and choice_:** Schools in both districts get the same funding per
    pupil and similar teachers.
  - **Toward _Luck and structure_:** Children who move from the poor district to the
    wealthy one when young close most of the gap.
- **Rules:** —

### The factory closure

- **Tests:** Causes of fortune: _Effort and choice_ ↔ _Luck and structure_
- **Also touches:** Desert, Reciprocity in support
- **Facet:** Recovery from a shared setback
- **Scenario:** A factory closes, and three hundred people lose their jobs on the same
  day. Five years later, some have retrained and earn more than before; others are
  still out of work. What mostly explains who recovered?
- **Localise:** The industry and the size of the town. Never change that the shock
  hit everyone on the same day.
- **Case for _Effort and choice_:** The shock was luck, but the recovery was choice.
  The same blow fell on everyone, and those who retrained, moved, or took a worse job
  to start again are the ones who came back. Effort is what separates people facing
  the same bad news.
- **Case for _Luck and structure_:** Who recovered tracks age, health, savings, and
  whether a partner's income could carry a year of retraining. A fifty-eight-year-old
  with a mortgage and a bad back faced a different problem from a thirty-year-old
  renter. The same day is not the same starting line.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Effort and choice_:** Retraining was free and open to all, and age did not
    predict who took it up.
  - **Toward _Luck and structure_:** Almost everyone over fifty is still out of work,
    however hard they tried.
