# Leadership

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Who decides_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Strong, decisive leadership ↔ Dispersed, checked authority
- **Definition:** Should executive power be concentrated in a strong leader able to act
  decisively, or divided and checked so that no one can act alone?
- **Facets:** Presidential vs. parliamentary; term limits; decree powers; monarchy as
  executive.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The third term

- **Tests:** Leadership: _Strong, decisive leadership_ ↔ _Dispersed, checked
  authority_
- **Also touches:** Popular sovereignty vs. constitutional constraint
- **Facet:** Term limits
- **Scenario:** A popular leader who has governed well for two terms asks voters to
  approve a change allowing a third. Should the limit be lifted?
- **Localise:** The office and the country's current limit. Never make the leader a
  real or recognisable figure.
- **Case for _Strong, decisive leadership_:** If the people want to keep a good leader,
  a rule forcing that leader out overrides their choice and throws away experience.
  Continuity lets long projects be finished instead of abandoned halfway.
- **Case for _Dispersed, checked authority_:** Term limits protect against anyone
  becoming indispensable. Even good leaders build networks of loyalty that make them
  harder to remove, and the rule is there for the day the leader is not good.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Strong, decisive leadership_:** The leader is halfway through a reform
    that would collapse without them.
  - **Toward _Dispersed, checked authority_:** The leader's allies already control the
    courts and the main broadcasters.

### The emergency decree

- **Tests:** Leadership: _Strong, decisive leadership_ ↔ _Dispersed, checked
  authority_
- **Also touches:** Security and civil liberties, Rule of law vs. discretion
- **Facet:** Decree powers
- **Scenario:** During a crisis, the legislature is deadlocked. The head of government
  asks for the power to rule by decree for six months. Should it be granted?
- **Localise:** The crisis may be economic, natural, or a pandemic. Never say what the
  decrees would be; that is a dial.
- **Case for _Strong, decisive leadership_:** In a crisis, delay costs lives and
  livelihoods. A leader who can act decisively, and who answers to voters afterwards,
  serves the country better than a gridlock that leaves everyone waiting.
- **Case for _Dispersed, checked authority_:** Emergencies are exactly when power is
  most easily taken and least easily given back. Requiring agreement slows bad
  decisions as well as good ones, and power must stay divided especially when it
  feels inconvenient.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Strong, decisive leadership_:** The power expires automatically, and the
    courts can strike down any decree.
  - **Toward _Dispersed, checked authority_:** The leader will not say what the decrees
    would contain.
- **Rules:** —

### The new constitution

- **Tests:** Leadership: _Strong, decisive leadership_ ↔ _Dispersed, checked
  authority_
- **Also touches:** Majoritarian vs. consensus
- **Facet:** Presidential against parliamentary government
- **Scenario:** A country is writing a new constitution. One option is a powerful,
  directly elected president who leads the government for a fixed term. The other is a
  parliamentary system, where the prime minister needs a majority and can be removed by
  it at any time. Which should it choose?
- **Localise:** If the person's country already has one system, the question may be
  whether to switch. Never name a real constitution as the model.
- **Case for _Strong, decisive leadership_:** A directly elected leader with real
  power gives the people one person to hold responsible, and the strength to act on
  the mandate they gave. Diffuse systems blur responsibility and produce weak, shifting
  coalitions.
- **Case for _Dispersed, checked authority_:** A powerful president with a fixed term
  cannot be removed when they fail. A parliamentary system keeps the leader answerable
  every day to colleagues and lets a failing government be replaced quickly.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Strong, decisive leadership_:** The present parliament has had six
    governments in eight years.
  - **Toward _Dispersed, checked authority_:** In the region, several such presidencies
    have slid into one-man rule.
