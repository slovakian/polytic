# Sex roles in family and public life

A Political Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _The state and the good life_.
Copied from `docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Distinct roles for the sexes ↔ Equal and interchangeable roles
- **Definition:** Should law and policy uphold distinct roles for men and women and the
  family form built on them, at home and in public institutions, or treat the sexes as
  interchangeable and diverse family forms alike?
- **Facets:** Marriage law and same-sex relationships; childcare vs. homemaker support;
  natalism; women in leadership, quotas, and the military; sex education.

## Using these skeletons

This is a Political Dimension: every skeleton asks what the law, the state, or public
institutions should do. Pole order means nothing. Localise only as
`docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic institutions, places, and amounts for
local ones; never change the collision, a fact either side relies on, or either Case.
For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial** variant that pushes against the person's
answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The home allowance

- **Tests:** Sex roles in family and public life: _Distinct roles for the sexes_ ↔
  _Equal and interchangeable roles_
- **Also touches:** Sex and gender, Welfare and social insurance
- **Facet:** Childcare against homemaker support
- **Scenario:** A government has a sum to spend on families with young children. One
  plan pays it as an allowance to whichever parent stays home to raise them, which in
  practice is mostly mothers. The other spends it on childcare, so that both parents
  can work. Which should it choose?
- **Localise:** The amounts and the country's current family support. Never make
  either plan more generous than the other.
- **Case for _Distinct roles for the sexes_:** Mothers and fathers bring different
  things to their children, and many mothers want to be at home with them when they
  are young. Policy should honour the family built on that, rather than push every
  parent into paid work and hand small children to institutions.
- **Case for _Equal and interchangeable roles_:** Either parent can raise children,
  and either can earn. Policy built around a mother at home keeps women out of careers
  and men out of caregiving, so support should treat both parents and both choices
  alike.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Distinct roles for the sexes_:** Most mothers of young children say they
    would stay home if they could afford to.
  - **Toward _Equal and interchangeable roles_:** Women who take the allowance earn far
    less for the rest of their working lives.

### The marriage question

- **Tests:** Sex roles in family and public life: _Distinct roles for the sexes_ ↔
  _Equal and interchangeable roles_
- **Also touches:** Transcendence, Legal moralism, Religion and state
- **Facet:** Marriage law and same-sex relationships
- **Scenario:** A country is deciding whether marriage in law should remain the union
  of a man and a woman, or be open to any two adults. Which should it be?
- **Localise:** The country's current law may be stated neutrally, as fact. Never
  frame either side as bigoted or as an attack on the family.
- **Case for _Distinct roles for the sexes_:** Marriage exists to unite the two sexes
  and the children who come from that union; it is more than a public seal on love.
  Redefining it changes what marriage means for everyone, and the law should keep the
  institution that every society has built itself on.
- **Case for _Equal and interchangeable roles_:** The law should treat every couple's
  commitment alike. Same-sex couples love, commit, and raise children too, and
  excluding them from marriage makes them second-class citizens in their own country.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Distinct roles for the sexes_:** Civil partnerships with identical legal
    rights already exist; only the name "marriage" is in question.
  - **Toward _Equal and interchangeable roles_:** Without marriage, partners cannot
    inherit from each other or make decisions in hospital.
- **Rules:** —

### The front line

- **Tests:** Sex roles in family and public life: _Distinct roles for the sexes_ ↔
  _Equal and interchangeable roles_
- **Also touches:** Sex and gender
- **Facet:** Women in the military
- **Scenario:** An army is deciding whether women may serve in front-line combat
  units on the same terms as men, including the same physical tests. Should they?
- **Localise:** The country's current rules may be stated neutrally, as fact. Never
  state how many women pass; that is a dial.
- **Case for _Distinct roles for the sexes_:** Men and women differ physically, and
  combat units depend on strength and on bonds that mixing changes. Keeping women out
  of front-line combat reflects a real difference and a duty to protect, not
  prejudice.
- **Case for _Equal and interchangeable roles_:** Anyone who meets the standard should
  be able to serve. Excluding people by sex rather than ability bars the women who do
  qualify, and keeps them out of the careers that lead to command.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Distinct roles for the sexes_:** In trials, mixed units performed worse
    than all-male units on combat tasks.
  - **Toward _Equal and interchangeable roles_:** The women who passed the same tests
    did as well as the men in combat.
- **Rules:** —
