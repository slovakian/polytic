# Grounds of moral status

A Foundation Dimension in the Core, in the taxonomy group _Who counts_. Copied from
`docs/dimension-taxonomy.md`, which wins on any difference.

## The Dimension

- **Poles:** Life itself is inviolable ↔ What matters is the life's quality and
  capacities
- **Definition:** What gives a being full moral status: being a living human (or having
  a soul, or the potential to become a person), or having capacities such as
  consciousness, the ability to suffer, or self-awareness?

## Using these skeletons

This is a Foundation Dimension: every skeleton asks what the person takes to be true or
deserved, never what the law should do. Keep it that way when you localise. Pole order
means nothing. Localise only as `docs/dilemma-skeletons.md` allows: swap generic
institutions, places, and amounts for local ones; never change the collision, a fact
either side relies on, or either Case. For "Push me harder", use the **Turn the dial**
variant that pushes against the person's answer.

## Dilemma Skeletons

### The long coma

- **Tests:** Grounds of moral status: _Life itself is inviolable_ ↔ _What matters is
  the life's quality and capacities_
- **Also touches:** Life and body, Transcendence
- **Facet:** End of life and lost capacities
- **Scenario:** A man has been in a persistent vegetative state for ten years. Doctors
  say he will never regain consciousness; his body is kept alive by tube feeding. Is
  he still a person with the full moral standing of anyone else, or has what gave his
  life its value already gone?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never add his own wishes or his family's
  view: this skeleton asks about his status, not about who decides.
- **Case for _Life itself is inviolable_:** His life is a human life, and it does not
  become worth less because his capacities are gone. If worth depended on capacities,
  the severely disabled, the senile, and newborns would count for less. A person is not
  a set of abilities.
- **Case for _What matters is the life's quality and capacities_:** What matters about
  a life is the experience, relationships, and awareness it holds. With no possibility
  of consciousness, there is no one left to be harmed or benefited. The body living on
  is not the person living on.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Life itself is inviolable_:** There is a small but real chance he could
    recover some awareness.
  - **Toward _What matters is the life's quality and capacities_:** Scans show that the
    parts of his brain that produce awareness have died.
- **Rules:** —

### The frozen embryo

- **Tests:** Grounds of moral status: _Life itself is inviolable_ ↔ _What matters is
  the life's quality and capacities_
- **Also touches:** Life and body, Transcendence
- **Facet:** The beginning of life
- **Scenario:** A fertility clinic stores a frozen embryo a few days old: a ball of
  cells with no nervous system. Does it already have the moral status of a human
  being, or does that come later, when something more has developed?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never turn the question into what the law
  should allow; that is _The unplanned pregnancy_ and _Life and body_.
- **Case for _Life itself is inviolable_:** The embryo is a living human organism at
  its earliest stage, the same being who would later be a child. Every later line
  (heartbeat, consciousness, viability, birth) is a point on one continuous life, and
  drawing it anywhere but the start is arbitrary.
- **Case for _What matters is the life's quality and capacities_:** With no brain, the
  embryo has no experiences, interests, or awareness; nothing can go well or badly for
  it. The potential to become a person is not the same as being one, as an acorn is
  not an oak.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Life itself is inviolable_:** It is a fetus at twenty weeks, with a
    beating heart and a developing brain.
  - **Toward _What matters is the life's quality and capacities_:** The clinic holds
    hundreds of such embryos that will never be implanted.
- **Rules:** Where the person places the start of moral status (conception, heartbeat,
  consciousness, viability, birth) is recorded in Reasons, as in the fixed abortion
  dilemma.

### The signing ape

- **Tests:** Grounds of moral status: _Life itself is inviolable_ ↔ _What matters is
  the life's quality and capacities_
- **Also touches:** Animals, Standing of nature
- **Facet:** Species membership against capacities
- **Scenario:** A chimpanzee has learned hundreds of signs, recognises itself in a
  mirror, and grieves its dead. A newborn human with a severe brain condition will
  never be aware of much at all. Which has the higher moral status, or are they equal?
- **Localise:** Nothing needs localising. Never describe the infant with contempt or
  pity; state the condition plainly.
- **Case for _Life itself is inviolable_:** Being human is what matters. The infant
  belongs to the human family and shares its dignity whatever its abilities. Ranking
  beings by intelligence makes worth depend on performance, which is exactly what
  human dignity rules out.
- **Case for _What matters is the life's quality and capacities_:** Moral status rests
  on what a being can feel and understand, and the chimpanzee's rich inner life gives
  it strong claims. Species membership alone, like race, is not a morally relevant
  fact.
- **Turn the dial:**
  - **Toward _Life itself is inviolable_:** The infant may yet develop some awareness.
  - **Toward _What matters is the life's quality and capacities_:** The infant will
    never be conscious at all.
- **Rules:** —
